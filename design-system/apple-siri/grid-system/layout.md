# Brand Name Page — Layout Brief

## Overall Page Structure

The page is a long-scroll, section-by-section narrative that tells the brand's story across roughly **10–12 distinct content blocks**. The flow alternates rhythmically between full-bleed immersive moments and restrained white-space sections, creating a breathing pattern: loud → quiet → loud → quiet. No persistent sidebar. Pure vertical scroll. The hierarchy is strongly typographic — massive display type anchors every section, with supporting imagery floating around or beneath the type rather than boxing it in.

---

## Header / Navigation

A **universal global nav** sits at the very top — full-width, white background, ultra-thin. Content is horizontally centered with the brand logo at dead-center, flanked symmetrically by product category links. Search and utility icons anchor the far right. The nav is extremely low-profile — approximately 44–48px tall — and feels almost invisible, designed to get out of the way immediately. Likely sticky on scroll.

---

## Hero Section

The hero is the most compositionally complex section on the page. It is **full-width, white background**, and uses a **radial scatter / floating product** layout rather than any grid or columns. Brand products are arranged in a loose orbital pattern around the centered brand wordmark. The products are **oversized, overlapping the viewport edges** — cropped by the frame — giving a sense of depth and abundance without being claustrophobic.

The brand wordmark itself is centered horizontally, sitting slightly below vertical center, rendered in a **gradient**. The dot or accent character is a deliberate typographic detail that signals the brand. Products radiate outward from this anchor point at varying angles and scales. This is not a grid layout; it is a **deliberate asymmetric scatter** with implicit radial balance. No text body copy in the hero — the wordmark *is* the message.

## Content Sections Below the Hero

### Section 2 — Four Statements (White, Centered Type)

A quiet, typographic-only section on a near-white background. Four short statements are stacked vertically, centered, with generous spacing between each. Each statement uses **large display type with a gradient** that shifts hue slightly per line. No imagery. Pure text hierarchy. The spatial rhythm is slow and deliberate — this is a palate cleanser after the visual density of the hero.

### Section 3 — Dark Feature Section

**Full-bleed dark background**. A two-part vertical composition: a category label sits in light medium-weight text at top-left. Below it, a massive headline fills most of the viewport width. The type is in a **left-to-right gradient**, enormous weight, almost the full width of the page. Body copy sits beneath in a centered column at roughly 40% page width — small, muted, restrained — then a text link below that. The contrast between the headline's scale and the body copy's humility is the defining tension here. Full-bleed background, no card or container.

### Section 4 — Split Product Demo (Dark-to-Light Transition)

This section begins on a **dark background and dissolves to white** — a gradient wash spanning the full width. Two product mockups are placed side-by-side: the left shows a primary interaction UI; the right shows a secondary state. The mockups are presented at a **slight 3D perspective tilt** (isometric-ish), not flat. They are center-anchored, taking up about 60% of the viewport width combined, floating freely against the transitioning background.

### Section 5 — Asymmetric Editorial Section

**White background**. The section header is again oversized display type anchored to the left edge, beginning offscreen at the top. A product image **floats freely to the right** of the type — not in a column, but truly floating, with generous negative space around it. Below, a product mockup appears left-aligned at a slight angle, and body copy sits to its right in a roughly 30% wide column. This is a **two-weight asymmetric composition**: type dominates the left half, imagery punctuates the right, and white space does the heavy lifting in between.

### Section 6 — Two-Card Feature Grid

**Full-bleed gradient background**. Two large rounded-corner cards sit side by side, each occupying roughly 45% of the page width with a small gutter between. The left card is dark; the right card is light. Each card contains an icon, a bold statement in display type, and a UI mockup below. This is the cleanest **2-column card grid** on the page — equal-width, equal-height, symmetric. The background gradient unifies them as a single section.

### Section 7 — Context Feature Section

**White background**. Massive display type is left-aligned, occupying the left ~55% of the viewport. The type uses a **vertical gradient**. Body copy sits to the right of the type in a narrow column (approximately 30% width), vertically centered against the headline. Below this text block, a **full-bleed photograph** bleeds to the bottom of the section. This is a classic **large-type + small-copy two-column** layout, with the photograph acting as a visual footer to the section rather than sitting beside the text.

### Section 8 — Full-Bleed Feature + Two More Cards

A **full-bleed gradient background** wraps this section. At top, a large rounded card contains a feature photograph — the card itself has about 80% page width, centered, with visible background showing on the sides. Below the card, the same two-card grid pattern repeats (light card + dark card, side by side), each showing a key feature. This section reuses the card-pair pattern but layers it beneath a large visual card, creating a **stacked hierarchy**: big image card → two feature cards.

### Section 9 — Transition Section

**Full-bleed colored background** that fades to white. Product mockups float above the colored section — likely the tail of the previous context. Below the fade, on white, massive display type appears in a **gradient**, again left-aligned and very large. The section is transitional — part gradient, part white — linking two contexts visually.

### Section 10 — Wearable Feature Card

**Gradient background**, full-width. A single large rounded-corner card sits centered, occupying about 80% page width. Inside the card: a key message in display type on the left, and a product mockup on the right — a **two-column card interior** with type left and product right. The product UI is legible. The card feels like a premium product showcase insert.

## Notable Layout Patterns

**Floating / Scattered Elements**
The hero is the standout — products are not constrained to any grid. They float freely, overlap the viewport edge, and orbit the wordmark. This "scatter" technique appears again in Section 5.

**Full-Bleed Color Transitions**
Multiple sections use full-width background gradients as section separators — there are no hard lines or containers. Sections dissolve into each other through color, creating a seamless scroll narrative.

**Oversized Display Type as Layout Architecture**
The headlines are so large they *are* the layout. They function simultaneously as typography and as full-width compositional elements, pushing imagery and body copy into relationship with them rather than the reverse. Type sizes appear to run from roughly 80px to 140px+ at desktop.

**Rounded Card Modules**
Cards with large corner radii appear repeatedly — product feature cards, two-up cards, photo-within-a-section cards. They create visual containment within otherwise borderless sections.

**Asymmetric Paired Columns**
Several sections pair a very large type column (~60–70% width) with a very narrow body copy column (~30% width), creating dramatic weight imbalance that reads as confident and editorial rather than templated.

**Gradient Type**
Nearly every major headline uses a linear color gradient within the letterforms — not gradient backgrounds. The gradient direction and color range shifts per section to mark context transitions.
