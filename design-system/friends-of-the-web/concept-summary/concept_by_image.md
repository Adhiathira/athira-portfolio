# Friends of the Web — Concept by Image

## Brand Premise

Friends of the Web presents a digital studio's identity through the texture of its own making — not by describing its process but by enacting it on the page. The homepage's central showcase is a collage of interactive project "windows" arranged in an overlapping, non-grid composition that functions as a live demonstration of the studio's capability: the site itself is the portfolio. This is a studio that treats its own homepage as a commission of comparable seriousness to the client work it contains.

## Color Architecture

The palette is white-ground with disciplined restraint. White is the universal background across all sections; black is the text color throughout; a bright, saturated green marks the active navigation state — the only structural UI accent in the system. A palette of small, secondary colors appears on the history timeline as dot markers (one color per year or era), functioning as a chronological legend rather than a decorative system. Color in this design comes from the client work — the project thumbnails carry all the warmth, variety, and energy, while the surrounding structure maintains strict monochrome. This is a gallery-wall decision: the work should not have to compete with the frame.

## Typography System

The type system is modern sans-serif throughout — clean, confident, and at a scale that communicates certainty without aggression. The introductory statement sections deploy the headline at a reading-distance scale that invites engagement rather than imposing itself. Body text sits at a comfortable reading size with generous line height, treating the reading experience as one that deserves patience. The history timeline introduces date labels at a secondary heading scale — smaller than body headlines, clear as navigation markers. Underlined text links serve as the primary CTA affordance across all sections, a deliberate return to the web's most legible interaction convention. There is no display type, no script, no typographic ornament — the system communicates that the studio's aesthetic intelligence is expressed through work, not self-promotion.

## Spatial System

The spatial logic is container-based and editorial. All sections use a centered maximum-width container with consistent horizontal margins, preventing content from touching viewport edges and maintaining a sense that each section occupies a considered field. The Introductory Statement sections are the most spatially generous — large top and bottom margins isolate each typographic block as a standalone declaration. The Vertical Timeline introduces the tightest rhythm: a repeating chronological list where consistent vertical spacing between entries creates a scrollable archive. The project showcase breaks from the grid entirely — an overlapping collage where media thumbnails are positioned compositionally rather than algorithmically, creating a dense creative energy that contrasts deliberately with the spare surrounding sections.

## Photography and Editorial Voice

The project showcase is the site's editorial heart. Client work thumbnails — mixing still images and looping video — are presented as overlapping "windows" in a collage composition, each piece slightly layered over or under its neighbors, creating a sense of accumulated creative output. On hover, windows react: likely scaling up and rising in z-order, the interaction implying that each project deserves closer examination. The effect is less a portfolio and more a studio wall — the kind of creative surface that accumulates over time, with each piece placed when it was made rather than arranged for presentation. The timeline page adds the archival voice: a chronological record of projects and milestones rendered as a clean, repeating vertical list with dated labels.

## Navigation and Wayfinding

The navigation bar is non-sticky — it scrolls away with the page, ceding the full viewport to content once the user commits to reading. This is a deliberate choice that communicates confidence: once you're in, the site trusts you to find your way back. The navigation structure is minimal: three primary links, a logo, and a CTA — no dropdowns, no mega-menus. The green active-state highlight on the current page link is the navigation's only color moment, making orientation clear without elaborating it. The overall wayfinding philosophy is one of simplicity: few pages, clear labels, no navigation architecture that implies complexity the site doesn't have.

## Overall Design Character

Friends of the Web is a creative studio site that uses restraint as a form of confidence. The white ground, the non-sticky navigation, the absence of scroll animations, and the lack of a comprehensive accent system all argue that the work speaks without enhancement. The project showcase collage is the one moment of designed intensity — overlapping, looping, hover-responsive — and it works precisely because everything surrounding it is quiet. The design system is the frame; the client work is the painting. Getting out of the way is the entire philosophy.
