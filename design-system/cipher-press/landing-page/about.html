About

Language deserves the same rigour as code.

Built by editors and engineers

Recto began as an internal tool for a team of editors working on long-form academic and literary publishing. The problem was always the same: the structural problems that compound over a long document were invisible until they were expensive to fix.

Origin

Started in a proof room

The first version of Recto was a set of scripts that an editorial team ran on InDesign exports to catch terminology drift and cross-reference errors before a manuscript went to typesetting. It worked well enough that other teams asked to use it. That question — "can we have that?" — became the product.

Philosophy

Deterministic, not generative

Recto does not use large language models to suggest edits, rewrite copy, or fill in missing structure. That is a different kind of tool. Recto's intelligence is rule-based: the rules come from the editorial standards your team already holds, made explicit and applied consistently. We believe the editor's judgment should be preserved and amplified, not replaced.

Approach

The document as a system

Every document has internal logic — a set of structural and terminological commitments it makes to the reader. Recto treats the document as a system with rules: when those rules are broken, the system surfaces a discrepancy. When they're consistently maintained, the system confirms coherence. Editorial judgment still governs which rules apply and when to make an exception. Recto handles the audit.
