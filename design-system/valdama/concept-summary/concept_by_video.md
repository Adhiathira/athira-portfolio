# Valdama — Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page loads into its fully resolved state: the navigation bar is already solid and opaque, the hero carousel is already populated with its full set of product photographs. There is no loading sequence, no entrance animation on load, no text that builds or resolves. The opening frame is a static composition — products in place, type in place, interface in full operational readiness. The user arrives to a site that is already ready, not one that performs its readiness for them.

## Hero Experience

The hero section introduces the only horizontal scroll axis on the page — a full-bleed carousel of large-format product photographs navigated by arrow affordances at the left and right edges. This carousel does not auto-advance; it responds only to user input. The interaction is deliberate and indexical — each arrow press is a product-by-product browse rather than an automatic slide show. The images fill the carousel without any text overlay, and below the carousel a single small headline positions the user in the content hierarchy. When the user scrolls past the hero, the page shifts entirely to vertical navigation and the horizontal axis is not revisited.

## Section Transitions

Every section transition is a clean straight-edge horizontal cut. The primary transition vocabulary is generous vertical whitespace — large, consistent margins between each content block that function as visual pauses, creating a measured cadence without any animated transition effects. Because the background is uniformly white across almost every section, the transition between zones is purely spatial: the user moves from one content register to the next without any color event, gradient fade, or geometric ornament to mark the boundary. The only exception is the footer, which uses a darker ground and the ghost CTA button styling as a terminal differentiator.

## Content Rhythm

Scroll reveals are applied at the section level rather than the element level. As a content block enters the viewport, the entire block — image and text together as a single unit — fades in and slides up slightly. There is no element-level stagger, no cascading sequence within a section: image and text are not separated into discrete reveals. This reinforces the site's block-based spatial logic and keeps the reading experience continuous rather than fragmented. The effect is brief and non-intrusive — a gentle introduction rather than a performance. In the product collection grid, the primary interaction affordance is the hover-state image swap, which reveals a secondary product photograph without any animation beyond an immediate frame change.

## Footer Approach

The footer arrives at the end of the scroll journey as a neutral closure — no entrance animation, no tonal shift beyond the structural shift from the product grid to the informational multi-column layout. Ghost CTAs mark the footer's conversion moments (newsletter signup, etc.) in the same typographic register as everything above. There is no dramatic dark-background terminal statement; the footer is continuous with the page's logic, not a punctuation mark.

## Motion Principles

The motion vocabulary is reduced to a single technique applied uniformly: fade-in and slight upward slide on section entry. There is no ambient motion, no continuous animation, no scroll-actor, and no section-specific motion personality. The consistency of the single technique is itself the principle — the site does not modulate its motion vocabulary to indicate hierarchy, excitement, or conversion urgency. Every section enters the same way. The interaction-layer's most expressive moment is the hover-state product image swap in the collection grid — a still-to-still substitution rather than animation, which fits the site's logic: change through replacement, not through motion.

## Video Placements

**1. Hero Carousel — Single clip slide**
- Suggested file: `hero-product-in-motion.mp4`
- Subject: A single product from the brand's collection — a piece of furniture or a light fixture — shown in a moment of positioning or installation: a hand setting an object on a surface, a lamp being angled, a chair being placed in a room with neutral walls and natural light; the background should be as minimal and neutral as the still product photography; no faces; 6–8s loop; the clip should be designed to coexist with the static photograph slides in the carousel without disrupting the aesthetic register
- Duration: 6–8s loop
- Why video: The hero carousel presents products as static, isolated objects. A single video clip among the still photographs would introduce a temporal dimension — the product moving, being handled, occupying space — without disrupting the overall carousel's character. The clip communicates scale, material presence, and the physical quality of the object in a way that a studio photograph cannot. It earns its presence only if the aesthetic and lighting are indistinguishable from the surrounding still images at a glance.

**2. Split Feature Section — Product context**
- Suggested file: `feature-ambient-context.mp4`
- Subject: A product placed in an architectural interior — a lit room, a material surface, a spatial threshold (doorway, shelf, corner) — with the product as the subject and the space as the supporting context; neutral whites, warm naturals, or concrete tones consistent with high-design residential or hospitality interiors; no people; slow, barely-perceptible camera drift or material light shift; 8–10s loop
- Duration: 8–10s loop
- Why video: One of the split media-text feature sections — likely the one introducing the collection's editorial statement — would benefit from ambient video in the media column rather than a still image. The video provides spatial context and materiality that isolated studio shots cannot: the product in a real environment, with real light and real surfaces. The motion is ambient and slow, consistent with the site's restrained, non-performative aesthetic.
