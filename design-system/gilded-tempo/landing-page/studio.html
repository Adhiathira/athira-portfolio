The lab

People and precision.

Amber Gate has been operating from the same building in Rotterdam since 1994. The equipment has changed; the standard has not.

We built this to do one thing correctly.

Maarten de Vos founded the lab after a decade working as a darkroom technician for a commercial printing house. When digital production eliminated that department, he took the chemicals and the temperature-controlled room and kept going.

Today three people run Amber Gate full-time. We process between 900 and 1,100 rolls a month. We do not scan with AI upscaling. We do not offer "express" push processing. We do not accept film that arrives wet.

The constraints are not boutique aesthetics. They are how you avoid ruining someone's irreplaceable negative.

Principles

Temperature holds.

Developer temperature is maintained to ±0.1°C. Not approximately. Not close enough. Every bath, every run.

Batch chemistry.

We calculate development time per chemical batch, accounting for seasoning and replenishment. Off-the-shelf time charts are a starting point, not a method.

No guessing.

If we are uncertain about your film, we test on a clear leader before running the roll. This takes time. It is the correct use of time.

Equipment

What runs the lab.

The machines are not decorative. Each piece handles a specific task and handles it completely.

Jobo CPP-3

Rotary processor. Handles 35mm, 120, 4×5, and larger sheet film. Temperature precision via water bath. The workhorse for color and black-and-white rotary development.

Noritsu LS-600

Flatbed film scanner. Maximum 4000 dpi optical; 6400 software interpolated. 48-bit colour output. No infrared dust correction — we clean by hand.

Durst M605

Colour enlarger for optical printing up to 50×60cm. Cold-cathode light source. All dodging and burning done manually in the darkroom.
