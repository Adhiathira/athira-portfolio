The Making

Every decision,
accounted for.

We document our process because we believe the decisions made during making are part of the object. The client should know what they are holding.

01

Brief and Measurement

We begin with a conversation — typically on site. We want to understand the building, the door, the room it opens into. Dimensions are taken by hand. We ask about existing hardware on the building, about what the client considers correct weight and temperature in the hand. A written brief follows, specifying material, finish, mechanical requirements, and quantities.

02

Drawing and Pattern

Drawings are made by hand before being transferred to CAD for dimensional control. The working drawing is used to make the sand-cast pattern — a physical positive form in hardwood or resin from which the casting mould is made. Pattern making is where the object's geometry is committed to. We present the drawing and a physical mock-up in timber before proceeding.

03

Casting

Sand casting is done in our workshop. Brass (CW614N) or bronze (LG2) is melted and poured into a two-part sand mould made from the pattern. Each pour is individual — the grain of the sand leaves a subtle texture in the metal that is part of the object's surface. Sprues are removed and the rough casting inspected against the drawing. Any casting defect that affects function or surface is rejected at this stage.

04

Filing and Machining

Functional surfaces — spindle holes, rose plate faces, hinge knuckles — are machined to tolerance on the lathe or mill. The rest of the object is worked by hand: filed, draw-filed, and in some cases burnished. The mechanical tolerances are tight. The surface outside those tolerances is intentionally imprecise — not rough, but not machined either. The edge between the two is where the object's character lives.

05

Finish and Delivery

Finish is agreed at the brief stage. Options include: natural burnished (unlacquered, will patinate in use), dark wax patina, or acid-applied liver-of-sulphur patina for bronze. We do not lacquer — the hardware should age. Objects are delivered with installation notes, a care sheet, and — on request — the original drawing. A first service appointment is offered at 12 months.
