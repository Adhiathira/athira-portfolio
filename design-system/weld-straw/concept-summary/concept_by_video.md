# Motion Brief: Weld & Straw

## Scroll Opening

The page opens on a pale field — `#f2f2f2`, the color of uncoated paper pressed flat. Nothing announces itself. The heading weight arrives first: Inter at 700, the letterforms already present when the eye lands, no fade-in required for the primary headline. What does enter is the supporting material — a hairline rule sliding in from left at 400ms ease-out, the subheading rising 16px over 750ms from its resting position. The page does not perform its own arrival. It simply becomes visible. Navigation items resolve at 200ms delay intervals from left to right, their opacity climbing from zero with a timing that suggests patience rather than sequence. The total duration of the opening pass is under two seconds. It does not feel short.

## Hero Experience

The hero is a candidate for ambient video. The light background register means any video here reads through a translucent layer rather than dominating from darkness — the effect is more like a window than a backdrop. The suggested subject: a slow pullback across a workshop surface, morning light raking at a shallow angle across the material being made, whether that is paper, cloth, ceramic, or something edged and precise. The motion is ultra-slow, barely registering as movement until the eye compares frames. Duration: 5s loop, no visible join. Asset filename: `hero-bg.mp4`. The headline sits above this in contrast at full Inter 700 weight — the type does not compete with the video; it anchors it. The languid 0.75s transition cadence of the motion system means any video here should share that pacing: nothing that cuts, nothing that accelerates unexpectedly.

## Section Transitions

Transitions between sections do not announce themselves. As the user scrolls from hero into the first product or feature split, the section below rises to meet the viewport — a 300ms opacity-and-translate sequence, `translateY(20px)` resolving to zero with ease-out. There is no parallax shunt, no clip wipe, no background color flash. The dominant mechanism is the content entering calmly from below its natural position. Between the feature section and any manifesto or statement section, the background tone may shift — the light `#f2f2f2` giving way briefly to the warm near-black `#1a1a18` — and this shift is the transition. No additional animation is required when the environment itself changes register.

## Content Rhythm

Elements enter the viewport with the patience of a page turn. Product photography or illustration columns reveal on a vertical mask: the image slides upward from beneath a clipping boundary over 700ms, the mask releasing the image from its base rather than fading it into existence. This preserves edge sharpness and prevents the milky ambiguity of opacity-only entrances on light backgrounds. Text elements within feature sections stagger at 80ms intervals per line, entering from 12px below their rest position at zero opacity — the stagger is slow enough to read as considered, fast enough not to feel broken. In manifesto or full-width typographic sections, the oversized heading enters the viewport already present, the scroll itself delivering it rather than an animation performing its arrival.

## Footer Approach

The footer resolves with the same reserve the page opened with. As the final content section passes mid-viewport, footer columns begin fading in at 1.5s ease — the slowest transition in the system, matching the `1.5s` duration token. Column by column from left to right, 100ms stagger. Any circular logo mark or brand graphic in the footer background fades in at 70% opacity on a 200ms delay, making it feel like something already there that the eye is now permitted to notice. There is no conclusive animation, no signature flourish. The page simply ends — the way a well-made object feels complete without needing to declare itself finished.

## Motion Principles

The timing philosophy is **patient precision**. The primary duration range is 0.75s to 1.5s — longer than most UI systems, shorter than motion design. Nothing snaps. Easing curves favor ease-out for entrances (fast arrival, slow settle) and ease-in-out for sustained transitions. The system does not spring or bounce; there is no elastic overshoot. These are materials settling, not mechanics releasing. The 0.75s button transition means hover states feel unhurried — not sluggish, but deliberate. The distinction matters: deliberate belongs to craft; sluggish belongs to latency. On light-background sections, shadow and depth cues replace the atmospheric weight that dark palettes provide automatically — layered z-index, directional shadows from a consistent upper-left light source, and gradient overlays that deepen toward section edges rather than centering.

## Video Placements

**1. Hero Background — `hero-bg.mp4`**
Section role: Above-fold hero atmospheric layer. The video shows a slow pullback across a flat material surface — stone, paper, or worked metal — caught in early morning window light. The surface texture fills the frame edge to edge, with no identifiable subject other than the material itself. Motion quality: ultra-slow lateral drift, imperceptible at first glance, filmed at high frame rate and played at 50% speed. Mood: material gravity, the quiet authority of craft. Duration: 5s loop, invisible join. Video serves this section because the light palette (`#f2f2f2`) background alone reads as blank — a still image here would be a void. The barely-moving texture transforms the hero from an empty field into an inhabited surface, giving the bold Inter headline something to press against.

**2. Product or Feature Showcase — `craft-detail.mp4`**
Section role: Feature split panel, occupying the left or center column of an asymmetric two-column layout. The video shows hands performing a precise material action — measuring, folding, cutting, or setting — in close crop. No face visible, only hands and material. Shot from directly above or at a shallow angle. Motion quality: natural-paced gesture filmed at 60fps, played at full speed for one motion cycle, then held on the final frame before looping. Mood: competence, material specificity, the irreducible evidence of human attention. Duration: 8–10s with a 2s hold frame before loop point. Video serves this section because static product photography cannot convey process — and in a design system this restrained, where no decorative element is allowed, the motion of making is the only narrative available.

**3. Manifesto or Statement Section — `grain-field.mp4`**
Section role: Full-width background behind an oversized typographic statement — the section where the brand's register shifts from informational to declarative. The video shows an exterior field or landscape surface filmed at low angle: grain stalks, tidal flat, or poured material at edge of frame. The shot is overexposed slightly — the light end of the `#f2f2f2` register — so the background does not compete with the dark headline type layered above it. Motion quality: slow lateral wind drift or surface movement, essentially still, the motion registering only over 3–4 seconds. Mood: origin, terrain, the source material before it becomes product. Duration: 10s, the extended duration allowing the viewer to settle into the background before reading the headline. Video serves this section because a declarative typographic section needs scale that no static image delivers — the horizon implied by a moving landscape makes the claim of the headline feel geographically real rather than rhetorically imposed.
