The story

Why we built it.

Parable began in a law library. A litigation associate was building a brief from forty-seven sources — cases, statutes, secondary commentary, internal memos. She had four PDFs open, two Word documents, six browser tabs, and a legal pad. The argument was in her head. Getting it out of her head and into a form the partner could review was its own project.

The tools she had were built for writing, not for thinking. Notes apps record. Word processors format. Neither understands that an annotation is a relational object — that it points at a source, responds to another note, supports a claim, or places an objection.

Parable was built to hold that structure. Not to replace the thinking — to hold it while the thinking happens.

What we believe

Three principles.

Arguments have structure.

A claim is not the same as a note. Evidence is not the same as context. The tools we use should distinguish between them — not flatten everything into text.

Reading is collaborative.

A case never has just one reader. A document reviewed by three people accumulates three layers of insight. Parable makes those layers visible without collapsing them together.

The document is not the workspace.

The annotation should live beside the source, not inside it. The original text is the record. The margin is where thinking happens. These are different things and they should be treated differently.

The people

Built by people
who read for work.

Parable is a small team. Former litigators, a computational linguist, two engineers who spent years in document management infrastructure, and one editor who has been annotating manuscripts since before she had email. We are not a startup that discovered legal research. We are practitioners who ran out of patience with the tools.
