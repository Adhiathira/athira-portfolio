Platform architecture

A method designed for collections that can't afford to lose data.

The logic behind every design decision in Verdant Index — from the schema structure to the change-log system — reflects a single principle: institutional collections are irreplaceable, and the software that serves them must be built accordingly.

How it works

From first import to fully navigable collection.

  1. 01

    Collection intake and schema mapping

    Every onboarding begins with a structured intake: we review your existing data formats, identify your collection's specific taxonomic scope, and map your field labels to the Verdant Index canonical schema. No assumption is made about how your data is currently structured.

  2. 02

    Supervised data migration with provenance preservation

    Migration is supervised, not automated. Every record is reviewed for completeness and consistency before it enters the system. Provenance data — collector, date, locality, acquisition route — is preserved verbatim from source materials, including handwritten annotations where digitised.

  3. 03

    Taxonomic validation against current nomenclature

    Every specimen record is validated against the current accepted taxonomy at the point of import — and flagged for review whenever a nomenclatural change occurs downstream. Synonyms and basionyms are cross-referenced automatically.

  4. 04

    Access control and change attribution

    Every change to every record is logged with the user's identity, timestamp, and a diff of what changed. Access is controlled by role — researcher, curator, administrator — with granular permission over which collections and record types each role can modify.

  5. 05

    Export and interoperability

    Records can be exported in Darwin Core, CSV, and JSON at any time. Integration with external herbarium networks (GBIF, Index Herbariorum) is available on institutional plans. Your data is yours — unencumbered, fully portable, and documented to the standard that other institutions expect.
