Chapter II

A reading experience,
not a content experience.

Every feature in Folio was designed around one question: does this serve the reader's attention, or does it steal it?

01

Editorial typography, natively.

Folio renders your prose in Lora at 1.85 line-height — the exact measure that reading researchers call the sweet spot for sustained attention. Drop caps, pull quotes, and marginal annotations are first-class citizens, not add-ons.

02

Chapter structure as navigation.

Long stories have structure. Folio lets you mark chapters with bronze numerals and gives readers a reading-progress bar — not a scroll bar — that maps the journey remaining. They know they're in the middle of something worth finishing.

03

Annotations in the margin.

Add contextual notes, citations, or author asides directly in the margin of your story. Readers hover a footnote marker and the annotation slides in from the right. Like reading a scholar's annotated edition. Like being trusted with the thinking behind the words.

Chapter III

Three steps to publishing that reads like a book.

Step One

Write in the editor designed for long stories.

Folio's editor is a distraction-free writing surface — warm off-white, Lora body type, no toolbars unless you need them. Write a sentence. Write a chapter. Write a book. The editor grows with your story without ever interrupting the act of writing. Paste from Word, import from Substack, or begin from a blank page. The story is yours before you write the first word.

"Writing on Folio felt like writing in a notebook — except the notebook already knew how to typeset itself." — Miranda Cho, author of The Longer View
Step Two

Structure your story with chapters and annotations.

Mark chapter divisions with a keystroke. The chapter number appears in bronze. Add marginal annotations — those author asides that make a literary essay feel like a conversation. Set a pull quote to emphasize the sentence that defines your argument. The structure of your thinking becomes the structure of the page.

Step Three

Publish to an audience that chose long-form.

Your Folio readers chose to be here. They signed up for depth, not content. When you publish, they receive a notification that reads like a letter, not an alert. Your story opens to a warm paper page with their progress bar waiting at zero — ready to begin. There is no algorithm deciding whether your story reaches them. You wrote it, they subscribed, they read it. That's the whole system.
