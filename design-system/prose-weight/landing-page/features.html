Features

Every feature is a decision about what reading deserves.

We didn't copy the feature list from other publishing platforms. We asked: what does long-form writing need that nothing else provides? The answer is in this list.

"Most publishing tools were designed for content. Folio was designed for reading."
Chapter I

The editorial layer.

Typography, structure, and the tools that have defined great print journalism for two hundred years — now native to the web.

01

Drop caps and opening marks.

Every chapter can begin with a drop capital — Playfair Display at 5rem, wine red, floating into the first paragraph. A typographic tradition from manuscript illumination brought into the browser without hacks.

02

Pull quotes as structure.

Mark any sentence as a pull quote and it becomes a visual anchor — Playfair Display italic, bronze left border, full measure. The sentence the reader will carry with them.

03

Marginal annotations.

Add annotations that live in the right margin. Source Sans Pro, 0.8125rem, warm slate. Readers hover a footnote marker and the annotation slides in — the scholarly aside, the contextual note, the author aside made elegant.

Chapter II

The reading layer.

Built for the reader's attention, not the platform's metrics.

Reading Progress

A bar that measures the story, not the scroll.

The reading progress bar at the top of every Folio page tells the reader exactly how much story remains. Wine red, two pixels, filling left to right as they scroll. It is the only interactive chrome on the page — a promise that this story has a shape, a beginning and an end, worth completing.

Chapter Navigation

Bronze chapter markers as wayfinding.

Readers can see the chapter structure of a long story before they begin. Bronze chapter numbers with generous letter-spacing. A sidebar navigation for stories over three chapters. The reader knows what they're committing to — and that the commitment is worth making.

"A reading progress bar is not a UX feature. It's a promise: this story has been designed to be finished." — Folio design notes, 2025
Typography Engine

Lora at 1.85 line-height — the reading sweet spot.

The Lora body type at 1.1rem with 1.85 line-height isn't a stylistic choice — it's grounded in reading research. At this measure and leading, the eye can track a line from end to return without losing its place. Readers can sustain attention for 15 minutes, not 90 seconds. That's what Folio is built to support.
