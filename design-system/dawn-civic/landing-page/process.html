How we work

The process

From survey brief to archival edition — four stages, no shortcuts.

"A map is an act of responsibility. It claims to describe the world as it is. We take that claim seriously."

— Ordnance Press Editorial Standard, 1923

Four stages.

01

Survey Brief

Every map begins with a document. The survey brief defines the geographic extent, the intended use, the required accuracy standard, and the archival classification. For institutional commissions, this brief is co-authored with the commissioning body. For catalogue editions, the brief is authored internally against our editorial standards. No map enters production without a signed brief.

→ assets/process-survey-brief.jpeg
02

Source Survey

We work from primary survey data wherever possible — municipal cadastral records, geodetic benchmark networks, and, for historic editions, original field survey notebooks held in national archives. Derived data is always declared. Our cartographers are trained in primary survey interpretation and in the historiography of cartographic sources. We know the difference between a measurement and an estimate, and we mark that difference on the sheet.

03

Hand Drafting

Ordnance Press maps are hand-drafted. The linework is produced using technical drawing instruments on translucent drafting film, then transferred to archival printing stock through a contact process that preserves line quality at the resolution of the original drafting. Each sheet is reviewed by a cartographer independent of the one who drew it, who signs the reverse with their name and the review date.

→ assets/process-hand-drafting.jpeg
04

Archival Edition

Print production uses acid-free archival stock with a predicted lifespan of 500+ years under standard archive storage conditions. Every edition is numbered, with a certificate of provenance indicating the primary data sources, the survey date, the cartographer's name, and the print run size. Institutional commissions receive a digitised master file in addition to the physical edition.

Standards

Who draws the maps

Ordnance Press employs six full-time cartographers and maintains relationships with a network of specialist consultants for regional survey work. All cartographers hold postgraduate qualifications in cartography or geodesy and have at least five years of primary survey experience. We do not outsource drafting. We do not use automated line generation. Every line on every map is drawn by a person who understands what it represents.
