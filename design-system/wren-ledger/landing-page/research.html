Research

How we conduct primary source investigation and what standards govern every conclusion we document.

Foundation

The Genealogical Proof Standard

Every conclusion in a Wren Ledger report must satisfy the Genealogical Proof Standard — the profession's authoritative benchmark for evidence assessment. This is not a preference or a quality level we offer as an option. It is the basis from which we work and below which we will not descend.

The Standard requires a reasonably exhaustive search of all sources likely to yield pertinent information; complete and accurate source citations for every record consulted; analysis of each source, information, and evidence for its value and limitations; resolution of conflicting evidence; and a written statement of the conclusions drawn from the evidence assembled.

Where the evidence is insufficient to meet the Standard, we report what the evidence does establish — and document clearly where the line of verifiable knowledge ends. We do not speculate past that line.

Primary Sources

Record types we work with

Vital Records

Civil Registration & Church Records

Birth, marriage, and death registrations from civil repositories (typically available from the mid-19th century onward), supplemented by parish records extending back several centuries. We work with baptism, marriage banns, burial registers, and episcopal confirmation books — often the only records available before civil registration began.

Access to these records varies significantly by country and region. We maintain working knowledge of which repositories hold which collections, and we work directly with original records wherever digitization is incomplete or absent.

Census Materials

Household & Population Surveys

National and regional census returns, household schedules, and population registers. Valuable for household composition, ages, occupations, and birthplaces — but assessed cautiously for accuracy of age and birthplace data.

Legal Documents

Notarial & Probate Archives

Wills, land transfers, contracts, and inventories. Among the richest genealogical sources: probate records name heirs explicitly and often resolve questions vital records cannot answer.

State Records

Military & Conscription Rolls

Military enlistment, discharge, pension, and conscription records. Valuable for confirming age, birthplace, physical description, and — in pension files — family relationships and post-service history.

Movement Records

Emigration & Immigration Documents

Passenger lists, ship manifests, port departure records, naturalization papers, and border crossing documentation. Critical for tracing families across national boundaries.

Output

What we deliver

  • Research Report

    A formal written account of all research conducted, findings established, and conclusions drawn. Includes complete source citations in standard genealogical format, assessment of evidence quality, and explicit notation of unresolved questions.

  • Genealogical Charts

    Indexed pedigree charts and family group sheets in both digital and print format. Charts are annotated with source references so any individual fact can be traced back to the record that established it.

  • Document Portfolio

    High-resolution copies or scans of key primary source documents, with translations provided for records in languages other than the client's own. Documents are indexed to the relevant individuals in the report.

  • Family Narrative

    An optional extended narrative placing the documented lineage in historical context — the conditions, events, and movements that shaped the lives of the individuals whose records we found. This is not embellishment; it is the record made readable.
