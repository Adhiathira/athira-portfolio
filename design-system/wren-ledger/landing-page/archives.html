Archives

The repositories we access — national archives, diocesan collections, regional registries, and institutional holdings across Europe, the Americas, and beyond.

By Collection Type

What archives hold, and how we reach them

Civil Registration

State-administered vital records from the 19th century onward. Typically held by national or regional archives — access varies by country, and many collections are only partially digitized.

Church & Diocesan

Parish registers, confirmation records, marriage banns. The deepest historical source for most European lineages — often running to the 16th century in well-preserved dioceses.

Notarial Archives

Legal instruments: wills, property transfers, apprenticeship bonds, commercial contracts. Rich for identifying family relationships not captured in vital records.

National Collections

Military rolls, census schedules, naturalization papers, land surveys, emigration records. Held by national archives and often accessible only through in-person visits or authorized agents.

By Geography

Regions of active research

We maintain working knowledge of repositories, access protocols, and record survival in the following regions. This list reflects current capacity; for regions not listed, please enquire.

  • British Isles

    England, Wales, Scotland, Ireland, Northern Ireland — civil registration from 1837–1864, parish registers to 16th century, census 1841–1921.

  • Western Europe

    France, Germany, Netherlands, Belgium, Switzerland, Austria — civil and church records, notarial archives, guild and municipal registers.

  • Eastern Europe

    Poland, Czech Republic, Slovakia, Hungary, Romania, Ukraine — metrical books, civil registration, Austro-Hungarian records, Pale of Settlement documentation.

  • Scandinavia

    Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland — among the best-preserved parish records in the world, household examination rolls from the 17th century onward.

  • Southern Europe

    Italy, Spain, Portugal, Greece — parish and civil records, notarial archives, emigration documentation for diasporic research across the Americas.

  • North America

    United States, Canada — federal and state/provincial records, naturalization, census, vital records, Indigenous records, freedmen's bureau documentation.

  • Latin America

    Mexico, Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Uruguay — church records from colonial period, civil registration, immigration records for European diaspora research.

  • Middle East & North Africa

    Israel, Morocco, Tunisia, Egypt, Turkey — Jewish community records, Ottoman archives, colonial-period civil documentation, Sephardic diaspora tracing.

Access

How we access restricted and remote collections

A significant proportion of genealogical records remain undigitized and accessible only in person or through authorized correspondents. We maintain a network of research agents in key repositories across Europe, the Americas, and the Middle East — researchers who work with us under Wren Ledger standards and document every record consulted.

For collections where we have established access protocols — national archives, diocesan offices, regional registries — we can typically confirm access timelines before an engagement begins. For novel repositories, we investigate access conditions as part of the initial research scoping.

We are transparent about what a collection is likely to yield before you commission us to search it. If the probability of finding a specific record type is low given survival rates or access conditions, we say so.
