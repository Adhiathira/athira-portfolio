Frequently asked questions

  • How long does a genealogical research engagement typically take?

    It depends entirely on the scope and the records available. A focused research question — tracing one line back three generations in a well-documented region — may take six to ten weeks. A multi-generational, multi-country engagement can run to eighteen months or more. We establish realistic timelines at the outset and update you as the research progresses.

  • What if the records needed to answer my question no longer exist?

    Record loss is a real condition of genealogical research. We are transparent about survival rates and destruction histories before you commission us to search. If a question cannot be resolved because the relevant records were destroyed, we document that clearly — along with what the surviving records do show — rather than substituting conjecture.

  • Can you prepare documentation for a legal proceeding?

    Yes. We regularly prepare genealogical documentation for probate matters, heirship determinations, and citizenship by descent applications. We are familiar with the evidentiary standards required by legal proceedings in England and Wales, the United States, and several European jurisdictions. We recommend engaging us as early as possible in proceedings where genealogical evidence is needed.

  • Do you work with DNA evidence?

    Yes, where it is relevant and useful. DNA evidence is most valuable when documentary records are exhausted — it cannot replace primary source research, but it can confirm relationships and break through brick walls that paper records cannot resolve. We assess DNA evidence under the same evidentiary standards as documentary records.

  • What does the initial consultation involve?

    A conversation — typically by telephone or written exchange — in which we discuss what you know, what you are trying to establish, and what the records are likely to show. We will advise on whether the research is feasible, what complications are likely, and what a realistic scope and timeline would look like. There is no obligation beyond the conversation.
