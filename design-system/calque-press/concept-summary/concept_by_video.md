# Calque Press — Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page loads fully composed: the nav is already in position, the first editorial cluster is on screen with all its elements at rest. There is no loading sequence, no staggered entrance, no countdown to the moment content becomes legible. The custom geometric dot cursor — if present — appears immediately. The opening is a fact, not an announcement. The restraint of this entry is itself information: a site this confident in its visual register does not need an overture.

## Hero Experience

There is no conventional hero section. Showcase pages begin directly with the first content cluster of the asymmetric editorial layout — an image and its paired text block, already composed in their final positions. The hero video candidate for this system is a near-still ambient background: a slow drift across a clean, minimal surface — white paper, an open book, a folded document in cool flat light — at a pace barely distinguishable from stillness. The video's subject is `hero-ambient.mp4`. The motion is so slow (0.5px/s) that it reads as a living texture rather than a scene. Duration: 12s loop. The confidence move here is that the video may be mistaken for a still; the discovery that it moves is its own small reward.

## Section Transitions

Section transitions are defined by structural shifts rather than visual events. The "transition" between content clusters is the act of scrolling through generous whitespace — the negative space between clusters is the transition, a typographic pause before the next content pair arrives. Between layout paradigms (editorial → grid → centered CTA), the structural shift is the event. No animated dividers, no color wipes, no section-entry hero moments. The scroll is the mechanism and the experience simultaneously.

## Content Rhythm

Scroll reveals are consistent and understated. As content items enter the viewport they execute a brief fade combined with a gentle upward slide — uniform across all content types, applied in sequence as each element crosses the viewport threshold. The stagger derives from scroll position rather than a timed sequence. The effect is a measured revelation that matches the site's overall pace rather than announcing itself as a designed moment. Motion here is a courtesy, not a feature.

## Footer Approach

Each page closes with the minimal centered CTA section before the footer: a headline, a short text block, and the pill-shaped CTA button centered in the viewport. This terminal section has a simple fade reveal — the page's single concession to visual gentleness. The footer itself is a utility block: same off-white ground, same black text, no color change, no visual announcement. The page closes as quietly as it opened.

## Motion Principles

The timing scale is measured and formal. Primary transition durations run 0.26–0.52s — long enough to feel deliberate, short enough never to feel indulgent. The easing palette is simple: ease for most transitions, linear for continuous elements, ease-in for panels that open. There is no spring physics, no bounce, no cubic-bezier theater. Motion is used where it aids the reading experience and nowhere else. The timing philosophy is one of pure content deference: the work on screen is the subject, and motion exists only to introduce it gracefully.

## Video Placements

**1. Hero — Editorial ambient background**
- Section role: Hero / opening composition
- Suggested asset filename: `hero-ambient.mp4`
- What the video shows: Extremely slow overhead drift across a minimal studio surface — white paper, a folded document, a pencil at rest — in cool, even natural light. Nearly motionless; perceptible only after 3–4 seconds of watching. The video reads as a living off-white texture rather than a distinct scene.
- Duration: 12s tight loop
- Why video serves this section better than a still image: The site's opening section is defined by a cool off-white ground that is structurally neutral. A near-still video gives that ground a material presence — the suggestion that it exists in physical space — without competing with the editorial photography or typography placed over it. The temporal quality here is not spectacle but depth: the page feels inhabited rather than rendered.

**2. Feature / Process section — Studio materiality**
- Section role: Process or craft showcase
- Suggested asset filename: `process-close.mp4`
- What the video shows: Macro shot of hands handling a printed document — folding, pressing, smoothing — in warm-neutral light against a pale surface. Handheld but controlled, shot at moderate speed so the tactile quality of paper reads clearly. 8s loop ending at a natural still point.
- Duration: 8s loop
- Why video serves this section better than a still image: A process section describing editorial or craft work earns a temporal representation that a still image cannot provide. The movement of hands on paper communicates the physical act of making — the weight of the object, the grain of the surface — in a way that a single frame collapses into ambiguity. The handheld quality adds presence without sacrificing the system's formal restraint.

**3. Closing CTA — Atmospheric negative space**
- Section role: Terminal CTA / closing
- Suggested asset filename: `closing-ambient.mp4`
- What the video shows: An extremely slow pull-back from a close crop of white paper or a printed page — barely moving, 2–3mm of travel over the full duration — in the same cool flat light as the hero. The subject is negative space approaching abstraction. 10s loop.
- Duration: 10s loop
- Why video serves this section better than a still image: The closing section is structurally minimal — a headline, a short paragraph, a pill button. A near-still ambient video gives this section depth and atmosphere without content, reinforcing the system's core proposition: that space itself is meaningful. The slow pull-back creates a subtle sense of the page opening outward — an appropriate metaphor for a closing invitation.
