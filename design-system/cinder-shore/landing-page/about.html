Our story

Started in a machine shop
on the Bergen waterfront.

Tidemark was founded in 1984 by hydrographic engineer Lars Eriksen, who had spent the previous decade maintaining obsolete temperature loggers on Norwegian continental shelf surveys. The instruments failed predictably, and the calibration documentation was always incomplete. He started Tidemark to solve both problems.

The first CTD prototype was built in a rented machine shop on Marineholmen and deployed in the FRAM Strait on a Norwegian Meteorological Institute mooring. It returned with 14 months of continuous data. The calibration held within specification. The company was four people.

Forty years later, Tidemark instruments have been deployed in every ocean on Earth, including under the ice of the Arctic and Antarctic, in the trench systems of the Pacific, and on the mooring arrays that underpin international climate monitoring programmes. We remain in Bergen. The machine shop is larger.

We do not have a consumer product line. We do not manufacture instruments for recreational diving. Our entire output goes to the approximately 800 marine research institutions, hydrographic services, and offshore survey companies worldwide who require measurement documentation that can support peer-reviewed publication.

What we hold to

Principles that have not changed in forty years.

Traceability over claims

Every calibration certificate names the reference standard, the measurement conditions, and the uncertainty chain. We do not certify performance without documentation that can be independently verified.

Fail in the lab, not at depth

Every instrument is pressure-tested to 110% of rated depth before shipment. Instruments that do not pass are not repaired and shipped — they are remade. Reputation is harder to recover than a unit.

Forty years from now

We design instruments for decade-scale data programmes. This means replaceable sensors, documented calibration history, and published software interfaces. We are not interested in planned obsolescence.
