# Lo and Behold — Concept by Image

## Brand Premise

A digital creative studio whose site functions as both portfolio and proof of craft. The design earns credibility through structural invention rather than biographical explanation: the conventional portfolio card is replaced with a custom "wavy-card" shape (a vertical rectangle whose top and bottom edges are defined by shallow concave arcs), organic multi-lobed blob shapes frame editorial media on project detail pages, and an animated sinusoidal wave divider marks the site's single most important structural boundary — the passage between atmospheric hero and evidential content. The site communicates that its makers understand what makes design feel handmade rather than assembled.

## Color Architecture

The palette operates in two registers that alternate as the user descends. Dark near-black or charcoal fills dominate the hero, typographic statement sections, and project detail pages — achieving a cinematic contrast that privileges light typography over visual noise. A light gray or off-white background shifts the portfolio grid section into a different key, marking the page's primary dark-to-light chapter break. The extracted `pageBackground: #eeeeee` confirms a medium cool gray as the structural surface. `headingText: #0057ff` establishes electric blue as the brand's chromatic identity — appearing on headings and as the defining accent of a largely dark-neutral system. `wp--preset--color--lime: #f0ff3d` is held in the palette as a secondary graphic accent. Navigation links are white against the dark hero. The color vocabulary is graphic and high-contrast — no soft gradients, no muted tonal ranges.

## Typography System

TT Norms Pro (proprietary geometric sans) operates as the sole typeface across all weights. The display scale lands at 60px with 400 weight and −2.4px tracking — a compressed geometric effect that gives headlines a tighter, more deliberate character than the weight alone would suggest. UI and label elements use 20px/400 — a dramatic size step down that concentrates visual authority in the display tier. Buttons are 14px/500. The type system communicates through scale contrast rather than serif/sans editorial split: one face, one family, a wide vertical range.

## Spatial System

Four section types define the page. Hero: full-bleed video background with centered wordmark and descriptive text cluster, animated sinusoidal wave at the bottom boundary. Typographic statement: a single large headline centered on a dark field — a pure reading pause, no imagery. Portfolio grid: two-column card layout on light gray background, each card framing its media through the custom concave-arced "wavy" mask. Asymmetric editorial sections (on project detail pages): composition-driven layouts that intentionally break from the grid, pairing oversized organic blob-shaped media frames with offset text blocks, behaving more like a print magazine spread than a web component. Section boundaries alternate between the animated wave (for the single cinematic transition) and straight horizontal edges (for all structural ones).

## Photography and Editorial Voice

Media is presented through two custom frame shapes that are the site's most distinctive visual decision. The wavy-card: a static, symmetrical concave-arc rectangle used consistently in the portfolio grid — its shallow curves sufficient to signal bespoke craft without overwhelming the content. The organic blob: a freeform, multi-lobed asymmetric shape used as a media mask on project pages — naturalistic, irregular, and conspicuously handmade. Both shapes place a visible designer's mark on the media container itself, not just on the media within it. Project media ranges from still photography to looping video within the same grid track.

## Navigation and Wayfinding

A sticky three-zone nav: wordmark left, 3–4 primary links center, solid-fill CTA button right. The nav opens fully transparent over the dark hero with light-colored text; on scroll past the hero, it transitions to an opaque solid dark background while text and links remain light. The nav height and padding do not change on scroll — only the background fill. A custom cursor is present throughout, providing ambient feedback on interactivity and reinforcing the premium register without requiring any visible UI indicator. The nav's adaptive dark transition is not a glass/blur effect — it is a solid fill.

## Overall Design Character

Lo and Behold is a studio site that makes its strongest arguments through structure and shape rather than through copy or case study length. The animated wave divider is the page's one performative gesture — reserved for the boundary that most deserves it, between atmospheric opening and evidential work. The wavy-card and blob shapes signal that the people who built this tool can build things that cannot be produced by a template. The electric blue heading accent and lime palette secondary are chromatic assertions against an otherwise achromatic dark-gray system. The result is a site that demonstrates taste precisely where taste is hardest to fake: in the structural decisions that are invisible when they work.
