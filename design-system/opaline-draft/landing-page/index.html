The Forme method

Structured from the first line. Not from the last.

Most specification tools are built around export — structured enough to print, too loose to query. Forme is structured at entry. Every value has a type, a unit, a tolerance model, and a revision ancestry before it leaves your screen.

Read the method

How it works

From nominal to signed-off in four steps.

Forme structures the specification workflow so every decision is recorded at the moment it's made — not reconstructed later from memory or file metadata.

  1. Step 01

    Create the base record

    Name the part, attach the geometry reference (STEP, DXF, or a hand entry), and set the design authority. Forme generates the record skeleton from the geometry's dimensional features.

  2. Step 02

    Assign tolerances and surface requirements

    Each nominal dimension gets a tolerance assignment: bilateral, unilateral, ISO fit, or custom. Surface finish call-outs are linked to the face reference, not a separate drawing layer.

  3. Step 03

    Review and resolve

    Engineering review happens inside Forme. Comments are attached to the specific field they address. Revisions branch from the current state — nothing is overwritten, everything is auditable.

  4. Step 04

    Issue and track the handoff

    Issue a supplier view, a drawing package, or a machine-readable JSON handoff. Track when the supplier accessed the record, what they acknowledged, and what questions they raised.

The material record

Surface is specification. Forme documents both.

The opaline near-white ground of a machined ceramic part is not an afterthought — it is a precision call-out. Forme records the Ra value, the process that achieves it, and the inspection method that verifies it. All three, linked to the face.
