# Quiet Plinth — Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page arrives fully assembled — white field, fixed navigation, hero split already in place. There is no loading sequence, no entrance orchestration. The visitor is not made to wait. The only motion at load is a 0.9s fade-in of the hero content panel text (heading, body, buttons), timed to feel like the page is finding its composure rather than performing it. The photograph on the left half is present from frame zero: still, lit, complete.

## Hero Experience

The hero split does not animate in the conventional sense. The left photographic panel holds. The right cream content panel delivers its text via a staggered fade-up sequence — heading first at 0.9s delay, body copy at 1.1s, buttons at 1.3s — each element rising 20px into position over 0.9s, eased out. The pacing is close to still life: deliberate, unhurried. If the hero is designated a video background candidate, the subject for the left panel is: close observation of a completed arrangement being set on a stone or concrete surface — a slow push-in, the light warming as the camera nears, 10s loop. The motion should feel gravitational rather than decorative.

## Section Transitions

As the visitor scrolls past the hero into the alternating split-media sections, each section performs a single reveal: the photograph fades in from opacity 0 over 0.9s as it enters the viewport, while the content column slides up 24px over the same duration with a 0.18s offset. The alternating left/right rhythm of sections creates a natural scan-path — the eye follows the photograph first, then settles on the text column. No section transition fights the photography for attention; every animation defers to the image.

## Content Rhythm

Within each section, text elements arrive in a tight stagger: heading appears 0.27s after the section container enters viewport, body copy at 0.45s, CTA button at 0.63s. The stagger is small enough to read as a single breath rather than a choreographed sequence. Blog cards in the three-column grid appear on a per-row basis — each row fades up once 60% of it is visible, with a 0.1s delay between cards within the row. The net effect is a page that unfolds at the pace of careful reading.

## Footer Approach

The footer arrives without ceremony — a continuation of the white field, the four-column structure present at first scroll. The only closing animation is a slow horizontal reveal of the copyright strip: the text slides in from left to right across 1.08s, arriving precisely as the footer becomes fully visible. No dramatic outro. The page ends the way it began: quietly, already in place.

## Motion Principles

The timing philosophy throughout is languid deliberateness. Primary durations cluster around 0.9s — unusually long for hover transitions in web design, but exactly right here: the extra time signals confidence rather than anxiety. The system treats motion as confirmation, not demonstration. Hover states at 0.45s are perceptibly slow enough to feel considered; the visitor notices not the animation but the consequence of the animation — the state they have arrived at. Easing curves favor ease-out (deceleration into the target state) over bounce or spring, because this design has no appetite for whimsy.

## Video Placements

**1. Hero Left Panel — Material ambient loop**
- Suggested file: `hero-material-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: Extreme close range on a floral arrangement being placed onto a slab of pale stone — the florist's hands withdraw from frame as the arrangement settles; a 10s loop held on the composition as warm morning window light shifts incrementally across the surface; barely any movement after the initial placement; the motion is in the light, not the subject
- Duration: 10s
- Why video serves this section: The hero's left panel is the primary argument for why this place is worth entering. A still photograph stakes the claim once. A near-still video restates it with the quality of presence — the light continues to exist, the arrangement continues to breathe. The temporal depth transforms the split-screen from a layout decision into an atmosphere.

**2. Services / Bespoke Events Section — Studio preparation ambient loop**
- Suggested file: `studio-preparation.mp4`
- Subject: Wide, mid-height view of a studio worktable in the early stages of arrangement — stems sorted by color and length, vases half-filled with water, a pair of hands working methodically through a pile of fresh material; the camera does not move; 8s loop; the motion is in the material, hands, and water, not the frame; natural directional light from a single window source
- Duration: 8s
- Why video serves this section: A bespoke/events upsell needs to communicate capability without promotional language. A near-still ambient video of active preparation — the actual labor of floristry in a specific room — does this more precisely than any copywriting can. It shows that the team is present, in the work, every day.

**3. Product Grid Section — Single stem detail loop**
- Suggested file: `product-stem-detail.mp4`
- Subject: A macro close-up of a single stem — perhaps a ranunculus or garden rose — in a glass vessel, its petals barely moving in a breath of air; very shallow depth of field, background a soft warm cream; 5s loop; the stem is the only subject; no hands, no studio context, no branding
- Duration: 5s
- Why video serves this section: The product grid is where design intelligence most often retreats to catalog photography. A single ambient detail video placed as an accent element — not replacing the product shots but positioned as one frame within the grid — gives the section a pulse. It reminds the visitor that these are living things, not catalogue entries.
