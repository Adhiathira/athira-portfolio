# Quiet Plinth — Concept by Image

## Brand Premise

A local Auckland florist positioned through aesthetic rather than scale. The Botanist Florist is a brick-and-mortar florist with an online delivery service, and its website makes no attempt to appear larger than it is — the design is grounded in the specific address, the physical studio, the named florists, and the texture of real flower-arranging work. The brand register is minimal and warm: premium local craft, not aspirational DTC. The site exists to move visitors from the homepage to a product category and a purchase, but it does this through editorial restraint and genuine photography rather than promotional pressure. The result is a site that feels like the brand's window display: composed, confident, and quietly beautiful.

## Color Architecture

White (#ffffff) covers all primary surfaces — navigation background, content panels, footer, blog section. A warm cream or light beige field appears as the secondary surface, most notably in the hero's right panel (the content side of the split-screen), creating a subtle temperature distinction between the photographic half and the typographic half. The dominant typographic color is near-black (#161616 body, #092222 headings — the heading color carries a barely perceptible cool-green undertone). The brand's identity color is dark forest green (#3f4d33), used for the primary CTA buttons, the logo wordmark, and the thin text labels in the page header. A warm golden brown (#b0915b) functions as the secondary accent — appearing as a subtle material signal in the photography rather than as a primary UI color. The palette communicates nature, warmth, and restraint without the beige-and-sage cliché: it grounds the brand in an actual place (a florist studio with concrete floors and warm directional light) rather than in a generic lifestyle category.

## Typography System

A two-family system creates the editorial character. Cormorant Garamond at low-to-medium weight handles all headings — its fine serifs and classical proportions carry the precision of a jeweler's hand, the kind of letterform that feels expensive not because it shouts but because it holds its ground in negative space. The heading tracking is deliberately opened: 0.06em at display sizes, so each character breathes without separating. Jost handles body copy and navigation links — a geometric sans with the clean neutrality of a well-made label, contemporary without being fashionable. Together, the two families map a familiar editorial split: Cormorant Garamond makes editorial statements, Jost moves information. The type scale is compact and the result is a typographic system that feels measured and considered without calling attention to itself.

## Spatial System

The page operates in two spatial modes. The hero is a full-viewport horizontal split: the left half is occupied entirely by a large photograph extending edge-to-edge of the left panel; the right half is a cream-toned content panel containing a section headline, a short body block, and two CTA buttons aligned left. The split is 50/50, creating compositional balance between the photographic and typographic halves. Below the hero, the page transitions to alternating split-media sections — each with a large rectangular photo on one side and a content column (heading, body, single CTA) on the other, alternating left/right positions across sections. A three-column blog card grid appears toward the footer. All sections use white background with a container-based layout; there are no full-bleed dark sections and no decorative section dividers. The footer continues on white, organized in four columns (logo, Links, Terms, Contact). Section padding is generous: 96px vertical breathing room frames each content block so text and image are never competing for the same air — the space itself is a design decision, communicating that everything on screen was placed deliberately.

## Photography and Editorial Voice

Photography is the site's primary differentiator. All images are shot in a warm, natural-light register consistent with the florist's actual working environment: the storefront exterior, the studio floor with arrangements in progress, product compositions on neutral surfaces. The photography avoids the generic lifestyle flatness of stock imagery — it shows real work in a real place, with ambient detail (arranging tools, flowers in buckets, the concrete floor) visible in the frame. There is a weight to these photographs that cannot be simulated: the roughness of dried stems, the cold resistance of glass vases, the soft resistance of fresh petals beneath a florist's thumb. The hero photo centers on a person holding a completed bouquet in front of the storefront window, directly connecting the digital experience to the physical location. This editorial honesty is the brand's defining design choice: it signals an owner-operated business with genuine craft rather than a platform-built floral delivery service.

## Navigation and Wayfinding

The navigation sits on a white background bar below a thin announcement bar at the very top (yellow/coral background with dark promotional text). The nav structure is standard: the stacked logo wordmark on the left, a small set of product and utility links in the center, and account/cart/search icons on the right. Navigation links are set in small uppercase Jost with 0.08em letter spacing — a refined tracking that reads as considered rather than decorative. The nav height is compact and unobtrusive. There are no dropdowns visible in the primary navigation; category access is direct. The nav does not compete with the content below it; it is a functional wayfinding bar, not a designed feature.

## Overall Design Character

The Quiet Plinth aesthetic is minimal, photo-forward, and materially grounded. Three decisions hold the whole system together: the warm-neutral white/cream surface that allows the floral photography to carry all chromatic energy; the 50/50 hero split that treats the storefront photograph with the same compositional weight as the editorial text; and the languid motion system (0.45s hover transitions, 0.9s accordion easing) that refuses urgency. The design does not attempt the premium DTC aesthetic. It is honest about what it is — craft, presented carefully. Every space that is left empty is a choice. Every transition that takes just slightly too long is a choice. The system implies a sensibility that knows when to hold back.
