How It Is Made

From specimen
to impression

A complete Bramble & Dyce edition takes twelve days from collection to dispatch. Each step is performed by hand, in sequence, with no mechanical shortcuts.

01

Specimen Collection

On the appointed day, specimens of the season’s chosen plant are collected from Mainland, Orkney, within a defined two-kilometre radius of the mill. The plant must be in peak condition: leaves intact, stem undamaged, roots cleaned. No substitutions from outside the boundary. This is not a rule about provenance. It is a rule about specificity.

assets/process-collection.jpeg
02

Preparation and Drying

Specimens are pressed flat between layers of blotting paper and left for a minimum of seven days under weight. The drying time determines the quality of impression: under-dried specimens cause uneven ink transfer; over-dried specimens become brittle and fracture under pressure. Neither is acceptable.

assets/process-drying.jpeg
03

Type Setting

The edition name, Latin binomial, and edition number are handset in lead type from cases acquired from a now-closed Edinburgh printing house in 2018. Spacing is adjusted by hand using brass furniture. The forme is locked into the chase. No two editions share a type arrangement.

assets/process-typesetting.jpeg
04

The Impression

Each of the forty sheets is pulled individually on a Adana Eight-Five platen press. The impression depth is calibrated to the stock weight: 220gsm uncoated cotton. Each pull is evaluated before the next. Any print with uneven impression, ink bleed, or registration error is destroyed. This accounts for an average of three sheets per edition.

assets/process-impression.jpeg

The press is a 1974 Adana.
Still running.

Acquired from a retired jobbing printer in Inverness, the press has required one replacement roller and one new spring in eleven years of weekly use. It does not need to be anything else.
