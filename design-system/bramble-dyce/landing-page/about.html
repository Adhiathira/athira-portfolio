A botanical press
in a stone mill,
Orkney

Bramble & Dyce was established in 2019 to produce a quarterly record of the indigenous plant life of Mainland, Orkney, in the form of hand-pulled letterpress prints.

assets/about-mill-exterior.jpeg

From a converted grain mill near the Ring of Brodgar

The press occupies what was once the grain-sorting room of a 19th-century mill at Stenness. The conversion took two years and preserved the original stone floor, wooden beam ceiling, and north-facing window that provides the flat, even light preferred for inspecting fresh impressions.

The decision to restrict subjects exclusively to plants found within a defined radius of the mill was made during the first season and has never been reconsidered. Orkney has more than 400 native and naturalised plant species. Forty-two of them have appeared as editions to date. At the current rate, the project has a century of material ahead of it.

No two editions are identical. Seasonal variation, specimen condition, press calibration, and the behaviour of the ink on any given day all contribute to differences between prints within the same edition. Subscribers who collect across multiple seasons sometimes find themselves tracking the same plant through its variations.

Bramble & Dyce does not have a social media presence. It does not accept press enquiries. The waiting list for new annual subscriptions opens once per season, for approximately forty-eight hours, and closes when capacity is reached.

Stenness Mill,
Mainland, Orkney

The mill sits approximately 800 metres from the Standing Stones of Stenness, on a track that is not maintained by the council. Visiting is not possible. The work is dispatched, not displayed.

Specimens used in each edition are recorded in a handwritten field journal maintained since 2019. The journal is held at the mill and is not available for review.
