The Process
Chapter II

From composition to impression

Every edition begins with a conversation about the text. Not the typography, not the paper, not the binding — the text. What does it ask of the reader? What duration of attention? What weight of encounter? These questions determine everything that follows: the typeface, the leading, the paper, the depth of the impression, the format of the finished object.

The process below describes a typical edition. Every project deviates from it. That is the point of working by hand.

Method
Steps

Six stages of making

I

Composition

The text is studied, the typeface selected. For hand-set work, type is composed letter by letter from the case — Caslon, Bembo, Baskerville, Joanna. For photopolymer editions, the compositor draws the letterforms digitally and has plates made from the original artwork.

II

Paper Selection

We source mould-made and handmade papers from Zerkall (Germany), Somerset (England), Magnani Pescia (Italy), and Khadi (India). Each paper responds differently to impression depth, ink absorption, and atmospheric humidity. Paper is dampened the morning of the press run.

III

Ink Preparation

Van Son rubber-base inks are mixed by hand to achieve the exact density and temperature the text requires. A cold black for poetry. A warmer black with raw umber undertone for prose. Transparent base added in increments of 5% until the ink sits on the surface correctly.

IV

Make-Ready

The form is locked into the press bed. Packing sheets are built up behind the tympan to achieve even impression across the entire form — thicker where the type is lighter, thinner where it is heavy. This calibration is the most time-consuming step and determines the final quality of every sheet.

V

Impression

The cylinder rolls across the inked form, pressing the dampened sheet into the type. Each pass is a single impression. Each sheet is inspected, hung to dry on lines strung across the studio, then pressed flat under weight for 48 hours. An edition of 80 takes approximately three days of continuous printing.

VI

Binding & Finishing

Sheets are collated, folded, and sewn. We work with bookbinders across Scotland for case binding, Coptic stitch, Japanese stab, and accordion-fold formats. Clamshell boxes, slipcases, and wrappers are fabricated to order. Every copy is numbered and signed.

Appendix

Substrates & Instruments

Papers

  • Zerkall mould-made, 115–285gsm
  • Somerset Book, 115gsm
  • Magnani Pescia, 210gsm
  • Fabriano Rosaspina, 285gsm
  • Khadi handmade, variable weight
  • Hahnemühle Bugra, 130gsm

Typefaces

  • Caslon Old Face, 10–24pt
  • Monotype Bembo, 12–18pt
  • Baskerville, 14pt
  • Joanna, 11–14pt
  • Gill Sans, 8–12pt (titling)
  • Custom photopolymer plates

Presses

  • Vandercook Universal III (1958)
  • Vandercook SP-15 (1967)
  • Vandercook 219AB (1963)

Inks

  • Van Son rubber-base black
  • Gamblin letterpress inks
  • Charbonnel etching ink (intaglio work)
  • Custom-mixed transparent bases
