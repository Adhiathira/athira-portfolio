History

Provenance

Veridian Instruments was founded in 1931 by Erich Wollner, a former employee of the Dresden scientific instrument industry. The building, the methods, and the capsule stack design have not changed.

1931 — Founding

The First Workshop

Erich Wollner established the workshop at Nikolaistraße 14 in January 1931, following twelve years as a precision mechanic at a larger manufacturer. He had one lathe, a letterpress inherited from a defunct print shop, and a clear view of what he wanted to make: instruments that told the truth at every scale graduation, not just at the certified points.

The first instrument — an aneroid barometer, serial number 001 — was delivered in April 1931 to a private meteorological observer in the Erzgebirge. It is still in use. The observer's grandson sends us the chart records each January.

1945–1960 — Continuity

The Decision Not to Leave

The workshop was damaged in February 1945. Erich Wollner's son, Klaus, rebuilt it in the same building. He declined offers to relocate to Munich or Hamburg. Dresden was where the instrument-making tradition lived, he reasoned; and where the knowledge was, there the work should stay.

Under the East German state, the workshop operated as a cooperative. Output was directed to meteorological stations and research institutes. Quality standards did not change. The state, for its purposes, required accuracy. We had always required it for our own.

1990 — Reunification

The Return to Private Production

Klaus Wollner's daughter, Astrid, registered the firm as a GmbH in October 1990. She had spent the preceding decade as the cooperative's technical director. The transition changed the paperwork. The work itself did not change.

Astrid introduced one modification: the calibration certificate, previously a single hand-written entry in a ledger, became a printed document accompanying each instrument. The ledger is still maintained alongside it. Both records are kept in perpetuity.

Today

The Third Generation

Astrid Wollner retired in 2019. The workshop is now led by Tobias Wollner and master instrument-maker Sabine Kreuz. The capsule stack geometry that Erich Wollner developed in 1931 — derived from his own calculations, never patented — remains the basis of every barometer we build.

We produce fewer than 200 instruments per year. We expect to continue at this rate. We are not planning to grow.

Ninety years without a modification to the capsule stack. Not because we could not improve it — because we have not found a reason to.
— Tobias Wollner, 2023
