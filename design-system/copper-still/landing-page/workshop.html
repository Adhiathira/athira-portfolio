Inside the Workshop

Each Veridian instrument moves through nine distinct stages. The building on Nikolaistraße has housed every one of them since 1931.

  1. Casting and Turning

    Cases and dials begin as brass or ebonite stock. Outer profiles are turned on a manual lathe. We do not use CNC. The maker adjusts for each piece.

  2. Dial Printing

    Scale divisions and numerals are letterpress printed onto a silvered dial blank. The press dates from 1948. Registration is checked under a 10× loupe before each run.

  3. Capsule Assembly

    The measuring element — whether a capsule stack, hair bundle, or bimetal strip — is assembled under controlled temperature. Each element is individually characterised before installation.

  4. Linkage and Leverage

    The mechanical linkage between the sensing element and the pointer is fitted and adjusted. Friction, backlash, and hysteresis are each measured and minimised at this stage.

  5. Calibration

    Each instrument is calibrated against reference standards traceable to PTB (Physikalisch-Technische Bundesanstalt). Five test points. Values recorded by hand on the calibration certificate.

  6. Finishing

    Cases are polished, lacquered, or left in a natural oxidising finish depending on specification. Glass is hand-fitted. Bezels are closed and sealed. No adhesives.

  7. Signing

    The instrument-maker engraves their initials and the production number on the back of the case. This is not a ceremony. It is accountability: the number links the instrument to the calibration record permanently.

  8. Packing

    Each instrument is fitted into a walnut box with a cotton-covered buffer. The calibration certificate, instruction sheet, and spare chart paper (where applicable) are placed alongside. The box is sealed with a wax disc.

  9. Dispatch

    Instruments are dispatched by specialist carrier. Tracked, insured, temperature-monitored. Lead time from order to dispatch: 8–14 weeks. We do not hold finished stock.

The Makers

Six Instrument-Makers and Two Apprentices

We are a small workshop. Every member of the team can build a complete instrument from blank stock. There is no assembly line. There is a queue — and there is time.

Apprenticeships are three years. Applications are considered once a year. We are not currently recruiting.
