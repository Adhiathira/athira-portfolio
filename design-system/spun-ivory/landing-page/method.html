How we work

Method

The approach

Measurement before
description

Before any description is written, the material is measured. Not estimated, not observed — measured. Weight in grams per square metre. Thread count in warp and weft. Shrinkage percentage between stages. Surface roughness by tactile index. These are the facts the record begins with.

Description comes after, and is always marked as such — a separate layer, attributed to the specialist who provided it, with a date. The distinction between measurement and interpretation is a design constraint, not a preference.

The principles

Six constraints that govern every edition

  • 01

    Direct contact only

    All sourcing decisions require physical presence. No material enters the catalogue without being handled by the maker first. This constraint rules out remote sampling and limits the pace of edition production — by design.

  • 02

    Measurement before language

    Quantitative data precedes any qualitative description. The measurement record is permanent and uneditable once set. The descriptive record can be revised, and each revision is dated and attributed.

  • 03

    Specimen integrity

    Physical specimens are mounted in archival conditions — acid-free board, light-blocked sleeves, humidity-buffered enclosures. Every edition is built to survive 200 years of passive storage without conservation intervention.

  • 04

    Closed editions

    Once an edition is exhausted, it is not reprinted. This is not scarcity positioning — it is archival discipline. Each edition is a closed record. A second run would be a different record.

  • 05

    Dual-layer annotation

    Every specimen carries two annotation layers: a typeset measurement index and a handwritten notation in the Bureau notation language. Neither replaces the other. The hand carries information the type cannot.

  • 06

    Time as a variable

    For materials that change with age — leather, certain papers, natural dye textiles — a follow-up specimen is included and dated for re-measurement at 5 years. The record is designed to receive future data, not just store past data.

The protocol

40 measurement points,
four material families

Paper
  • Grammage (g/m²)
  • Surface pH
  • Opacity (%)
  • Formation index
  • Fold endurance
  • Surface roughness (Bekk)
  • Fibre direction
  • Colourfastness index
Textile
  • Thread count (warp/weft)
  • Weave structure
  • Pile height
  • Drape coefficient
  • Wet tensile strength
  • Shrinkage (wash/dry)
  • Dye origin
  • Light fastness
Clay · Leather
  • Firing temperature (°C)
  • Shrinkage rate (%)
  • Water absorption (%)
  • Temper (leather)
  • Grain depth
  • Tanning agent
  • Patina rate (5-year)
  • Surface tooth (tactile)
