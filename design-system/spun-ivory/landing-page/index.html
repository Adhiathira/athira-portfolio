The Studio

Precision instruments for the
material world

We design and produce reference systems for craftspeople who work with materials that resist digital description — uncoated papers, raw textiles, stoneware bodies, vegetable-tanned leathers. The systems combine physical sampling, structured annotation, and an archival format built to outlast the tools that created it.

Founded in 2019 by Margot Fleury and Sébastien Ardent, Ivoire Bureau works directly with paper mills, weaving houses, ceramic studios, and tanneries across France, Japan, and Portugal. Each system is made in a limited edition — a finite set, numbered, dated, and bound in the material it documents.

The Systems

Four dimensions,
one reference

Each Ivoire Bureau system focuses on a single material family. Within it: physical specimens, a measurement protocol, a notation guide, and an archival sleeve rated for 200 years.

01

The Paper System

Uncoated, handmade, and machine-made papers from 18 mills. Grammage, surface texture, opacity, and fold-resistance documented against 40 measurement points. Housed in a linen-wrapped portfolio, Edition 60.

02

The Textile System

Woven and non-woven textiles from Japan and Portugal. Thread count, warp/weft ratio, surface pile, and drape characteristics. Specimens mounted on acid-free board with annotation tabs.

03

The Clay System

Ceramic bodies from stoneware to porcelain, fired at documented temperatures. Colour shift on firing, surface tooth at three stages, shrinkage rate, and water absorption measured and indexed.

04

The Leather System

Vegetable-tanned and chrome-free leathers from four tanneries. Temper, pull, grain structure, and patina behaviour over time. The only system with a 5-year follow-up specimen included in the edition.

How it's made

A method built for
materials that age

→ assets/process-ambient.mp4
  1. 01

    Source selection

    We visit each mill or studio to select material candidates directly. Sourcing is never done remotely — the hand must be involved before the record begins.

  2. 02

    Measurement protocol

    Each material family has its own 40-point measurement protocol, built with domain specialists — papermakers, weavers, ceramicists, tanners. Measurements are taken on fresh and aged specimens.

  3. 03

    Annotation and indexing

    Every specimen is annotated by hand using the Bureau notation guide, then typeset into the archival index. The handwritten and printed records coexist — neither replaces the other.

  4. 04

    Edition binding

    The completed system is bound in a numbered edition. Each copy is identical in content; the housing material — linen, calfskin, or plain board — varies by edition series.
