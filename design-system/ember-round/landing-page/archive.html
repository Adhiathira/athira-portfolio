Rare & historical cultivars

The seed archive

Limited-quantity releases of rare, historically significant, and nearly-lost cultivars. Each lot is described in full — origin, germination record, estimated stock.

Current archive lots

Available now

All archive lots are available in quantities of 40–100 seeds. Once sold, they are not restocked unless the source farm produces a sufficient harvest in a subsequent season.

Featured lot

1887

Mortgage Lifter Tomato

Bred by M.C. Byles of Logan, West Virginia, by crossing four large-fruited tomato varieties over six years. Named after the practice of paying off a mortgage from tomato sales. This strain descended directly from Byles' original 1940s distribution, maintained by a single family in Appalachia. 52 packets remaining.

Pre-20th century

1860

Lazy Wife Bean

Pennsylvania Dutch string bean documented before 1810. Named not for the gardener but for the pod's stringless character. Vigorous climber, white seeds with pale streaks. 28 packets remaining.

Near-extinct

1940

Costoluto Genovese

Ribbed flat Ligurian tomato. Once the dominant market tomato of Genoa. Acidic, complex, suited to cooking. Largely displaced by commercial varieties in the 1980s. 40 packets remaining.

Documented provenance

1912

Reine des Violettes Radish

French market radish with an unusual purple-violet interior and ivory exterior. Recorded in the 1912 Vilmorin seed catalogue and now extremely rare outside of specialty French seed libraries. Mild flavour, excellent in thin slices with cultured butter. 60 packets remaining.

Single source

1936

Tarbais Bean

The authentic cassoulet bean from the Hautes-Pyrénées. IGP-protected in France but this seed line pre-dates the commercial IGP selection. Sourced from one farm in the Bigorre region that has maintained the original strain through open pollination without commercial selection pressure. 35 packets remaining.

Regional rarity

1950

Pimiento Morrón de Lodosa

Navarre sweet pepper. The variety used for Spain's conserva industry in the post-war period, now displaced by Piquillo. Sweet, thick-walled, excellent roasted. 48 packets remaining.

Collector's lot

1903

Tom Thumb Lettuce

Victorian dwarf butterhead, documented in the 1903 Suttons catalogue. Forms a perfect compact head in 45 days. Suitable for windowsill cultivation. 90 packets remaining.

A note on archive seeds

These seeds are living documents.

Archive seeds require attention. Some have lower germination rates than current-season varieties — this is expected and documented on each packet. We recommend ordering archive seeds only if you intend to grow them out and ideally save seed yourself. Growing these varieties keeps them alive. That is the point.
