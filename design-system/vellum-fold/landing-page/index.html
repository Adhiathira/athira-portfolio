Chapter I

The Studio

Deckle & Thread began in a converted printworks on the edge of the old quarter, where the air still carries the mineral scent of wet pulp and linseed. We make paper the way it was made before machines intervened — by hand, one sheet at a time, from raw cotton linter beaten into fiber.

Our bindery sits alongside the vat room. Here, signatures are folded, awls pierce station marks, and needles draw linen thread through kettlestitched spines. Every volume that leaves this studio carries the texture of human attention — the slight deckle at the page edge, the faint tooth of handmade laid lines.

Chapter II

The Craft

From raw cotton linter to finished codex, each piece passes through four deliberate stages. Nothing is rushed. The paper sets its own pace.

01

Beating the Fiber

Raw cotton linter is soaked overnight, then beaten in a Hollander beater until the fibers separate into a cloudy slurry. The length of beating determines everything: a short beat yields a coarse, textured sheet; a long beat produces translucent vellum.

02

Forming the Sheet

The mould and deckle are dipped into the vat. With a practiced shake, pulp distributes evenly across the screen, water drains, and a wet sheet forms — its edges naturally irregular, the signature of handmade paper.

03

Pressing & Drying

Wet sheets are couched onto woolen felts, stacked, and pressed under weight to expel water and bond fibers. They then hang in the drying loft, where slow air circulation firms the sheet without warping.

04

Binding the Volume

Dried sheets are folded into signatures, pierced at sewing stations, and stitched onto linen tapes with a kettlestitch binding. Boards are laced on, the spine is rounded, and the cover — cloth, leather, or decorated paper — completes the codex.
