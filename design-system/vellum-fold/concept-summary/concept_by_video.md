# Appleyard Flowers — Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page begins as a book begins — with the cover lifting. The initial load presents a brief moment of weighted stillness: a cream-toned surface, the texture of heavy uncoated paper barely visible, occupying the full viewport. Then the cover lifts. The hero photograph is revealed not through a fade but through a vertical wipe that moves upward like a front board being opened, the three-tier navigation settling into place at the top as though it were the head of the spine — fixed, structural, permanent. The entire above-fold composition resolves in this single unhurried gesture: the lifestyle photograph, the uppercase headline with its yellow accent, the supporting copy, and the two CTA buttons arriving together like a title page that has always been there, waiting to be uncovered.

## Hero Experience

The hero is a frontispiece — a single full-bleed image that establishes the visual and emotional register of the entire volume before a word of body text is read. The lifestyle photograph holds still. It does not parallax, zoom, or drift. But the stillness is not emptiness; it is the composed stillness of a plate illustration tipped into a book, fixed to the page with intention. The centered text block overlaid on the photograph carries the quiet authority of a half-title: uppercase, tracked, unhurried. The social proof band below — dark, horizontal, typographic — functions as the colophon line at the bottom of a title page, establishing provenance and trust before the reader turns to the first chapter. This hero is a strong candidate for ambient video background: the stillness of flowers breathing almost imperceptibly would give the frontispiece the quality of a living illustration.

## Section Transitions

Pages turn. Each section boundary in the scroll is a page flip — not a dramatic origami fold but the quiet, gravity-aware motion of a single leaf of paper curling away from its neighbor. The white-to-light-gray color shifts that define Appleyard's section rhythm become the visual equivalent of recto meeting verso: white sections are recto pages (the right-hand side, where the eye lands first), gray sections are the verso (left-hand, supporting, quieter). The transition between them is a soft horizontal wipe with a faint paper-curl shadow at the leading edge — a 0.4s ease-out movement that carries just enough dimensional suggestion to register as a physical page turning without becoming theatrical. No section slides laterally or fades through opacity. Every transition moves vertically, as pages do.

## Content Rhythm

Elements enter the viewport the way words appear to a reader scanning a new page — not all at once, but in the order the eye naturally moves. Headings settle first, arriving with a gentle downward ease as though being pressed into the paper by a letterpress platen: a 0.3s translate-Y from 8px above, with a slight deceleration that suggests weight meeting resistance. Product cards in the three-column grid stagger left-to-right with 80ms delays between columns, mimicking the horizontal sweep of reading. Each card's photograph resolves a beat before its text label, the way an illustration catches the eye before its caption. The accordion sections open with a measured vertical expansion — the paper unfolding — rather than a snap or a bounce. The entire content rhythm is languid, unhurried, governed by the tempo of someone reading for pleasure rather than scanning for information.

## Footer Approach

The final section is the back matter. The newsletter form and footer link columns arrive on the same continuous light-gray field that began in the accordion section above — a single unbroken sheet, like the endpapers of a clothbound book, running from the last chapter through to the back board. The newsletter input field appears with a soft horizontal rule animation, as though a pen line were being drawn across the page to mark where the reader should write. The footer links resolve in a single quiet group, no stagger, no choreography — they are the index, the colophon, the printer's mark. The copyright strip at the very bottom is the final line: the book closes. A faint downward settle of 2px on the entire footer block, lasting 0.2s, gives the closing the barely perceptible weight of a back cover coming to rest.

## Motion Principles

All motion in this system obeys the physics of paper. Pages have weight — transitions use ease-out curves with gentle deceleration, never spring or bounce, because paper does not spring. Elements settle downward, not upward, because gravity acts on sheets. Timing is languid: nothing completes in less than 0.2s, most transitions occupy 0.3–0.5s, and the longest gestures (page-turn section transitions) take 0.4–0.6s. There is no overshoot, no elastic snap, no parallax drift. The motion vocabulary is: settle, curl, reveal, rest. Color transitions between white and gray sections are instantaneous at their boundary — a hard cut, like the clean edge where one page ends and the next begins. Interactive hover states on buttons and cards use a 0.2s ease transition that adds a faint shadow or border darkening, as though a finger were pressing lightly on the page and the paper were responding to the pressure.

## Video Placements

**1. Hero Background — Frontispiece floral ambient loop**
- Section role: Hero / above-fold brand statement
- Suggested file: `hero-frontispiece.mp4`
- Subject: An extreme close-up of flower petals and stems filmed through a sheet of translucent vellum paper — the flowers slightly diffused, slightly warm, as though viewed through the tissue interleaf that protects a plate illustration in a fine book. A barely perceptible shift in light moves across the surface over the loop duration, suggesting a page being slowly turned nearby, the reflected light changing. The color temperature is natural, warm, ungraded. The camera is locked.
- Duration: 10s
- Why video: The hero is the highest-impact creative position and currently relies on a static photograph. A near-still ambient video seen through a translucent paper layer would accomplish what no photograph can: it would give the frontispiece the quality of a living illustration, breathing so slowly that the viewer may not consciously register the motion. The vellum diffusion layer ties the video directly to the book metaphor while preserving the editorial register of the existing photography. The overlaid uppercase headline and CTA buttons remain fixed — text printed on the page, not part of the moving image beneath.

**2. Category Page Header — Paper-texture establishing loop**
- Section role: Category page header / photographic right panel
- Suggested file: `category-paper-texture.mp4`
- Subject: A slow overhead shot of a single flower stem being laid onto a sheet of heavy cream-colored cotton paper — the kind used in letterpress printing. The flower is placed gently, and the camera holds as it settles. The paper's texture — its tooth, its faint deckled edge at the bottom of frame — is as much the subject as the flower. Warm directional light from the upper left casts a soft shadow of the stem across the paper surface. No hands visible after the initial placement.
- Duration: 5s
- Why video: The category header's asymmetric split layout — dark text panel left, photographic panel right — creates a natural container for atmospheric video in the right panel only. A video that foregrounds paper as a material surface, with the flower as the object placed upon it, reinforces the book metaphor at the exact moment the visitor transitions from brand experience to transactional browsing. It says: even in the catalogue, there is craft, there is considered surface, there is the weight of real material.

**3. Social Proof Band — Ink-on-paper typographic ambient loop**
- Section role: Trust / social proof band between hero and content body
- Suggested file: `social-proof-ink.mp4`
- Subject: A macro close-up of letterpress type being impressed into soft cotton paper — the metal sort pressing into the sheet, ink transferring, the paper fibers compressing and then relaxing slightly as the pressure releases. The footage is abstracted enough that no specific letters are legible — it is texture, pressure, and material rather than readable text. The color palette is the existing dark band's near-black with warm undertones from the paper.
- Duration: 5s
- Why video: The social proof band is a dense, horizontal, typographic element that currently reads as flat and utilitarian — a necessary trust signal but not a designed moment. A subtle background video of letterpress impression in macro would transform this band into a material statement about the weight and permanence of the trust claims it contains. Review ratings and counts overlaid on footage of type being physically pressed into paper creates a subconscious association between the words and the act of committing them to a permanent surface. The video plays at reduced opacity (40–50%) behind the existing white text to maintain legibility.
