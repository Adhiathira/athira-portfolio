# Appleyard Flowers — Concept by Image

## Brand Premise

A premium UK flower delivery service positioned at the intersection of accessible gifting and editorial floristry. The site operates as a transactional e-commerce platform with a brand register borrowed from luxury retail: uppercase Gothambook headings, strict rectilinear geometry, and a white-dominant palette that places photography at the center of every composition. The design does not attempt artisanal warmth or editorial narrative — it delivers floral product through a clean, efficient shopping interface with a premium surface treatment. The priority is conversion and scannability, achieved through a confident, disciplined aesthetic rather than elaborate storytelling.

## Color Architecture

White (#ffffff) is the unbroken dominant surface. Near-dark charcoal (#363636 body text, #464646 headings) provides typography on white throughout. The product experience is almost entirely achromatic — white, light gray, and dark gray — which removes visual interference from the product photography and allows floral color to read without competition. A saturated yellow accent appears in the hero section headline, functioning as a promotional burst against the photography background — the only chromatic interruption in the system. The category page header introduces a dark gray-blue solid field for text safety on image-heavy pages, providing a structural contrast panel without introducing a new brand color. The overall palette is deliberately neutral so that the flowers themselves carry all the chromatic weight.

## Typography System

Gothambook dominates the entire type system. All headings — h1 through body text — use this condensed geometric humanist. A distinctive typographic treatment defines the display register: headings are set in uppercase with generous letter-spacing (h1: 17px with 4px letter-spacing; h2: 20px with 1px letter-spacing). This combination of uppercase, tight tracking, and small-for-headline sizing creates a retail fashion aesthetic — confident without being large. Body copy and paragraphs sit at 12px Gothambook, a compact scale that prioritizes information density. On category pages, a script-inflected or elegant serif typeface replaces Gothambook for hero headlines — a deliberate typographic shift that signals "product editorial zone" versus "promotional homepage," adding a touch of florist artisanship to the category experience.

## Spatial System

The layout is a strict vertical stack of rectangular modules with no geometric deviation. The hero is a full-width, full-bleed lifestyle photograph with a centered text block overlaid — headline, supporting copy, and two CTA buttons arranged in a centered column. A thick full-width social proof band (review ratings, count) separates the hero from the content body below it. The main content follows a clear alternating pattern: a light gray utility section (search/filter), then white card grids, then gray accordion, then gray footer. The card grids operate on a consistent three-column structure with even gutters — bordered category cards for navigation, borderless minimalist product cards for transactional browsing. The category page header introduces an asymmetric split: a solid dark gray-blue left panel containing text, a large photographic panel occupying the right 60–70%, creating a high-contrast text-safe zone without overlay.

## Photography and Editorial Voice

Photography is the primary content and the design's defining visual investment. The hero lifestyle photograph is art-directed with a natural text-safe zone in the center — subjects arranged to frame a visual clearing where the overlaid headline sits without competing with the image composition. Category photography is rich and thematic, used both as hero backgrounds and as grid card imagery. Product photography shoots all items on a consistent clean neutral light-gray background, removing all context to focus entirely on the flowers themselves — a clinical e-commerce treatment that aids comparison. The photography register shifts between editorial (hero, category pages) and catalogue (product grid), and the design system accommodates both without visual conflict.

## Navigation and Wayfinding

The navigation is a three-tier sticky assembly that remains fixed throughout the entire scroll experience. The top tier contains the brand wordmark centered, a search input left, and utility icons (account, bag) right. The middle tier carries the primary product category links. The bottom tier presents value proposition messaging. All three bars are on a solid opaque white background from load — no transparency, no scroll-triggered changes. The sticky assembly is persistent and comprehensive, making the full site navigation always available regardless of scroll depth. This prioritizes utility and user orientation over a minimal aesthetic.

## Overall Design Character

Appleyard Flowers operates with the material discipline of a well-bound book — every surface considered, every margin intentional, nothing decorative that does not earn its place on the page. The strict 90-degree shape language (all buttons, cards, inputs, sections carry sharp corners — no rounded forms anywhere in the system) recalls the crisp right angles of a freshly trimmed text block, the kind of uncompromising geometry you find in a fine press edition where the paper knife has done its work cleanly. The uppercase Gothambook type, set small and tracked wide, reads like foil-stamped titling on heavy uncoated stock — restrained, precise, carrying authority through compression rather than scale. The alternating white and light-gray section rhythm functions like the recto-verso pacing of a printed volume: each spread a discrete composition, each turn of the page a soft reset that keeps the eye moving without fatigue. There is a weight to this design that is not visual heaviness but material conviction — the sense that every element has been set into place with the deliberation of metal type locked into a chase. Motion is absent, and the absence is itself a material statement: this is a surface that holds still, that trusts the impression it has already made, the way a printed page trusts ink that has dried and will not move again.
