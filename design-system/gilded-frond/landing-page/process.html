Process
How We Work

Every plate is a record of sustained attention.

No digital tools enter the Flora Carta process. Illustration is made by hand from direct observation of pressed specimens and living material. The record-keeping is the art.

From Field to Folio

The method, in eight stages.

A typical edition moves from initial species selection to finished folio in approximately six months. The illustrations are made at the illustrator's studio from pressed or living specimens. No photography is used as a reference base — only direct observation.

01

Species selection and thematic brief

Flora Carta's editorial committee, working with an assigned scientific adviser, selects the twelve species for each edition. A thematic framework is agreed before any illustration begins. The theme is not a caption — it is a question the illustrations must answer.

02

Specimen collection and herbarium consultation

Each illustrator collects or receives living material and consults herbarium sheets from partner institutions — Chelsea Physic Garden, Kew, Edinburgh Royal Botanic Garden, and private collections. Reference specimens are never dried for this purpose; they are drawn from existing herbaria.

03

Preliminary pencil study

A full-scale pencil study establishes proportion, compositional structure, and botanical accuracy. Scientific advisers review this stage before any paint is applied. Corrections at this stage are expected; corrections after this stage indicate a problem.

04

Medium selection

The governing technique for each edition — gouache, transparent watercolour, copperplate engraving, or lithographic ink — is selected for its period accuracy and for its capacity to render the specific optical character of the subject. Copperplate is not used because it is traditional; it is used when no other technique can convey the subject's structure at the required level of detail.

05

Illustration, first sitting

The first session establishes structural forms and base tones. For gouache and watercolour, this is a wash of the dominant colour family. For copperplate, this is the complete line drawing before any tonal work.

06

Botanical accuracy review

At approximately fifty per cent completion, a formal accuracy review is conducted with the edition's scientific adviser. Morphological errors at this stage can require significant rework — the original specimen must be re-examined.

07

Completion and final documentation

The finished plate is photographed in controlled flat lighting for the archive record before it leaves the illustrator's studio. The photograph becomes the digital archive entry. The plate itself travels to the printing studio.

08

Printing, binding, and dispatch

Plates are reproduced on Hahnemühle archival paper (300gsm cold-press as standard; Fabriano Artistico for editions requiring different surface characteristics). Folios are bound in cloth covers with gold foil titling and enclosed in a rigid slip case. Dispatch is tracked worldwide.

Materials & Paper

The substrate is part of the argument.

Materials are selected with the same rigor applied to species and technique. Nothing is a default — everything is justified by the subject.

Primary Substrate

Hahnemühle 300gsm Cold-Press

The standard Flora Carta substrate. German-milled cotton rag paper, cold-press surface for watercolour and gouache work. 300 years conservation lifespan. Acid-free, buffered.

Alternative Substrate

Fabriano Artistico Hot-Press

Used when subject transparency is critical — seaweeds, lichens, glass-wing insects. The smoother surface allows finer wash gradations and more faithful colour in the printing process.

Copperplate Medium

18-gauge copper, acid-etched

Plates are etched in nitric acid and inked by hand for each pull. Each folio of twelve copperplate illustrations requires approximately 180 individual plate-inking operations.

Binding Material

Japanese Cloth & Gold Foil

Folio covers are wrapped in Japanese book cloth (silk-grain finish) with edition title and Roman numeral in gold foil blocking. Slip cases are 3mm grey board covered in matching cloth.

Pigments

Artist-grade, lightfast-rated

All pigments used in Flora Carta illustrations are artist-grade, with a minimum lightfastness rating of II (ISO 105-B02). Pigments with fugitive characteristics are avoided even when optically superior.

Archive Format

TIFF, 600dpi, AdobeRGB

Every completed plate is documented in TIFF format at 600dpi in AdobeRGB colour space before leaving the illustrator's studio. These originals form the digital archive available to subscribers.

The Living Archive

Forty hours of looking, preserved on a single sheet of paper.

The botanical illustration folio is a form of knowledge storage. Every plate documents not only the plant but the observation — the hours spent looking, the decisions made about what to represent and what to omit. That record is what a photograph cannot provide.
