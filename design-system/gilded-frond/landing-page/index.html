The Archive

Where botanical science meets the painted page.

Flora Carta began as a private response to the loss of the illustrated botanical monograph — a form of publication that flourished from 1750 to 1950 and nearly vanished with it. Working from herbarium sources at the Chelsea Physic Garden and private collections across Britain, our artists produce twelve illustrations per seasonal folio, each rendered in the governing style of the source period: gouache, watercolour, copperplate, or lithographic ink.

Since 2019, we have produced twenty-two folios. 1,400 subscribers in 47 countries receive each edition. No digital distribution. No reproduction prints. Every folio is a physical object — the kind that rewards sustained looking.

Edition XXII Winter 2024–2025

Roots & Dormancy: Underground Structures of the British Hedgerow

Twenty-two illustrators from our associate roster spent autumn 2024 excavating root systems along three miles of Kentish hedgerow. The resulting twelve plates document rhizomes, taproots, mycorrhizal networks, and bulb-forming species in cross-section — forms rarely painted because they require the destruction of their subject. Printed in warm sepia tones on 300gsm cold-press.

The Craft

Each plate takes between forty and ninety hours to complete.

Our illustrators work from pressed specimens, herbarium sheets, and field sketches. No digital tools enter the process. Pigment is mixed by hand. Every stroke is an act of sustained attention — the kind that produces knowledge, not just likeness.
