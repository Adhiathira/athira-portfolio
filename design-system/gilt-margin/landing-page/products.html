The Collection

Everything from the hive.

Four varietal honeys in limited quantities, raw beeswax in three forms, propolis tincture, and whole comb sections by commission. All harvested once per year.

Varietal Honeys

Each honey is bottled by hive location, not blended. The character of the land — altitude, sun exposure, forage mix — is in every jar.

→ assets/product-corbezzolo.jpeg
Corbezzolo Very limited

Wildflower, Nuoro Hills

Dark amber. The corbezzolo blossom has a faint bitterness that persists into the honey — distinctive, divisive, and produced in the smallest volume of any varietal we harvest.

Harvest: Late October Altitude: 620m Crystallisation: Slow
→ assets/product-thyme.jpeg
Thyme Available

Macchia alta, above 600m

Golden. Intensely aromatic — the dry macchia scrub at altitude produces a honey that crystallises within weeks of bottling and keeps for years without degrading.

Harvest: July–August Altitude: 680m Crystallisation: Fast (2–3 weeks)
→ assets/product-cistus.jpeg
Cistus Available

Lowland scrub, valley floor

Pale amber to off-white. The cistus flower is strongly perfumed and the honey carries that quality — a more delicate entry than the corbezzolo, longer on the finish.

Harvest: May–June Altitude: 340m Crystallisation: Medium
→ assets/product-multifloral.jpeg
Multifloral Available

Mixed macchia, several sites

Mid-amber. The hives that produce this run across several forage zones — the flavour is more complex than any single varietal, and it changes year to year in ways that are difficult to predict.

Harvest: August Altitude: 400–500m Crystallisation: Variable

From the same hives

Raw beeswax, three ways.

The wax cappings removed during honey extraction are melted once and poured into moulds. No bleaching, no chemical treatment. The pale yellow colour is the wax's own; the faint honey scent is residual from the comb.

Tincture

Propolis

Dark resinous propolis dissolved in food-grade alcohol. Used historically as a preservative and antimicrobial. 30ml dropper bottles, raw and unstandardised.
