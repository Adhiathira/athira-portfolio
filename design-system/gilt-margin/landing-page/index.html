What we believe

Honey doesn't need help.

Industrial production filters out everything that makes wildflower honey distinct — the pollen, the wax particles, the particular bitterness of corbezzolo blossom. We leave it in. The texture is coarser. The color varies year to year. That's the point.

Unfiltered

Cold-extracted directly from the comb. No heat treatment above 38°C — the temperature of the hive itself.

Unblended

Each harvest is bottled by hive location. The taste of the east-facing slope differs from the valley floor. We bottle them separately.

Unhurried

We produce one annual harvest per hive. When a varietal runs out, it is gone until the following spring.

How it's made

  1. 01

    Spring survey

    In March we walk each hive site and assess forage — which plants are flowering, in what density. This determines which hives will produce which varietal that year.

  2. 02

    Single harvest

    One extraction per hive per year, in late summer. We leave enough honey in the hive for the colony to winter on — we take only the surplus.

  3. 03

    Cold extraction

    Frames are uncapped by hand and spun in a radial extractor at ambient temperature. No warming, no pressing — the honey falls by centrifugal force.

  4. 04

    Settle and jar

    The extracted honey rests in tanks for 72 hours — long enough for wax particles to rise but not long enough for micro-filtration to become necessary. Then straight to glass.
