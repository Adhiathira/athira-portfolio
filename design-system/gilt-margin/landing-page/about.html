How it started

We inherited six hives from a neighbour who was leaving. We didn't know anything about beekeeping — we learned as we went, badly at first, then better. Thirty years later we have forty-eight hives spread across three sites in the macchia above Nuoro.

The decision not to filter or blend came early. Our first harvest tasted different from anything we'd bought in a shop. The difference was what the industrial process removes. We decided to leave it in.

We are a small operation and intend to stay that way. We don't ship at scale, we don't take wholesale orders, and we don't blend varietals to fill volume shortfalls. When a varietal runs out, it is gone until the following year.

Where the hives are

→ assets/about-site-east.jpeg
East slope — 620m

Corbezzolo site

The east-facing slope above the village. Corbezzolo dominates; the blossom is short-lived and the yield is small. This is where we produce the most distinctive and limited honey.

→ assets/about-site-ridge.jpeg
Ridge — 680m

Thyme site

The highest site, exposed to the prevailing wind off the sea. The thyme scrub here is dense and ancient — the honey it produces crystallises fast and holds its flavour for years.

→ assets/about-site-valley.jpeg
Valley — 340m

Cistus & multifloral site

The lowest site, in the valley floor where the forage is most varied. The cistus honey is delicate and quickly exhausted; the multifloral from the surrounding scrub is the largest-volume harvest.

What we don't do.

—

We don't heat the honey above 38°C. That's the temperature inside the hive. Above it, enzymes break down.

—

We don't micro-filter. The pollen in unfiltered honey is why you can taste the difference between a corbezzolo and a cistus.

—

We don't blend varietals to fill gaps. A sold-out honey is sold out. We don't substitute.

—

We don't take more than the colony can spare. The hive winters on its own stores.
