# Brand Name — Layout Brief

## Overall Page Structure

The page flows through five distinct zones: a full-viewport hero, an editorial text section, a split-image feature section, a CTA pair section, a second full-bleed image section, an asymmetric news grid, and a full-bleed newsletter footer. The rhythm alternates between dark/immersive full-bleed moments and light/airy white-background editorial sections — a classic luxury pacing technique that makes each section feel like a breath in or out.

---

## Header / Navigation

**Dual-layer fixed navigation** — a signature pattern. Two navigation bars overlap:

- **Top bar (minimal):** Logo mark flush left in a refined serif, six nav items spread across the right two-thirds with generous tracking. Sits on a thin horizontal rule.
- **Floating overlay nav:** On the hero, the same nav items appear again centered at roughly 1/4 from the top of the viewport, overlaid directly on the imagery in large display type. This creates a cinematic double-exposure moment — navigation becomes part of the visual composition, not separate from it.

**Vertical sidebars:** Two rotated-text labels run vertically on the extreme left edge and extreme right edge. These are persistent across the full page scroll — sticky vertical context strips that also serve as micro-CTAs. They define the outermost boundary of the content column and create strong vertical rails that frame every section.

---

## Hero Section

**Full-viewport, full-bleed immersive.** The hero occupies 100% of the viewport — no margins, no padding visible. A hero image bleeds to all four edges.

- The display wordmark appears at roughly center-left to center, in enormous dark serif type (approximately 1/3 of viewport height), overlaid directly on the image with no box or background. Pure typographic overlay.
- Below the wordmark, the nav items appear as a secondary centered type element — navigation embedded into the hero composition.
- The left and right vertical text strips appear in this section at the extreme margins, further reinforcing the frame.

The spatial hierarchy is: image as atmosphere → wordmark as identity → nav as utility. All three layers occupy the same physical space, creating depth through typographic scale alone.

## Content Sections Below Hero

### Section 2 — Editorial Text Block
**Full-width white background, single column of large display text.** A wide paragraph of body-to-display scale text runs across roughly 60–70% of the viewport width, left-aligned with significant left margin. Sparse, high air — this section is almost entirely negative space with one large type element. The text acts as a decompression chamber after the intense hero.

### Section 3 — Two-Column Image Feature
**Asymmetric two-column layout on white.** Two images sit side by side but are **not equal width** — each occupies roughly 30–35% of the content area, with a significant white gap between them. The images are tall (roughly 3:4 ratio), letting the white background breathe around and between them. No captions visible. The asymmetry in vertical alignment between the two images adds casual editorial tension — one reads slightly higher than the other in the viewport during scroll.

### Section 4 — CTA Pair
**Two pill-shaped CTA buttons, one per side of a white field.** Primary CTA appears left-center and Secondary CTA appears right-center. Both use the same pill/rounded-rectangle outline style. This section is minimal — almost entirely white negative space framing two action buttons. The breathing room signals premium restraint.

### Section 5 — Full-Bleed Secondary Image
**Another 100vw image break** — full-bleed, no margins, landscape orientation. This functions as a palette cleanser and visual anchor between the CTA section and the content grid below.

### Section 6 — Content Grid (Asymmetric, Offset Grid)
**Multi-column irregular grid with deliberate positional offsets.** This is the most complex layout section. Content items (date + title pairs) are distributed across approximately three implied columns, but they are **not aligned to a regular grid baseline**. Items in the middle column sit vertically higher than items in the left and right columns. This creates a staggered, editorial magazine feel — like items placed on a drafting table rather than a spreadsheet.

- Left column: 2–3 items, positioned lower in the section
- Center column: 2–3 items, positioned higher — the first appears significantly higher than any other item
- Right column: 2 items + a circular arrow/next CTA at the far right

The irregular vertical rhythm prevents the section from reading as a data table. It reads as editorial curation. Date stamps appear in small, lighter weight above each title in slightly heavier weight — a clear typographic hierarchy pair.

## Footer

**Full-bleed landscape photography + centered newsletter form + utility strip.**

The footer has three layers stacked vertically:

1. **Newsletter module:** Centered on the landscape image — a single text input with an underline style (no box) and below it a pill-shaped Action Button. Both elements are light on dark imagery. The form is narrow relative to the viewport — roughly 40% width, centered.

2. **Footer utility bar:** A narrow accent band at the very bottom edge of the viewport. Contains copyright left, "BACK TO TOP ↑" center, secondary link right, and site credit far right. Extremely compact — this bar is maybe 30–40px tall.

The footer's use of a full landscape photograph contrasts with the close-up imagery above — zooming out to environment and place.

## Notable Layout Patterns

### Persistent Vertical Sidebar Labels
Rotated text elements occupy the far left and far right margins of every viewport state, creating a permanent frame. This pattern is uncommon — it uses the dead margin space as functional wayfinding and brand reinforcement simultaneously.

### Full-Bleed Image Rhythm
The page uses full-bleed images as section dividers rather than decorative elements — they function as visual chapter breaks. Each time a full-bleed image appears, it resets the visual "temperature" of the page.

### Typographic Overlay Navigation
Navigation embedded directly into the hero image at display scale is a high-confidence design move — it only works when the brand identity and photography are strong enough to carry the weight. It collapses the usual separation between wayfinding and identity.

### Negative Space as Premium Signal
Throughout, the white sections use extreme negative space. The CTA section is essentially 90% empty. The editorial text section has one text block in a sea of white. This spatial generosity communicates luxury without stating it — it's the layout equivalent of a quiet room.

### Staggered Editorial Content Grid
The offset vertical positioning in the content grid section is intentional asymmetric rhythm. Items are placed to create diagonal reading paths across the grid rather than linear left-to-right or top-to-bottom scanning — slowing the reader down and inviting browsing rather than scanning.
