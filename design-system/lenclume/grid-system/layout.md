# Brand Name — Layout Brief

## 1. Overall Page Structure

The page follows a vertical editorial scroll of approximately 7–8 distinct sections. The rhythm alternates between full-bleed cinematic moments and contained typographic/content blocks — classic luxury editorial pacing. White space is generous and intentional; content never feels crowded. The overall flow:

1. Hero (full-bleed video/image)
2. Editorial intro (text-heavy)
3. Two-column photo spread
4. Call-to-action pair with full-bleed image below
5. News grid
6. Newsletter signup (full-bleed)
7. Footer (bright accent color, high contrast)

---

## 2. Header / Navigation

The navigation sits **centered horizontally** on top of the hero image, floating over it with no visible background — fully transparent. The brand wordmark is large and dominant, placed above the nav links. Below it, nav items are evenly spaced in a single horizontal row.

Two **vertical sidebar labels** flank the entire page — one label runs bottom-to-top on the far left edge, and another runs top-to-bottom on the far right edge. These persist across the full scroll and act as fixed/sticky anchor labels. They use rotated small-caps text and sit flush against the viewport edges, creating a framing device that brackets the entire reading experience. This is a strong signature pattern for the site.

A **persistent footer bar** sits at the very bottom of the viewport — a thin strip containing brand identity marks (awards, logos, wordmark centered) and a newsletter signup prompt at far right. This is always-visible.

---

## 3. Hero Section

Full-bleed, edge-to-edge photographic image covering 100% of the viewport. A cinematic photograph fills the entire frame; there is no container or bounding box. The navigation floats transparently over the top portion. No overlay gradient is visible — the image itself provides enough contrast for the text above it.

The layout is a single column with centered text — the wordmark and nav stack vertically in the upper-center of the image. There are no split columns or offset boxes in the hero.

---

## 4. Content Sections Below the Hero

### Intro / Editorial Text Block
A full-width typographic section on a light background. A single large paragraph of serif body text is set at a generous scale — roughly equivalent to a display pull-quote. The text is left-aligned and runs the full column width. Spacious top and bottom padding. No images. Pure typographic hierarchy.

### Two-Column Photo Spread
Two photographs side by side at equal width — roughly 50/50 split with a small gutter between them. Both images are portrait-oriented and tall. No captions visible. The layout feels editorial, like a magazine spread. Images bleed to the section edges but not full viewport width — there's a slight margin on either side mirroring the sidebar labels.

### CTA Pair + Full-Bleed Image
Two pill-shaped buttons ("Primary CTA" / "Secondary CTA") sit side by side, centered, with equal weighting. Below this CTA pair, a full-bleed landscape image fills the section edge-to-edge — a dramatic photograph that functions as a visual chapter break before the news section.

### News Grid
A three-column asymmetric grid of news items. Each cell contains a date (small, monospaced feel) and a headline below it. Some cells contain only text (no thumbnail images visible), creating a sparse, editorial rhythm. Vertical spacing between items is generous. A circular arrow button (→) appears at the far right, suggesting a horizontal scroll or pagination affordance — the grid may scroll laterally. The section header sits in the top-left, small and capitalized, acting as a label rather than a headline.

---

## 5. Newsletter Section

A full-bleed section with a moody landscape photograph as background. Content is centered horizontally with:
- A small label in caps at top
- A short descriptive sentence
- An underlined email input field (minimal, no box border)
- A pill-shaped action button below

The layout is a single centered column with loose vertical stacking. The photo bleeds to all edges. The light text on dark image creates high legibility without a separate overlay panel.

---

## 6. Footer Layout

The footer breaks into **two distinct bands**:

**Upper footer band** — bright vivid accent background, high contrast. Divided into roughly four horizontal columns:
- Left: address block + social links
- Center-left: navigation column 1
- Center-right: navigation column 2
- Far right: a lone utility link

At the very top of this section: brand identity marks repeated (awards, logos, wordmark) in a row — a brand identity ribbon.

**Lower band within footer** — a scrolling marquee ticker of related brand or product names runs horizontally in a continuous loop. This creates kinetic energy in an otherwise static layout.

**Bottom strip** — a narrower, slightly different-toned bar with copyright info on the left, "BACK TO TOP ↑" centered, and a site credit with a circular badge on the right.

---

## 7. Notable Layout Patterns

**Rotated sidebar labels** — Vertical text elements on the left and right viewport edges are the most distinctive layout signature. They persist across all sections, creating a fixed frame that reinforces brand identity continuously during scroll. They function simultaneously as navigation affordances and decorative framing devices.

**Persistent mini-footer bar** — A thin strip fixed to the bottom of the viewport throughout the page scroll, housing credibility markers and a newsletter CTA. This ensures brand identity markers are always visible.

**Full-bleed image chapter breaks** — The page uses full-width photographs as visual punctuation between content blocks, rather than relying solely on whitespace. This creates cinematic pacing.

**Typographic scale as hierarchy** — Sections are distinguished more by type size and weight shifts than by background color changes (which stay largely neutral until the accent-color footer). The editorial intro text is disproportionately large compared to body conventions — a deliberate choice that signals restraint and confidence.

**Scrolling ticker in footer** — The infinite marquee adds motion to the footer without JavaScript-heavy animation, reinforcing the sense of a larger brand ecosystem.
