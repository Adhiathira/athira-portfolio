# Brand Name — Layout Brief

## 1. Overall Page Structure

The page is structured as a linear, single-column vertical narrative with approximately 5–6 major sections visible in the screenshot. The flow reads: **header → hero text → full-bleed hero visual → bottom bar**. The visible portion suggests a long-scroll, cinematic storytelling format where each section occupies significant vertical real estate. The overall rhythm is slow and deliberate — generous whitespace, large elements, nothing crowded.

---

## 2. Header / Navigation Layout

The header uses a **three-zone horizontal split** anchored to a light background:

- **Left zone:** Brand mark — large, bold, dark, uppercase. Flush left with generous left margin. Takes roughly 25% of the width.
- **Center zone:** Hero tagline text bleeds vertically into the header space — this is **not a nav item** but the hero headline itself, occupying the center 45% of the width. It's positioned mid-header, creating an intentional overlap or adjacency with the nav.
- **Right zone:** Two pill-shaped CTA buttons — Primary CTA (dark/filled) and Secondary CTA (light/outlined). Both are rounded-rectangle capsules with subtle dot decorations. They're right-aligned with matching right margin to the brand mark's left margin.

A standalone separator icon sits between the center content and the right CTAs, acting as a decorative or interactive separator element. The header feels **unconventionally wide and open** — it is not a tight nav bar but an expansive top zone.

---

## 3. Hero Section

The hero is a **two-layer stacked composition**, not a traditional split-column:

- **Top layer (light background):** The headline text floats on the light background, positioned in the center-left area of the viewport. It's large — approximately 3–4 lines of heavy body/display text. It appears to be cut off at the bottom edge, implying it overlaps with or transitions into the visual below.
- **Bottom layer (full-bleed visual):** A large, near-full-width dark image panel sits below the text zone. It's inset slightly from both edges — roughly 5% margin on each side — giving it a **framed, gallery-like quality** rather than true edge-to-edge bleed. It has noticeably rounded corners (8–12px range), reinforcing the premium, modern aesthetic.

The image itself is a **hero visual** in dark tones against a near-black background. The visual occupies roughly 65% of the viewport height.

The spatial relationship between text and image is **overlapping or tightly abutting** — the text zone transitions directly into the image without a clear horizontal rule or gap. This creates a sense of the image "emerging" beneath the headline.

---

## 4. Bottom Bar (Status / Scroll Indicator)

Anchored to the very bottom of the visible viewport is a **full-width bottom bar** on the light background:

- It contains four `+` plus/cross symbols evenly distributed at the four horizontal extremes (far left, center-left, center-right, far right).
- Between the inner two crosses: a small, spaced uppercase scroll prompt label.
- This functions as a **persistent scroll prompt**, likely fixed or sticky. The `+` symbols may animate or serve as decorative anchors suggesting the page is "pinned" and ready to scroll.

The bar has minimal height — it's purely typographic/iconographic, functioning as a footer-of-the-hero rather than a site footer.

---

## 5. Notable Layout Patterns

**Floating / layered header text:** The hero headline text is positioned in the header zone but is clearly content, not navigation. This blurs the boundary between nav and hero — a deliberate design choice to make the page feel like one continuous canvas.

**Inset full-bleed image with rounded corners:** The hero visual avoids true edge-bleed in favor of a slightly inset, card-like panel. This maintains whitespace breathing room while still dominating the viewport. It signals "crafted detail" over raw impact.

**Capsule/pill UI elements:** The navigation CTAs use rounded-pill shapes with micro-decorations (dots). These contrast with the editorial, serif-adjacent feel of the headline, creating a UI-meets-editorial tension.

**Light-to-dark tonal shift:** The page transitions from a very light upper zone to a near-black image panel — this creates a **dramatic tonal split** that anchors the visual weight in the lower two-thirds. The eye is drawn down.

**Spatial economy:** Despite the large elements, the page uses **restraint** — two nav items, one headline, one image, one scroll indicator. Nothing competes. Hierarchy is achieved through scale and contrast alone, not density.

---

`★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────`
The layout uses what designers call "architectural negative space" — the light header zone isn't empty, it IS the design. The headline text positioned as both content and navigation element collapses the typical header/hero boundary, making the entire above-fold feel like a single unified composition rather than stacked components. This is a technique borrowed from editorial magazine layouts, applied to web.
