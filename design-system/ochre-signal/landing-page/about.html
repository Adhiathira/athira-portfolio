Pellum began as a question: what happens when ink-making is treated as a craft, not a supply chain? Every decision since has been an answer.

The Maker

Mira Solano

Founder & Ink Maker

Mira spent nine years as a conservation technician — working alongside paintings that had survived centuries on the strength of their original materials. She grew frustrated that the inks and pigments available to working artists bore no resemblance to the materials she was restoring.

In 2019 she began milling her own pigments in a converted laundry room, starting with iron ochre collected from a quarry in the Roussillon. The first twelve bottles went to calligraphers she knew personally. Word spread.

Pellum now operates from a small studio in the Hudson Valley. Each batch is numbered. Each source is named. Nothing anonymous leaves the studio.

What We Hold To

Three Commitments

01

Named Origins

Every raw material has a source we can point to on a map — a deposit, a farm, a grower. "Natural" without specificity is a marketing word. We don't use marketing words on bottles.

02

Numbered Batches

No batch is anonymous. Each carries a number and a season. When the batch is gone, it is gone. The next one may be subtly different — that is not a defect, it is a record of the material.

03

Small Volume

50–120 units per batch. We have turned down wholesale arrangements that would require scaling beyond what a single maker can control. Control is the product.

The Space

Hudson Valley,
New York

The studio is a converted barn workshop — 900 square feet, north light, stone floor. Visitors by appointment only, twice a year.

The space was chosen for its humidity, which stabilises the milling process. The stone floor keeps temperatures even. The north-facing windows keep the pigments out of direct light during preparation.

Open studio days happen in early spring and late autumn. Dates are announced to the mailing list only.
