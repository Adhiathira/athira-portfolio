Science & Process

The Physics of Structural Color

Unlike pigments that absorb light, structural color emerges from the interference of light waves reflected across precisely engineered nanoscale film architectures.

How It Works

Interference, Not Dye

01

Thin-Film Deposition

Using physical vapor deposition (PVD) and atomic layer deposition (ALD) techniques, IRID applies ultra-thin dielectric films — layer by layer — with sub-nanometer precision. Each layer is a carefully calibrated optical element, not a colorant. The material stack architecture determines the spectral outcome entirely.

02

Wavelength Interference

When light encounters a thin-film interface, partial reflection occurs at each layer boundary. Reflected wavefronts recombine — constructive interference amplifies specific wavelengths, destructive interference cancels others. The thickness of each film, measured in tens of nanometers, determines which wavelength dominates. This is color from geometry alone.

03

Angle-Dependent Color

As the viewing angle shifts, the effective path length through the film changes, causing the peak reflected wavelength to shift toward shorter wavelengths — the characteristic iridescent "color travel" seen in butterfly wings and opal gemstones. IRID's stack designs tune both the primary color and the angular color travel range, enabling from subtle shimmer to dramatic spectral sweep.

Our Process

Deposition Capabilities

Every IRID coating begins in the deposition chamber. Our five core capabilities define the range of structural color outcomes achievable across any substrate geometry.

  1. 01
    Conformal Thin-Film Coating Uniform deposition across complex 3D geometries, including concave surfaces and microstructured substrates, with ±2nm thickness control.
  2. 02
    Multilayer Stack Engineering Up to 40 alternating dielectric layers per stack, enabling high-reflectance narrow-band, broadband, or gradient spectral profiles.
  3. 03
    Gradient Thickness Patterning Spatially varied film thickness across a single surface produces continuous color gradients without masking or printing processes.
  4. 04
    Substrate-Agnostic Adhesion Adhesion layers optimized for glass, metal, polycarbonate, textile composite, and ceramic substrates without surface pretreatment.
  5. 05
    Protective Overcoat Integration Hard oxide capping layers provide scratch resistance (9H) and UV stability without altering the spectral output of the underlying film stack.
