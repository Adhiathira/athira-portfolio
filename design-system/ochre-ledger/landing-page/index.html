On specification

Specification is where architecture becomes construction.
Most practices treat it like an afterthought.

A specification document is a legal instrument, a material library, a supplier relationship, and a design decision — all in one. Burnham gives it the precision and care it deserves: structured, searchable, version-controlled, and always connected to the drawings that reference it.

What Burnham does

Built for the whole practice — not just the spec writer.

01

Specification library

Build and maintain a living master specification — clause by clause, section by section. Every entry versioned, searchable, and linkable to drawings.

02

Material intelligence

Attach manufacturer data sheets, performance certificates, and sustainability credentials directly to specification clauses. No more hunting through inboxes.

03

Supplier relationships

A structured supplier register built into every project — contact records, product approvals, NBS references, and substitution logs in one place.

04

Version control

Every change is timestamped and attributed. Issue a revision, compare it to the previous version, and understand exactly what changed and why.

05

Drawing cross-reference

Link specification clauses to drawing references. When a drawing changes, see which spec clauses may need updating — before the contractor notices.

06

Issue management

Issue specification sets to contractors, respond to RFIs, log substitution requests, and maintain a complete audit trail from tender to handover.

Practice workflow

From drawing office to building site — one document thread.

Burnham sits between your CAD environment and the contractor's inbox. Start a specification from your practice library, build it up through design development, issue it at tender, and maintain it through construction — all without breaking the thread of intent that started on your drawing board.

See the method

The workflow

From blank sheet to construction issue — four stages.

Stage 01

Build your practice library

Import your existing specification clauses or start from the Burnham template set — organised to NBS sections, RIBA Plan of Work stages, or your own taxonomy. The library is shared across all projects in your practice.

Stage 02

Build a project specification

Open a new project, pull the clauses you need from the library, and customise them for the specific materials, suppliers, and standards that apply. Draw cross-references are pulled from your CAD export or added manually.

Stage 03

Issue and respond

Generate issue packages for tender, construction, and handover stages. Contractors receive structured PDFs with linked data sheets. Substitution requests come back into Burnham as tracked records — not email threads.

Stage 04

Close the loop

At handover, export a final as-built specification including all approved substitutions, updated data sheets, and a complete revision history. The building record your client actually needs.
