# Brand Name Layout Brief

## Overall Page Structure

The page is interrupted at load by two overlapping modal layers — a newsletter signup and a cookie consent banner — so the underlying content is partially obscured. However, enough of the background is visible across the 5 frames to identify the underlying layout as a **full-bleed editorial grid** with a dark-toned, product-heavy structure beneath. The visible structure includes:

- A thin top navigation bar
- A full-viewport hero (blurred/obscured by modal)
- What appears to be a multi-column product grid below
- Multiple content sections visible at the bottom of later frames

---

## Header / Navigation

A **minimal, fixed top bar** running the full width of the viewport. It sits above everything — including the modal overlay. The bar is extremely thin (approximately 40–50px), rendered in near-black, and contains:

- Left: brand wordmark or logo
- Right: utility icons (account, bag, search)
- A secondary sub-nav row beneath the primary bar, slightly lighter in weight

The header does not compete for attention — it recedes into the page, leaving the hero and modal as the dominant visual forces. This is characteristic of premium editorial design: navigation is present but deliberately understated.

---

## Hero Section

The hero is **full-bleed and cinematic** — it occupies the entire viewport width and appears to run close to full height. The blurred background across all 5 frames shows:

- A **large-format editorial photograph** (subject in setting)
- The background content **shifts frame-to-frame**, suggesting an **auto-advancing carousel or scroll-driven sequence** — the blurred imagery cycles through multiple editorial stills (varied background tones: warm, neutral, dark, mixed)
- The hero appears to be a **single-image full-bleed layout** with no visible text overlay — the image is the hero

The background blur is a deliberate design choice: the modal dims and blurs the page content to force focus onto the foreground layer.

---

## Modal Layer 1: Newsletter Signup (Primary Modal)

This is the dominant foreground element across all 5 frames. It is a **split two-column floating card**:

- **Left column:** Full-height editorial photograph (sharp/unblurred), cropped tightly — approximately 40% of the modal width
- **Right column:** Form content on a light neutral background — approximately 60% width — containing:
  - Headline in tight, bold condensed uppercase: "Primary CTA Headline"
  - Short body paragraph in monospace-adjacent regular weight
  - A single-line email input with hairline border
  - Three inline preference checkboxes on one row — equal spacing, no visual hierarchy between them
  - Two stacked consent checkboxes with legal copy
  - A full-width dark CTA button ("Action Button") at the bottom
  - An "×" close button positioned at the top-right corner of the modal

The modal floats centered (slightly right of center visually due to the image weight on the left) against a **darkened, blurred full-page backdrop**. It is not anchored to any grid — it reads as a self-contained unit hovering in space.

**Spatial rhythm of the right column:** generous internal padding, tight line-height on the headline, comfortable paragraph spacing, then the form fields have loose margins between them. The CTA button is flush to the column edges — no inset margin — which gives it maximum visual weight.

---

## Modal Layer 2: Cookie Consent Banner (Secondary Modal)

Positioned in the **lower-right quadrant**, partially overlapping the newsletter modal. This is a **smaller floating card** with:

- Light background with rounded-corner treatment (softer than the sharp-edged newsletter modal)
- Long legal copy paragraph, small font size, left-aligned
- Two stacked CTAs: a full-width dark "Primary CTA" button, and a lighter text link "Secondary CTA" with an icon prefix

The z-layering here is notable: the cookie banner sits **on top of** the newsletter modal, which itself sits on top of the blurred page — three distinct depth planes.

## Notable Layout Patterns

### Layered Depth (Z-axis composition)
The most distinctive layout pattern here is the **deliberate three-layer stack**: page → newsletter modal → cookie banner. Each layer has its own visual treatment (blurred, sharp card, light card), creating a sense of depth without any 3D transforms. This is a spatial hierarchy communicated through focus and contrast, not position.

### Background Carousel as Spatial Context
The hero background cycling through multiple editorial stills (visible in the background across frames) while the modal remains static creates a **figure/ground tension** — the background is alive and moving, but you cannot access it until the modal is dismissed. This is a sophisticated attention mechanism that communicates editorial richness without overwhelming.

### Typography as Layout Signal
The newsletter headline — bold, condensed, all-caps — reads at roughly 2× the size of the body copy. This 2:1 ratio creates a strong vertical rhythm within the modal's right column. The monospaced quality of the type (tight tracking, no kerning softness) reinforces the brand's visual identity even in a functional UI component.

### Modal as Brand Expression
The split-image/form layout of the newsletter modal is not generic — it carries brand DNA. The sharp editorial photograph taking up 40% of the modal width means even a compliance-driven popup communicates the brand's visual identity. The image is not decorative; it is load-bearing for brand perception.
