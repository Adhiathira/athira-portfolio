# Motion Brief — Dark Insurgent Challenger

This is a creative brief for a motion director translating a static, type-led dark system into time. The source is not a decorative site; it is a high-contrast studio composition where oversized white type sits as architecture on a near-black ground (`#19191c`), and where accent color functions as a rationed code-editor signal rather than ornament. The motion job is to give that composition velocity without turning it into a toy. Read every direction below through one filter: this system will front a B2B SaaS platform, so the motion must earn a challenger's edge while still reading as enterprise-grade polish. Snap-to-rest, not bounce. Kinetic, not chaotic. The signature easing of the source — `cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`, a hard acceleration into a long deceleration — is the spine of everything that follows.

## Scroll Opening

The opening beat should feel like a system booting under load, not a page fading in. Hold the near-black ground for a beat with nothing but the `{ }` bracket label alive bottom-left, then let the hero headline arrive as two mass strokes cutting up from the baseline — a fast upward clip-reveal per line, offset by roughly 80ms so the second line chases the first rather than moving in lockstep. Nothing eases in softly; each line accelerates hard and decelerates into a dead stop, so the type appears to slam into position and lock. Scroll should feel weighted and deliberate — a slight scroll-smoothing (lerp around 0.08–0.1) so the long dark field glides rather than jerks, reinforcing the sense that the reader is dragging a heavy, precise instrument down the page.

## Hero Experience

The hero's rule is that the type is the subject and the 3D objects are punctuation, so motion must protect that hierarchy. The butterfly-form split-gradient object top-center and the cyan sculptural knot bottom-right should drift on a slow, continuous parallax — a few degrees of rotation and a small positional float tied loosely to pointer position, never fast enough to pull focus from the letterforms. The ghost pill CTA bottom-right is the payoff of the composition: it should be the last element to resolve, its `#fd356e` border drawing on as a single stroke that completes the diagonal tension between the bottom-left bracket label and the bottom-right action. On hover the CTA does not fill — it pulses its border luminance and nudges by one or two pixels, keeping the ghost-only discipline intact. The whole hero should hold still once settled; stillness after a violent entrance is what makes it read as controlled rather than busy.

## Section Transitions

Transitions here are mode-switches, not decorative wipes. The marquee bands are the kinetic core: oversized text tears horizontally across the frame at a steady velocity, colored pill labels riding inside the letterforms, the line ripped past both viewport edges so the crop implies infinite continuation. Tie marquee speed to scroll velocity with inertia — the band lurches when the user scrolls fast and coasts to near-still when they stop, so the motion always feels driven by the reader rather than looping on its own clock. The hard-cut from the marquee runway into the two-column tools rows should be exactly that: a hard cut, no cross-fade. Each tool row snaps in against its `rgba(172, 172, 175, 0.12)` hairline divider — graphic clipping in from its column edge, category label and headline settling a beat later in the row's assigned accent (magenta primary, cyan secondary). The dark ground never changes across these transitions; the only relief in the entire scroll is the final break onto the lifted dark footer surface, which should feel like an exhale — a genuine deceleration in pacing, not another cut.

## Content Rhythm

Rhythm is built on scale contrast and staggered arrival. The type system lurches from 14px body straight to massive display with no transitional sizes, and motion should honor that jump rather than smooth it — small elements arrive in tight, quick staggers (40–60ms apart) while display type arrives as single heavy strokes. Motion priority follows reading priority: the section's largest visual anchor resolves first, then the headline, then supporting copy, then the CTA, then any secondary list or card items cascade in last. Keep the stagger disciplined; the difference between challenger-kinetic and gimmick is whether the eye is being led in sequence or merely decorated. Everything decelerates into a locked rest state — no residual motion, no idle float except the hero's peripheral 3D objects.

## Footer Approach

The footer is the release. The near-black-to-lifted-surface shift is the single biggest tonal event on the page and it should be paced, not snapped — let the lifted dark surface (`#252529`) rise into frame as the dark plugin-grid settles above it, so the reader feels the page decompress. Inside the lifted surface zone, motion drops almost to zero: the newsletter underline input draws its `#fd356e` focus stroke on focus as the only surviving flash of the primary accent, tying the footer conversion moment back to the hero CTA with a single color signal. The legal strip and logo mark simply fade up quietly. Nothing in the footer should compete for attention; its job is to prove the system knows when to stop moving.

## Motion Principles

Four rules govern the whole build. First, one easing voice — the decelerated snap-to-rest curve from the source is the brand's kinetic signature; resist the urge to introduce spring or bounce. Second, motion is reader-driven — marquees, parallax, and reveals respond to scroll and pointer input rather than running on autonomous loops, which keeps the site feeling like an instrument under the user's hand. Third, stillness is a design element — every animated element must arrive at a dead-stop rest state, because the contrast between violent entrance and total stillness is what reads as premium rather than frantic. Fourth, accent color is rationed in motion exactly as it is in layout — `#fd356e` appears only in the CTA border draw and the input focus stroke; the cyan secondary accent animates only within its assigned rows. Motion must never spend a color the layout does not already own.

## Video Placements

**1. Hero background field — `hero-bg.mp4`.** Section role: the full-viewport dark ground behind the oversized white headline. What it shows: a near-black `#19191c` field with a single slow-moving volumetric light gradient drifting behind the type — subject is atmosphere, not object; motion quality is glacial, sub-pixel, almost subliminal drift with faint grain; mood is dark-studio depth, a room with one moving light source. Duration: 10s narrative loop so the drift never visibly repeats within a normal read. Why video beats a still: a still `#19191c` fill is inert, but a barely-moving light field gives the architectural type something live to sit against, adding depth behind the letterforms without breaking the type-as-subject rule — the motion is felt, not watched.

**2. Marquee runway band — `marquee-kinetic.mp4`.** Section role: the horizontal-scroll feature band that tears oversized text across the frame between hero and tools rows. What it shows: an ambient loop of the accent pills and abstract 3D forms in slow lateral travel and gentle rotation, ready to composite behind or beside the tearing type; motion quality is steady, mechanical, conveyor-like; mood is a production line of capabilities in motion. Duration: 5s tight loop, since the band's perceived speed is driven by scroll inertia layered on top of the base loop. Why video beats a still: the marquee's entire meaning is implied motion frozen at the crop, and a genuine looping bed lets the band keep a low idle drift even when scroll stops, so the section reads as alive between interactions rather than as a static strip waiting to be scrolled.

**3. Tools-section anchor visual — `tool-orbit.mp4`.** Section role: the left-column abstract graphic in one flagship two-column tool row. What it shows: a single sculptural 3D form (in the row's assigned accent, e.g. the cyan knot) rotating slowly on one axis inside its column bounds; motion quality is smooth, weighted, product-render precision; mood is a specimen under studio light. Duration: 5s tight loop. Why video beats a still: the tools rows are where the site shifts from brand spectacle to product explanation, and a live rotating render signals engineering craft — it demonstrates the object rather than depicting it, which is exactly the credibility a B2B challenger needs at the moment it starts making functional claims.
