# Hermès — Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page loads on a full-viewport-width image field — no animation on arrival, no visible load-in transition. The warm cream background (`#fcf7f1`) is present from the first frame. A single italic EBGaramond label anchors the lower-left corner of the hero image, at full opacity, already placed. The site does not announce itself; it has been here. The visitor is not welcomed; they have arrived somewhere that was not waiting for them specifically.

## Hero Experience

The hero is a static image composition — no ambient video, no parallax, no background motion. The full-bleed image bleeds edge to edge horizontally and extends to the bottom fold with no clipping, achieving near-full-height viewport coverage. The single lowercase EBGaramond italic text label at the lower-left is the only informational element. There is no CTA button in the hero. The absence is deliberate: the only affordance offered is to scroll. The hero makes one statement and provides one path forward.

## Section Transitions

As the user scrolls through the site, sections transition without animation. Full-bleed images are replaced by other full-bleed images, then two-column image pairs, then the four-column category grid — all on the same unbroken cream background with no dividers, no color breaks, and no geometric transitions. The scroll reads as moving through a document, not navigating between scenes. The pacing is entirely user-controlled; no scroll-triggered animation choreographs the content's arrival.

## Content Rhythm

There is no scroll-triggered content rhythm. Individual elements within sections do not animate into view. Images do not fade in, headlines do not slide up, grid cards do not stagger. The page is a sequence of still compositions — each typographically complete, each photographically resolved. The only continuous animation in the page experience is the carousels: three distinct Swiper implementations (runway, product detail, category browsing) use 1-second linear visibility transitions between slides. The carousel is the site's primary interactive motion: controlled by the user, mechanical in its timing (linear easing, no spring), and marked by a pagination counter (`01/61`) that measures progress through the collection rather than celebrating each advance.

## Footer Approach

The footer continues on the same warm cream background as the rest of the site — no dark footer zone, no color break, no visual terminus. The four-column link grid, newsletter CTA row, and bottom copyright strip are typographically continuous with the page above them. The scroll journey ends not with a closing statement but with the document simply ending: the cream field concludes, a copyright line marks the final line. The site does not need to announce its ending.

## Motion Principles

The motion philosophy is mechanical precision over expressiveness. The navigation bar's 0.3s ease container transitions handle the only global animation. The carousel's 1-second linear transitions are the only content animation. The navigation link hover uses a two-phase underline animation — the underline contracts from 100% to 0% anchored at the right edge, then expands back to 100% anchored at the left edge — a retract-then-extend mechanical wipe that communicates exactitude rather than fluidity. No other motion exists in the system. This is a site that trusts the photograph to hold attention without animation support. It is among the most restrained motion systems in contemporary luxury e-commerce.

## Video Placements

This site does not use ambient video in its current design, and its aesthetic does not call for it. The design's confidence in still photography — full-bleed, edge-to-edge, silent — is its most radical statement. Adding video would shift the register toward spectacle, which is precisely what this design refuses. However, if a single video placement were to be considered:

**Optional — Hero atmospheric loop (near-still)**
- Suggested file: `hero-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: A near-imperceptible drift across a textured material surface — leather, silk, or woven fabric — in warm directional light; so slow the viewer may not register it as video; 8–10s loop, shot at same warm cream color temperature as the site's photography; the movement should be indistinguishable from high-resolution image compression artifacts
- Duration: 8–10s loop
- Why video: The hero's full-bleed static image is already at the edge of its format — it has as much presence as a photograph can carry. A near-still video would add one quality the photograph cannot: a sense that the material is alive. This is the only register in which video earns its place in a system this restrained. The visitor should not be able to name whether they are seeing a photograph or a video. The uncertainty itself is the effect.
