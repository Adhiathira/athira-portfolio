About

FIELDSET is an independent quarterly publication for working engineers. No investment. No advertisers. No sponsored content. Funded by readers who believe engineering has a written record worth keeping.

The Argument

Editorial Philosophy

Engineering decisions are made in meetings, in Slack threads, in pull request comments, and in the two-hour window before an incident escalates. The reasoning disappears. The decision persists. The next engineer inherits the result without the argument.

FIELDSET exists to publish the argument. A post-mortem is not a blame document — it is a reasoning document. An RFC is not a proposal — it is a decision in progress. An architecture decision record is not documentation — it is the written form of institutional memory. When these documents are published, engineering becomes a discipline with a written record. When they are not published, it remains a series of undocumented choices.

We do not publish abstractions. We publish decisions. The documents in FIELDSET are primary sources: the actual post-mortem as submitted, the actual RFC draft with its revision history, the actual incident timeline with the Slack thread attached. No editorial translation. No retrospective wisdom. The engineering in its own words.

"A specification is a contract written in advance. The question is whether anyone reads it after the contract is signed."

Document Standards

Eight Categories
01

Post-Mortem

Timeline + contributing factors + immediate response + systemic remediation. Published within 90 days of incident. The five-whys analysis is optional; the timeline is not.

02

Incident Report

Real-time timeline of a service disruption. Slack thread excerpts permitted and encouraged. Published as-submitted — editorial cleanup is not applied to timestamps or communication records.

03

RFC Draft

A change proposal at any stage of its lifecycle — open, accepted, or rejected. Revision history must be included. An accepted RFC without its alternatives is an incomplete document.

04

RFC Commentary

A structured analysis of a published RFC — examining the reasoning, the deferred edge cases, and the implementation divergence. Must cite the specific RFC version being examined.

05

Architecture Decision Record

The ADR format: context, decision, status, consequences. Superseded ADRs are published with their successor — the full chain of decisions, not just the current one.

06

Infrastructure Note

Practitioner notes on a specific infrastructure concern: configuration, capacity, observability, or operational behavior. Field notes, not tutorials. The reader is assumed to be at parity.

07

Spec Review

A technical examination of a published specification against observed implementation behavior. Where the spec and the production system diverge — and why.

08

Essay

A structured argument about an engineering practice, concept, or decision. Must take a position. Opinion without evidence is not an essay. Evidence without a position is a data dump.

Submit

How to Contribute
1.

Determine Document Type

Identify which of the eight document categories your submission falls under. A document that spans multiple categories should be split into separate submissions or restructured to fit one category. Hybrid documents are rejected — not because they lack value, but because they lack form.

2.

Prepare the Document

Plain text or Markdown only. No formatted Word documents. No PDFs. Company names and individual names may be anonymized — but technical details may not be. A post-mortem with anonymized infrastructure specifics is not a post-mortem.

3.

Structural Review

FIELDSET editors review for structural completeness only — does the document contain the required elements for its category? We do not rewrite, polish, or improve your writing. A document that is technically complete but poorly written will be published poorly written. Writing is engineering.

4.

Publication

Documents are held for the next quarterly issue. Accepted documents are published without modification to any technical content. The version published is the version submitted. The permanent archive version is the version published.
