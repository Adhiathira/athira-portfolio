— Method & Protocol

A measurement is only as good as the method that produces it.

— Test Standards

Three international standards govern every QP result.

ISO 5626

Fold Endurance

Paper and board. Determination of folding endurance. Specifies the double-fold method and the tensile load applied during folding. The primary standard for QP-7 results.

TAPPI T511

Fold Endurance (Alternative)

MIT fold endurance test for paper and paperboard. Alternative to ISO 5626 used in North American laboratory practice. QP-7 calibrated to both; values are not directly convertible.

ISO 534

Paper Thickness

Paper and board. Determination of thickness, density and specific volume. Governs the measurement geometry, contact pressure, and cycle time for QP-12 caliper readings.

— Step by Step

Four stages. No shortcuts.

01

Sample Conditioning

23°C / 50% RH / 4 hours minimum

Each test sample must be conditioned at 23°C and 50% relative humidity for a minimum of four hours before measurement begins. Moisture content is the primary variable affecting fold endurance — an unconditioned sample can read up to 18% higher than a properly conditioned one due to plasticisation of the fibre network. The QP protocol is strict: conditioning is not optional and cannot be abbreviated. Samples are held flat in a conditioning chamber, not stacked. Coated papers require an additional 2 hours.

02

Instrument Calibration

Before each test session

The QP-12 is zeroed against the 250µm reference gauge block at the start of each session. The QP-7's fold tension is set using the dead-weight calibration set: two screws on the spring-load mechanism are adjusted until the pointer aligns with the 0.5 kN or 1.0 kN mark. Both adjustments are logged on the QP data sheet with the technician's initials. Instruments that have been transported require a 20-minute thermal rest before calibration — particularly in cold or humid environments.

03

Measurement Cycle

QP-12: 2.2 seconds / QP-7: until fracture

The QP-12 measurement cycle completes in 2.2 seconds from contact to recorded value. The instrument applies a controlled contact pressure, holds for 0.5 seconds to allow fibre compression to stabilise, and releases. The QP-7 counts double-folds automatically; the fold mechanism is motorised and stops at fracture detection via the resistance sensor. Minimum recommended sample count for a statistically valid result is five specimens, with outliers flagged if the range exceeds 15% of the mean.

04

Reporting

ISO 5626 / ISO 534 format

QP results are reported against the relevant standard. Fold endurance is expressed as the logarithm (base 10) of the double-fold count — the MIT fold number. Thickness is reported in micrometres, rounded to the nearest 1µm. Conservation studios typically report QP-7 values alongside Betti number readings from separate folding endurance tests; the two methods use different fold tension geometries and are not interchangeable. Binderies use QP-12 caliper data to select scoring blades: a 120gsm sheet reading 138µm requires a different blade than one reading 152µm.

— Why Conditioning Matters

A 4% humidity shift changes your fold reading by 8%.

The cellulose fibres in paper are hygroscopic — they absorb and release moisture with changes in ambient relative humidity. At low humidity, fibres contract and stiffen, making them more brittle and reducing fold endurance. At high humidity, they swell and become more pliable. A single percentage point change in RH shifts fold endurance by approximately 2%.

This is why QP results from an unconditioned environment cannot be compared against certified values. The QP protocol mandates conditioning not as bureaucratic caution but as a physical necessity: without it, fold numbers are site-specific rather than universal. Conditioning chambers are available from QP as an accessory to the instrument package.
