# Verso — Concept by Image

A publishing studio where the page is the medium. The split-screen structure mirrors the physical act of opening a book — the spine at center, facing pages spreading outward. The terracotta left panel is ink; the vellum right is paper.

Ultra-light Cormorant Garamond at 11rem carries the gravity of display type in art books — where a single word commands an entire page. Every section is a two-page spread. The split line is not a divider; it is the spine of the book.

The color language is archival: near-black ink (#1a1714), deep plum (#4a3f5c) for endpapers, terracotta (#c94a35) for the tension of a red-cloth binding, warm vellum (#f4f0eb) for the page itself. No digital-native colors — every value references something you could hold.

The typographic hierarchy follows the editorial logic of art books: the display heading dominates at extreme scale, body copy is almost whisper-quiet at weight 300. Captions carry the most tracking — 0.14em — because in print, metadata earns its space through restraint.

Panels swap sides on alternating sections, creating the visual rhythm of page turns. The eye moves left-right, left-right — never static, always in motion through the material.
