VERDURE

The Studio

An editorial practice dedicated to the physical and intellectual life of letterforms.

Founded 2011
V

erdure began in a converted pressroom in East London, when two editors with backgrounds in fine-press publishing decided that the industrial history of typesetting deserved its own dedicated monograph series. The studio was never conceived as a commercial outfit — it was conceived as an argument: that type is not decoration but architecture, not style but substance.

In the first three years, we produced four specimen books entirely by hand, working with letterpress printers in Germany, Belgium, and the Scottish Highlands. Each volume was limited to two hundred and fifty copies, case-bound in natural bookcloth with a sewn text block. Distribution was by post and by word of mouth among typographers, archivists, and printing museum curators.

The studio's editorial philosophy has never changed: every commission begins with primary research. We do not reproduce existing histories — we interrogate them. Our authors are historians, practising type designers, and working printers who bring disciplinary rigour alongside aesthetic feeling. The result is books that hold their own on a historian's shelf and a designer's workbench alike.

Today, VERDURE publishes twelve to sixteen titles each year across three series: the Specimen Library, the Monograph Series, and a more recent strand of editorial system commissions for institutions and cultural organisations. The printed object remains the point. We have never published digitally and have no plans to do so.

What We Believe

Typography is not a tool for making things look considered. It is the mechanism by which considered thought becomes legible. A page set badly does not merely look wrong — it thinks wrong. The hierarchy dissolves, the argument blurs, the reader loses the thread. This is why we treat every typeface decision as an intellectual one, not an aesthetic one.

We believe in slowness. The books we make take eighteen months to three years from commission to publication. That duration is not inefficiency — it is the minimum time required to do the research, set the type with care, print proofs, revise, and bind correctly. Speed is the enemy of precision, and precision is the only thing that distinguishes a specimen from a catalogue.

And we believe in paper. Not as sentiment, not as nostalgia, but as the correct medium for this information. A typeface printed at 1200 dpi on a 130gsm uncoated stock reveals things about its construction that no screen can reproduce. The grain of the ink, the impression into the sheet, the shadow of the letterform — these are primary evidence. We preserve them.

Marguerite

Marguerite Voss

Founding Editor

Marguerite has spent twenty years in type publishing, beginning with a decade at the type archive in Leipzig before establishing VERDURE in 2011. Her monograph on the Didot family remains the definitive English-language account of neoclassical type design and its influence on nineteenth-century book production.

Casimir

Casimir Holdt

Production Director

Casimir trained at the Stempel foundry archive in Frankfurt and subsequently with the production department at Monotype in Salfords, where he developed his practice of letterpress proofing as a form of historical verification. He joined VERDURE in 2014 and oversees all production decisions from paper selection through to binding specification.
