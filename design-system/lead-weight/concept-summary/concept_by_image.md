# Prism Drift — Concept by Image

## Type as Architecture

Prism Drift is constructed entirely from type. There are no decorative illustrations, no photograph-as-atmosphere, no abstract shape system competing with the letterforms. Every element of the page — from the full-bleed masthead to the narrowest footnote column — derives its structural logic from typographic mass. The grid exists to govern the reading experience; spatial decisions (column widths, margins, vertical rhythm) are made in service of the text, not in service of a brand expression separate from the text. When letterforms are deployed at display scale — upward of 120pt — they cease to function as pure language and become architecture: weight-bearing forms that hold the visual composition the way columns hold a building. Display type in this system is not announcement; it is structure. The editorial hierarchy from headline to subhead to body to caption is not a cascade of typographic styles — it is a weight register, a controlled sequence of typographic mass from heavy to light that the eye navigates before the mind reads a word.

## Weight Register and Editorial Hierarchy

The system operates across a compressed weight register of two: 900 Black for all display-scale elements, 300 Light for all body and supporting text, with no intermediate weights appearing on principal surfaces. This is not a limitation; it is the hierarchy mechanism. Typographic mass does the work that color, elevation, and ornament perform in lesser systems. A 900 Black headline at 96px commands a section not through contrast of hue but through contrast of weight register — the visual gravity of its forms draws the eye before any semantic content is processed. The reading texture of body columns, set in 300 Light at 16–17px with generous leading at 1.65, is deliberately low-mass: a field of air around the prose that makes the Black display elements feel seismic by comparison. The display-to-body weight ratio of 900:300 is not a typographic flourish; it is the entire editorial hierarchy system. Every spatial and chromatic decision in the design exists to protect that ratio and keep it legible.

## Negative Tracking and Column Measure

At display sizes the system applies negative letter-spacing, typically −0.03em to −0.05em, tightening the typographic mass of headlines into a dense optical block. Loose tracking at large scale is a mechanical default of screen rendering that this system corrects. The result is that large type locks together visually, reading as unified mass rather than a sequence of isolated glyphs with air between them. Body columns are set to a controlled measure of 60–70 characters per line (approximately 32–38em), the range empirically optimal for sustained reading comprehension and standard in quality editorial and book typography. Where two-column layouts are used, the split is unequal: a narrow annotation column at roughly one-third width sits alongside a two-thirds prose column. This asymmetry mirrors the structure of scholarly and editorial print — where marginalia and body text occupy distinct registers of authority — and it creates a reading texture in which the annotating voice and the primary voice are spatially separated without being visually disconnected. The x-height contrast between display and body cuts is preserved across all column widths; no scale compression is applied at smaller viewport breakpoints that would collapse the weight ratio.

## Hairline Rules as the Sole Decorative Element

No shadows. No gradients. No box borders beyond 0.5px hairline rules. These rules appear at three positions in the system: a full-width rule beneath the masthead navigation, section-separating rules between editorial blocks, and an optional inset rule between annotation and prose columns in two-column layouts. At 0.5–1px, these rules do not read as containers or frames — they register as pauses, the typographic equivalent of a breath between sections. Their thinness is critical: a 1px rule at normal screen resolution is barely present, which is the intention. It is visible enough to organize, absent enough not to intrude. Any heavier rule weight would cross the threshold from organizational device into decoration, and this system will not cross that threshold. The hairline rule is the one decorative permission the system grants itself, and it takes it at the thinnest possible weight consistent with rendering.

## Warm Paper Surfaces and Near-Black Text

The surface system is built on warm paper tones rather than clinical whites. Background values occupy the range #F7F4EE to #F2EDE3 — cream, ivory, slightly warm — suppressing the harsh blue-white of default screen backgrounds and evoking the material quality of a well-produced printed page. Text is set in a near-black, #1A1714, which is marginally warmer than pure #000000 and resolves the subtle tension between screen rendering and the warm surface beneath it. This pairing is not sentimental nostalgia for print; it is a precision decision about reading comfort and perceptual tone. Warm-ground, near-black text reduces the physiological harshness of high-contrast reading over extended engagement. The surface feels like something that was made with considered material choices, not output to a default renderer. A secondary background tone at #EDEAE2 distinguishes subsidiary sections — footnotes, sidebars, pull-quote zones — from the primary reading surface without introducing a chromatic departure from the warm paper register.

## Editorial Accent as Punctuation

A single accent color — deep navy approximately #1B2A4A, or a restrained indigo at #2D2F6B — appears in the system at low frequency and high specificity. It marks active navigation states, inline hyperlinks in body text, an occasional pull-quote hairline, and initial caps in long-form editorial passages. The accent does not saturate the page. It appears as visual punctuation, functioning the way a second-color pass functions in offset printing — a precision addition, not a coating. Its scarcity is what gives it force. If the accent appeared on more than three to five percent of the visible surface at any scroll position, it would cease to punctuate and begin to decorate. The system does not permit this. The deep navy is cool against the warm paper surface, which is intentional: the thermal contrast between surface temperature and accent temperature gives the accent its visual authority without requiring it to be loud.

## Masthead Typography and Uppercase Labels

Navigation and section labels are set in uppercase, tracked at +0.15em to +0.20em, at a scale small enough to register as indexing rather than headline — typically 11–12px. This is the typographic convention of quality editorial and print publication: uppercase running heads, section markers, and captions are not declarations; they are organizational instruments. The increased tracking compensates for the optical narrowing that uppercase letterforms produce at small sizes, restoring legibility while maintaining the understated register that distinguishes a label from a headline. The x-height contrast between these uppercase labels at 11px and the body prose at 16–17px is a deliberate register separation — the label occupies a different scale tier, never competing with the body for reading attention. The masthead logotype uses Black weight uppercase for the brand mark and Light weight for navigation links, maintaining the weight contrast logic even at the smallest structural level of the interface.

## Interaction States and Typographic Logic

Hover states and focus indicators respect the typographic foundation without departing from it. Interactive text links reveal a 1px underline on hover — not a background fill, not a color shift, not a button transformation. The underline appears at the baseline, matching the hairline weight used in structural dividers throughout the system. This consistency between structural rules and interaction indicators is deliberate: the system's decorative vocabulary and its behavioral vocabulary share the same formal element. The underline reveal is not an animation of duration; it appears at the moment of hover without a transition delay, consistent with a reading experience in which interaction is instant and untheatrical. Visited links retain the same near-black text color because the system has one text color and will not introduce a secondary text color tier for state communication. Focus states for keyboard navigation use the single accent color as a 2px offset outline — the minimum accessibility-compliant indicator, drawn at a weight heavier than the hairline rules to distinguish it as interactive rather than organizational. Typographic mass is stable under interaction; the weight register does not shift, scale, or animate in response to cursor events.
