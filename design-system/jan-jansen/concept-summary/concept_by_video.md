# Jan Jansen Amsterdam — Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page loads with the editorial hero photograph already resolved at full opacity — no fade-in shimmer, no loading skeleton. The Jan Jansen logotype appears first, dropping into position from 8px above with an ease-out curve over 400ms, followed immediately by the navigation items cascading left-to-right at 60ms stagger intervals. The hero headline "Be your best self" begins invisible and rises 12px into its resting position over 600ms with a gentle ease-out, arriving just as the CTA button fades in at full scale. The effect is a composed entry — unhurried, sequential, confident — as if a curtain is being drawn back with one deliberate pull rather than a flourish.

## Hero Experience

The hero section is a strong candidate for ambient looping video, given its reliance on a single large editorial image that currently reads as static. The hero should use a video background showing the model in motion within the Haussmann archway — a slow, deliberate walk toward camera on the red carpet, shot at 48fps and played back at a subtly slowed rate to create a processional quality. The subject is a woman in a deep crimson coat and boots, moving through natural morning light filtered through the stone columns. The video communicates momentum without urgency: the brand's customer is always moving, and always arriving.

## Section Transitions

As the user scrolls past the hero into product sections, sections enter via a gentle vertical reveal — content panels rising from 20px below their resting position as they cross 15% of the viewport. There is no parallax distortion on text elements, which preserves legibility and respects the editorial seriousness of the brand. The crimson CTA buttons across sections animate their border from 0 width to full on hover, describing a contained rectangle that reads as a frame being drawn around the invitation.

## Content Rhythm

Individual product cards and editorial images enter viewport with a staggered opacity-plus-translate sequence: each item fades from 0 to 1 opacity while moving 16px upward, with a 100ms delay between adjacent items in a row. The timing curve is ease-out cubic, so items decelerate into their positions rather than snapping into place. This rhythm creates a sense of measured presentation — each piece shown deliberately, as in a curated display rather than a feed. Text labels and price points follow their images by 120ms, arriving after the visual has registered.

## Footer Approach

The footer appears via a slow background-color transition as the user scrolls its top edge into view — the background shifts from white to the pale sage-grey (#D1D2CB) border token used elsewhere in the system, grounding the close of the page with tactile warmth. Logotype, newsletter input, and link columns all enter together at a single 300ms fade rather than staggering, signaling that this is the container, not the content. No closing animation fires on exit; the footer simply rests.

## Motion Principles

All timing uses ease-out curves to mimic the deceleration of a considered gesture. Duration windows sit between 300ms and 700ms — fast enough to feel responsive, slow enough to feel deliberate. Nothing bounces, nothing springs, nothing overshoots. The single motion accent in the entire system is the CTA button border-draw on hover: a 200ms stroke animation that frames the invitation without decoration. For a brand whose visual language is compression and precision, motion should function the same way — each animation removes clutter rather than adding it.

## Video Placements

**Hero Section**
- Section role: Primary brand statement and first impression
- Suggested asset filename: hero-paris-red-carpet.mp4
- What the video shows: A woman in a crimson coat walks slowly toward camera through a symmetrical stone archway in morning Paris light; the red carpet beneath her feet is visible; natural light flickers slightly as clouds shift overhead; 10-second seamless loop
- Duration: 10s loop
- Why video serves this section: The static photograph communicates the moment; video communicates the approach — the deliberate walk toward the viewer activates the brand's core message of self-possession in motion rather than in pose

**Collection Editorial Section**
- Section role: Seasonal collection introduction, midpage
- Suggested asset filename: collection-atelier-light.mp4
- What the video shows: Close-up footage of leather shoe uppers on a white surface in a bright Amsterdam atelier; natural light from a north-facing window rakes across the surface at an oblique angle, revealing the grain and sheen of the leather; no visible hands or product movement — only light moving across material; 5-second loop
- Duration: 5s loop
- Why video serves this section: Product photography shows form; ambient material video shows substance — the viewer understands the quality of the material through the behavior of light on its surface, which no still photograph conveys with the same immediacy

**Red Carpet Category Section**
- Section role: Occasion-wear category landing within the editorial flow
- Suggested asset filename: red-carpet-arrival.mp4
- What the video shows: A pair of heeled shoes descending the shallow steps of a well-lit European building entrance in late afternoon light; only the feet and lower hem of a gown are visible; the step is marble or stone, pale and smooth; the motion is slow, one step at a time, looping seamlessly at the landing; 5-second loop
- Duration: 5s loop
- Why video serves this section: The Red Carpet category is built around an idea — the moment of arrival — rather than a functional product attribute; video of the act of arriving on a grand staircase communicates this idea more directly than any headline or product shot
