Capabilities

Everything your team needs to own latency

From ingestion to alert, Conduit closes the loop between trace data and the engineers who need to act on it.

📡
Ingestion

OTel-native collector

Accepts OTLP over gRPC and HTTP. No custom agents, no SDK changes. Point your existing exporters at Conduit and start ingesting in minutes.

🗺
Service Graph

Live dependency mapping

Automatically derives your service topology from span parent-child relationships. See how a latency spike in one service propagates downstream in real time.

📊
Analytics

p50/p95/p99 per endpoint

Latency histograms broken down by service, operation, environment, and custom span attributes. Slice any dimension without pre-defining queries.

🔔
Alerting

Regression detection

Statistical baseline anomaly detection on p99 latency and error rates. Alerts route to Slack, PagerDuty, or any webhook — with trace context attached.

🔍
Explorer

Full-fidelity trace view

Drill into any individual trace with a Gantt-style span waterfall, attribute inspector, and linked log events from your existing log provider.

🔗
Integrations

Works with your stack

Native integrations for Datadog, Grafana, PagerDuty, and GitHub. Connect CI/CD pipelines to correlate deploys with performance regressions automatically.

Setup

Up and running in three steps

01

Configure your OTLP exporter

Update your OpenTelemetry SDK configuration to point your OTLP exporter at the Conduit collector endpoint. No code changes required beyond your config.

OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT=ingest.conduit.dev
02

Define your baseline

Set latency and error rate SLOs per service or per operation. Conduit builds a rolling statistical baseline over your first 24 hours and begins anomaly detection automatically.

p99_latency_budget: 200ms
03

Connect your alert channels

Route alerts to Slack, PagerDuty, or any webhook. Every alert includes the full trace ID, the affected service, the delta from baseline, and a direct link to the explorer.

alert_channel: slack://eng-on-call
