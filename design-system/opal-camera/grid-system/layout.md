# Brand Name — Layout Brief

## Overall Page Structure

The page flows through five distinct zones: a full-bleed cinematic hero, a product showcase grid, a full-bleed transitional image band, a three-column product card grid, and a typographic footer with an oversized brand lockup. The rhythm alternates between immersive full-width moments and structured grid layouts — dark then light, atmospheric then clinical, emotional then informational.

---

## Header / Navigation

A fixed, transparent navigation bar floats over the hero. It's horizontally divided into three zones:

- **Left anchor:** Brand wordmark, minimal weight
- **Center:** Three top-level nav items with dropdown sub-links rendered below each — these hang visibly in the open state, creating a vertical expansion that feels like a magazine dropdown rather than a flyout panel
- **Right anchor:** A secondary brand/app mark (monochrome, circular)

The nav is sparse and high-contrast — light type over the dark hero image. On light backgrounds, the nav text inverts to dark. No border, no background fill — it reads as pure type floating in space.

---

## Hero Section

**Full-bleed cinematic, two-layer overlay composition.**

The hero spans 100% viewport width and approximately 85–90% viewport height. A moody lifestyle photograph fills the entire frame — warm tones, soft natural light, shallow depth of field. The image has a dark vignette or slight color grade that darkens one edge, making light text legible without an explicit overlay.

Two content elements float over the image:

- **Bottom-left:** A large, two-line headline set in light color — bold, grounded, occupies maybe 30% of horizontal width
- **Below the headline:** A pill-shaped Primary CTA button — rounded rectangle, high-contrast accent color against the dark photo, compact and confident
- **Top-right:** A sign-up module — a translucent frosted panel with a label, a text input, and a pill-shaped submit button. This floats independently in the upper-right quadrant, creating a deliberate asymmetry with the headline

The hero uses **overlapping layers**: nav on top, floating sign-up module at mid-right, headline and CTA anchored bottom-left. Nothing is centered. The tension between the bottom-left text and top-right module creates dynamic diagonal spatial energy.

---

## Content Sections

### Section 2 — Full-Bleed Transitional Image Band

A second lifestyle photograph runs full-width at reduced height — roughly 40–45% viewport height. No text overlay. Pure visual breathing room. This functions as a palette cleanser and spatial separator between the hero and the product grid below. The image is cropped tightly and feels cinematic in its horizontal aspect ratio.

### Section 3 — Three-Column Product Card Grid

A clean light background section divided into three equal-width columns by thin vertical rules (1px hairline dividers spanning the full section height). Each column contains:

- **Top:** Product name in large, bold sans-serif — center-aligned, heavy weight
- **Below name:** Short descriptor tagline in muted color — center-aligned, one line
- **CTA:** Accent-colored pill button (same system as hero CTA) — center-aligned
- **Bottom:** Product image — each product rendered at consistent scale, bottom-anchored within the column

The three-column layout is strictly symmetric — equal column widths, equal internal spacing. The accent pill buttons create a horizontal alignment that ties the three columns together visually. This section feels like a structured product catalog page: efficient, legible, no decoration beyond the hairline rules.

## Footer

**Two-zone footer with a massive typographic centerpiece.**

The footer is split horizontally:

### Upper Footer — Oversized Brand Wordmark

The brand name set in an enormous, ultra-heavy sans-serif that runs nearly the full width of the viewport — the letterforms bleed off both left and right edges. The first letterform alone occupies roughly 40% of the viewport width. This is a pure typographic statement: the brand name becomes a visual texture, a design element rather than a label.

A thin horizontal rule separates this wordmark zone from the content below.

### Lower Footer — Four-Column Navigation Grid + Sign-Up

The footer is divided into a left zone and a right zone by the continuation of the oversized type column:

- **Left (narrow column):** Newsletter/sign-up headline in medium weight, followed by a short descriptor paragraph in muted color, then a circular/pill arrow button — dark fill, light arrow
- **Right (wider zone):** Four labeled columns — each with 3–4 text links below their muted uppercase category label

### Legal Strip

A minimal one-liner at the very bottom: company name left, rights statement center, and a scroll-to-top button (circle with upward arrow) anchored far right.

## Notable Layout Patterns

**Floating/overlapping elements in the hero:** The sign-up widget and the headline/CTA intentionally occupy opposite corners, creating an asymmetric two-point composition over the photography. Neither element is centered — the hero resists convention.

**Full-bleed imagery as structural dividers:** Both the hero and the transitional band use photography as layout infrastructure, not decoration. They define zones by mood and density rather than by grid lines.

**Hairline column rules:** The product grid uses 1px vertical lines as a grid expression — they're structural and decorative simultaneously, a pattern associated with editorial/magazine layout.

**Typographic overflow as decoration:** The footer wordmark intentionally overflows the container. This is a deliberate design choice — using scale and cropping to create texture from letterforms, common in high-craft brand identities.

**Consistent CTA system:** The accent pill button appears identically in the hero, the product grid, and implicitly throughout — it's a repeating component that creates visual rhythm and brand recognition across the scroll experience.

**Spatial rhythm:** Dark → light → dark → light → light. The color alternation mirrors the emotional journey: cinematic/aspirational → functional/informational → bold/typographic.
