# Weld Ivory — Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page arrives already composed. A near-white field fills the viewport; the navigation is transparent, its letter-spaced Manrope links floating above the surface. The headline — EB Garamond italic, open-tracked, at the largest heading scale — is present at full opacity from the first frame. There is no entrance animation: the design has been here, waiting. As the visitor settles, a subtle ambient warmth is already in motion behind the hero — soft, atmospheric, low-contrast — suggesting material depth rather than announcing it.

## Hero Experience

The hero is a live composition rather than a reveal sequence. A video background loop — slow, nearly still, close to texture — runs beneath the headline and product object at full bleed. The 3D product rotates independently at its own unhurried pace, inhabiting the center of the frame. The motion vocabulary here is dissociated: the background video, the product rotation, and the scroll gesture are three independent tempos operating simultaneously, each unaware of the others. The headline sits behind the product in Z-order; the CTA (Apple blue pill) is the topmost layer, the only element that reads as interactive.

**Hero video candidate:** `hero-bg.mp4`
A slow horizontal drift across an ivory textile surface — wool, linen, or pressed paper — at near-macro distance. Morning light from a single direction, soft shadow edge moving imperceptibly across the frame. 10s loop, no sharp cut. The motion should be detectable only in retrospect, not during viewing. The surface should evoke weight and texture without representing any specific material.

## Section Transitions

Content sections arrive from below, sliding over the hero as the scroll proceeds. The critical transition device is the organic drip divider at the base of each light-background section — an irregular liquid-spill boundary with 3–4 large soft drips descending into the dark transitional zone. This shape is static but its presence makes the boundary feel poured rather than cut. The scroll-triggered entrance for section content follows the languid timing of the motion system: 0.45s base duration, 0.15s stagger between headline → body → CTA. The effect is a slow cascade, not a snap.

## Content Rhythm

Individual elements enter the viewport with gentle fade-up reveals. The stagger step is 0.15s — perceptible but not mechanical. Headlines arrive first, body paragraphs follow a quarter-second later, and CTAs last. The sequence reads as the content composing itself in real time, word by word in visual terms. The product scroll actor — the rendered 3D bottle — continues its path through sections, appearing in the foreground of some, receding behind media in others. Its movement is the page's motion signature: continuous, autonomous, unhurried.

## Footer Approach

The page closes on a dark-background section before the footer. As the user scrolls into this final zone, the nav bar transitions to a frosted light state — its letter-spaced typography and Apple-blue CTA still visible, now hovering above a dark ground. The footer likely mirrors the transitional dark register, closing the alternating rhythm that has paced the entire scroll.

## Motion Principles

The motion vocabulary is unhurried. The 0.45s base duration creates a pace that asks the visitor to slow down — transitions do not confirm input immediately but absorb it, resolving a half-beat later. Stagger intervals of 0.15s give sequential reveals a deliberate quality: this is a system that sequences, not one that snaps. The full-screen transitions (0.75s) are the slowest elements on the page — used only for section-level shifts where the change should feel like turning a page. Reduced motion support is built in; all timing collapses to 0.01ms for users who prefer it.

## Video Placements

**Placement 1 — Hero background**
`hero-bg.mp4`
Slow horizontal drift across an ivory textile surface, morning light, near-macro. 10s loop. The hero needs temporal depth — a still image here would feel inert against the rotating 3D product. The ambient movement creates the impression of a living space without competing with the product's own motion.

**Placement 2 — Product showcase section**
`product-ambient.mp4`
Close-up of the product object on a surface, gentle light rotation — not the same 3D render, but a physical version photographed with a slow camera movement around the subject. 5s loop. The product showcase section is the moment where the digital 3D object should be grounded in physical reality; video achieves this transition from render to object more convincingly than a still image.

**Placement 3 — Studio or editorial section**
`studio-light.mp4`
Wide interior — table surface, natural light from a tall window, objects arranged but not styled. Slow zoom out, barely perceptible. 10s loop. The editorial section between product and commerce needs atmospheric credibility; video provides the temporal dimension (light shifting, dust, warmth) that a still cannot. This section is where the brand's personal voice is strongest — the video grounds that voice in a specific, physical place.
