How It's Made

From precipitation to print.

Silver-gelatin emulsion production has not changed in its fundamentals since 1880. What has changed is the consistency of who makes it and the care of how it is done. This page documents what we do, in the order we do it.

The Sequence

Seven stages. Forty-eight hours.

Stage 01

Silver Nitrate Preparation

Duration: 2 hours

Silver nitrate is weighed and dissolved in distilled water at room temperature. Concentration is measured by refractive index to within 0.2%. This step is done the morning before the main precipitation, allowing full dissolution and temperature equilibration. The water is sourced from our own distillation unit; municipal water contains chloride ions that will trigger premature precipitation.

Temperature

18°C

Concentration check

Refractive index

Water source

On-site distillation

Stage 02

Gelatin Pre-soak

Duration: 45 minutes

Photographic-grade gelatin is soaked in cold water to swell the granules before heating. This produces a more uniform melt without localized overheating. The gelatin we use is bovine bone gelatin at 220 Bloom. We have used the same supplier since 1974. A different gelatin makes a different emulsion — this is not a replaceable component.

Gelatin grade

220 Bloom

Soak time

45 min cold water

Source

Since 1974

Stage 03

Precipitation

Duration: 4 hours (Baryta), 2.5 hours (Linen)

Silver nitrate solution is added to potassium bromide-gelatin at controlled rate, temperature, and stirring speed. The rate of addition determines grain size. Faster addition = coarser grain. The Baryta emulsion is precipitated more slowly than the Linen, producing the finer grain that registers at the resolution of the baryta interlayer.

Precipitation is done in complete darkness with safelight off. There is no automation. The rate is regulated by a stoppered addition funnel and a clock.

Baryta time

4 hours

Linen time

2.5 hours

Control

Manual addition funnel

Stage 04

Ripening

Duration: 90 minutes at 52°C

The emulsion is held at elevated temperature while silver halide crystals reorganize and grow through Ostwald ripening. This is where contrast and photographic speed are determined. We ripen at 52°C for 90 minutes. The chemistry log from 1928 shows the same temperature and time — it was correct then and has not needed changing.

Temperature

52°C

Duration

90 minutes

Protocol source

Palacios log, 1928

Stage 05

Washing

Duration: 6 hours (three washes)

Soluble salts — excess potassium nitrate, unreacted bromide — are washed from the emulsion in three changes of distilled water. The emulsion is allowed to set between washes and then cut and rewashed. This step cannot be accelerated. Residual soluble salts cause emulsion fog, mottled fixing, and — over time — image deterioration.

Stage 06

Hand Coating

Duration: 1 paper per 4 minutes

The redispersed emulsion is poured onto a temperature-controlled coating plate at 38°C and spread with a precision-gap coating knife. The gap is set per grade: 180 microns wet for Linen, 230 microns for Baryta, 280 microns for Carta. Each sheet is coated individually and transferred immediately to the drying rack.

There is no continuous coating line. There is no automated weight check. Quality control is the person coating: if the emulsion does not spread cleanly across the paper surface, the sheet is discarded. The reject rate for Baryta grade is approximately 12%.

Coating temperature

38°C

Baryta wet gap

230 microns

Reject rate (Baryta)

~12%

Stage 07

Drying & Inspection

Duration: 18–24 hours

Coated sheets are hung on wire racks in the same room where the chemistry is mixed. Temperature and relative humidity are controlled, but not mechanically — the building itself, a converted lighthouse keeper's station built in 1908 with stone walls 60cm thick, maintains conditions within acceptable range for most of the year. In summer we hang fewer sheets per day.

After drying, each sheet is inspected under a Macbeth daylight fluorescent lamp for coating defects: pinholes, streaks, uneven density. Defective sheets are destroyed. Acceptable sheets are cut to format and packaged in black polyethylene.

18–24 hours

Inspection lamp

Building year
