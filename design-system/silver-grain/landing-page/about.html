The Archive

History

Loam & Silver was not founded as a business. It was preserved as a record. What follows is what we know, and what we can prove.

Edith Palacios-Vane, 1894–1959.

Edith Palacios-Vane spent thirty-four years with the Spanish Hydrographic Institute. In parallel, she maintained a private darkroom in the basement of her house in Ribeira that was, by any working photographer's account of the period, the most precisely equipped in northwestern Spain. She mixed her own developers from published formulae and her own modifications of those formulae. She kept a chemistry log from 1928 to 1958 that records every batch, every deviation, and every failure.

She did not consider herself a photographer in the artistic sense. She considered herself a technician who had learned, over thirty years, what silver and gelatin would do if treated correctly. The prints she made were working documents: tide records, survey photographs, hydrographic plates. Their beauty was incidental and, to her, irrelevant.

When she died in 1959, her darkroom was intact. Her estate engaged a young photographic chemist — Javier Gallo Ferrer, twenty-six years old, employed by the Institute in a capacity that mainly involved maintaining the fixation tanks — to evaluate the equipment. He spent three months cataloguing it. He never left.

People

The record so far.

Edith Palacios-Vane

Founder (posthumous) · 1894–1959

Hydrographic surveyor, Spanish Hydrographic Institute, 1925–1959. Author of three formulae still in active production: the Palacios Developer, the LV-30 paper developer, and the slow-clearing archival fixer. Her chemistry log — 847 pages, carbon copy — is held in our coating room. It is not archived. It is consulted.

Javier Gallo Ferrer

Chemist & Coater · Est. 1961

Began working from Edith Palacios-Vane's chemistry notes in 1961. Established production at Punta Carreiro in 1963. Has not moved. Has not automated. Has trained two assistants in fifty years. Currently produces Baryta and Carta grades personally. Does not give interviews.

Gallo Ferrer Family

Second Generation · Since 2009

Javier's son, Marcos Gallo Rodríguez, took over Linen grade production in 2009. He handles chemistry preparation for all three grades. The coating table is still operated by his father. The formula books remain in the same condition they were found in 1961: water-stained, marginally annotated, irreplaceable.
