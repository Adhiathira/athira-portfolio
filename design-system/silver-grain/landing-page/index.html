The Work
"A print from our Baryta grade is an object you hold differently. Not because we say so — because the paper makes you."

Chemistry that develops with time, not against it.

Loam & Silver began when Edith Palacios-Vane's darkroom was dismantled after her death in 1959. Her former assistant, a photographic chemist named Javier Gallo Ferrer, preserved her formulas — not as artifacts, but as working instructions. He began producing them in small batches under a lighthouse keeper's station at Punta Carreiro. By 1965 he was supplying four Spanish film schools and a single Paris printer. The operation has not changed its physical address since.

We produce three silver-gelatin paper grades: Linen, Baryta, and Carta. Each is coated by hand on a single coating table, dried on wire racks in the same room where the chemistry is mixed. There is no continuous coating line. There is no automated quality assurance. There is a coating knife, a thermometer, and a person who has been doing this for a long time.

Fellowship access is open to working photographers, darkroom printers, and photographic conservators by annual nomination. Fourteen fellowships are active at any given time. The median tenancy is three years. There are no application fees. There is a waiting list.

How It Works

From silver nitrate to your print.

  1. 01

    Silver Nitrate Preparation

    We begin with silver nitrate dissolved in distilled water. The concentration is measured to within 0.2% by refractive index, not by weight alone. Temperature is held at 18°C throughout.

  2. 02

    Emulsion Precipitation

    Gelatin is pre-soaked, then silver nitrate is added to potassium bromide solution in controlled increments. The precipitation reaction determines grain size. Our Baryta emulsion takes 4 hours.

  3. 03

    Ripening & Digestion

    The emulsion is held at elevated temperature while the silver halide crystals reorganize and grow. This is where contrast and speed are determined. We ripen at 52°C for 90 minutes.

  4. 04

    Washing & Redispersion

    Soluble salts are washed out in multiple distilled water changes. The emulsion is then redispersed in fresh gelatin before coating.

  5. 05

    Hand Coating

    The liquid emulsion is applied to paper base with a precision-gap coating knife at 38°C. Each sheet is coated individually. There is no batch coating. There is no continuous line.
