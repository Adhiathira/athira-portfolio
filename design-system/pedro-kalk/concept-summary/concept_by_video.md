# Pedro Kalk — Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page loads with the fixed nav already in place and the hero composition fully established: editorial text content left, character image right, logo marquee already in slow horizontal auto-scroll at the section's bottom. No page-load entrance animation; no element staging sequence. The marquee is the only motion at load — a slow, continuous drift of brand logos from right to left, functioning as ambient credibility.

## Hero Experience

The hero is static in its primary content — text and image hold position without scroll-triggered animation. The logo marquee auto-scrolls continuously as an independent ambient element. As the user scrolls, the hero gives way normally to the section title divider below it. There is no parallax layering on the hero, no scroll actor originating from the hero. The hero's strength is in its composition, not its motion.

## Section Transitions

All section transitions are straight horizontal edges with background color changes providing the only separation signal. Light dusty pink-white body throughout (hero → section title → masonry grid → CTA section); dark charcoal-to-black footer as the hard close. The hero-to-section-title transition is the one moment where the visual density drops dramatically — from a complex two-column composition to a single centered headline with a horizontal rule. The masonry-grid-to-CTA transition drops density again. The CTA-to-footer inversion (light to dark) is the page's single most structurally significant visual moment.

## Content Rhythm

Motion is applied sparingly and gently. The masonry portfolio grid is the primary animated element: images fade in as the section scrolls into view, with a possible very slight stagger through the grid items. The effect is a graceful appearance of a dense visual block rather than item-by-item revelation. All other sections — hero, section title, CTA, footer — appear without scroll-triggered animation. The page's motion vocabulary is minimal by design: motion appears once, at the most content-rich moment, to prevent a visual shock of simultaneous image loading.

## Footer Approach

The footer uses a dark near-black background that creates an immediate visual finality — the tone shift from the warm light page body to the dark footer is the page's "closing statement." Large centered wordmark at the top with a single social icon. Horizontal rule. Two-column sub-footer with legal text and nav links. No entrance animation. The footer does not animate; it simply arrives with the color change as its own announcement.

## Motion Principles

Motion on this site is functional and minimal. The logo marquee auto-scrolls at a steady, unhurried pace — the only element that moves continuously on its own clock. The portfolio grid fade-in is the only scroll-triggered reveal. Everything else is static. The page communicates its editorial confidence through restraint: no GSAP timeline orchestration, no scroll actors, no animated dividers. The architecture itself carries the visual interest — the composition-driven hero, the masonry grid, the dramatic footer inversion — without requiring motion to make the case.

## Video Placements

**1. Logo Marquee Strip — Enhanced auto-scroll with motion**
- Suggested file: `logo-marquee-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: A slow-motion close-up drift across a physical surface (clean desk, studio table) with brand/client artifacts partially visible — a printed sheet, a device screen, a physical object — shot from directly above; the camera drifts at the same pace as the marquee's auto-scroll; 8s loop; this video would serve as the background layer of the logo marquee section, giving the scrolling logos a spatial context that suggests the logos represent real collaborations rather than digital icons
- Duration: 8s loop
- Why video: The logo marquee strip in the hero is currently a text/image element on the page background. A very slow ambient background video in this position — one that reads as context rather than content — would deepen the credibility signal of the social proof strip by grounding it in physical studio space rather than floating it on a flat color.

**2. Masonry Grid Section — Ambient studio or process background**
- Suggested file: `portfolio-bg-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: An extreme close-up of a warm, barely-lit surface — textured paper, a linen fabric, a studio wall — with 1–2% motion; a living still photograph rather than video; 12s loop; used as the full section background behind the masonry grid of photographs; the video provides textural warmth to the page's off-white background in the section with the highest visual density; the motion is barely perceptible but enough that the section background reads as alive rather than flat
- Duration: 12s loop
- Why video: The masonry grid is the most content-dense section on the page. A near-static ambient video as the section background adds depth beneath the photographs and text cell without competing with them — it turns the background from a flat surface into a subtle material presence, consistent with the warm, tactile register of the `#f6f1f1` page color.
