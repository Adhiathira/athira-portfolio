# Sakari Sake — Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page does not load into content — it loads into an age-gate modal that occupies the full viewport. The modal requires interaction before the site is accessible; the page behind it is not visible. Once cleared, the hero arrives fully composed: dark or textured background, floating animated leaf elements already in motion, central product photography in position, headline visible. No entrance sequence for the hero itself — it is a live state on arrival.

## Hero Experience

The hero's primary motion is atmospheric rather than narrative. Decorative leaf elements float across the composition at multiple depth layers — foreground leaves at one speed, background elements at a slower drift — creating a sense of spatial depth without explicit parallax scroll triggering. The motion is continuous and ambient: the leaves do not stop, repeat on a seamless loop, and function as a living background. The product photography at center is static. As the user begins scrolling, the sinusoidal wave boundary at the bottom of the hero section translates upward at a scroll-proportional rate — the wave surface appears to rise and eventually consume the hero as the next section scrolls into view. This is the single most kinetically distinctive moment on the page: the transition is not a cut or a fade but a liquid shape movement.

## Section Transitions

The sinusoidal wave is the primary transition mechanism between light and dark sections. The wave — large amplitude, asymmetric, approximately 20–25% of viewport height — is rendered as a CSS or SVG clip shape at the boundary between sections and translates upward on scroll rather than oscillating in place. The effect reads as liquid: one section's background appears to flood over the bottom edge of the previous section as the wave shape rises. Same-register sections (light-to-light or dark-to-dark) are joined by straight horizontal edges with no divider — the wave only appears at light-dark or dark-light crossings. The full-width background video sections serve as their own transition type: a hard straight edge cut into a full-bleed cinematic environment before cutting back out.

## Content Rhythm

Motion is applied at two scales. At the section level: the wave translate and video sections provide large-scale temporal rhythm through the page. At the element level: the floating animated leaf motif recurs in hero and product showcase sections as a recurring ambient element — it does not follow scroll directly but loops continuously. Individual content elements (headlines, body text, product photography) appear to arrive without dramatic scroll-triggered reveal. The editorial emphasis is on the environmental motion — the leaves and wave — rather than choreographed element entrance sequences.

## Footer Approach

The footer returns to the light section register after the dark editorial sections. No distinctive animation or transition event marks the footer's arrival — it appears with a straight horizontal edge as the final section of the light-register content chain. The footer's presence is signaled by the register return, not by motion.

## Motion Principles

The motion vocabulary has two distinct registers. Ambient environmental motion runs continuously on its own clock, independent of scroll: the floating leaf elements loop at a gentle pace in the hero and product sections — a living atmospheric layer. Scroll-structural motion is tied directly to the scroll position: the sinusoidal wave translates at a 1:1 scroll rate, creating the liquid boundary effect. The full-width background video sections are a third register: editorial-cinematic, where the video runs on its own clock (not scroll-driven) and functions as a narrative pause in the page's journey. The total effect is a site that never feels static but also never feels agitated — the ambient motion is slow and sensory, the structural motion is decisive, and the video chapters are contemplative.

## Video Placements

**1. Hero Section — Animated floating leaf ambient loop**
- Suggested file: `hero-leaf-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: Multiple stylized botanical leaf elements floating in slow, gentle paths across a dark or deep-textured field — different sizes moving at different speeds and depths; foreground leaves slightly faster and larger, background leaves slow and translucent; 10–12s seamless loop; the motion is organic and unhurried, suggesting natural drift rather than animation
- Duration: 10–12s loop
- Why video: The floating leaf elements in the hero create the site's primary atmospheric identity. A static version of these elements would read as flat illustration. Video preserves the multi-layered depth and the sense that the composition is alive — that the product exists in a natural, living environment rather than a studio space.

**2. Editorial Narrative Section — Atmospheric historical footage chapter**
- Suggested file: `editorial-historical-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: Black-and-white archival-style footage of a traditional craft process — hands working, a vessel being filled, steam rising from a container, water moving — shot in a way that feels documentary and intimate rather than promotional; slow camera movement or static frame with subject motion only; 8–10s loop
- Duration: 8–10s loop
- Why video: The full-width background video sections function as cinematic chapter breaks in the page's narrative structure. A still image cannot replicate the temporal quality of craft-in-motion — the gentle movement communicates process, patience, and authenticity that is core to the brand's editorial voice.
