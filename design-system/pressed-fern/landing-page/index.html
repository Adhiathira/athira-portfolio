The Collection

Heirloom varieties
from every climate.

Each entry in our archive is the result of years of careful selection, cross-generational cultivation, and precise documentation. These are not curiosities. They are the living evidence that diversity is the source of resilience.

Solanum lycopersicum
Brandywine Pink

Irregular shouldered fruit of the deepest rose-pink. A tomato bred in the 1880s by the Amish for flavour above all else. Still unsurpassed in late-summer kitchens.

Heirloom · Open-pollinated
Phaseolus vulgaris
Dragon Tongue

A Dutch wax bean from 1885: pale-yellow pods striped with deep violet that cook to buttery green. Produces abundantly in poor soil and forgives the irregular gardener.

Open-pollinated · Pre-1900
Daucus carota
Cosmic Purple

A deep-violet-skinned carrot with an orange centre that bleeds like ink into the flesh when sliced. Descended from ancestral purple carrots from Afghanistan, rediscovered in the 1990s.

Heritage · Root Vegetable
Cucurbita pepo
Rouge Vif d'Étampes

The Cinderella pumpkin. Flat, deeply ribbed, brilliant burnt-orange. Grown in France since at least 1883. More art than vegetable — the perfect subject for botanical illustration and autumn kitchens.

French Heritage · Pre-1900
Lactuca sativa
Forellenschluss

Austrian trout lettuce — lime-green leaves dappled in burgundy freckles. Named by Viennese market gardeners in the 17th century for its resemblance to brook trout. Still perfect 400 years on.

Austrian Heritage · Pre-1700
Capsicum annuum
Fish Pepper

An African American heirloom from the Baltimore-Philadelphia corridor, documented in use before the American Civil War. Variegated white-and-green foliage, ivory fruit that matures to fiery red.

African-American Heritage
Cultivation Wisdom

Knowledge passed
hand to hand.

Every variety in our archive comes with growing notes assembled from cultivators across three generations. Not instructions: observations. The kind of knowledge written in soil, not spreadsheets.

01
Seed Selection

Selecting seed from the truest specimens — those that show the original character of the variety, not its adaptations to convenience.

02
Isolation & Purity

Maintaining genetic integrity through careful isolation distances and hand-pollination where needed. The work behind the seed that nobody sees.

03
Harvest & Ferment

Tomato seeds fermented 48–72 hours to remove germination-inhibiting gel. Bean seeds dried slowly on the vine. Each species has its own calendar of readiness.

04
Archive & Distribute

Seeds stored at 35–40°F in sealed glass with silica desiccant. Distributed annually to stewards for grow-outs that maintain living genetic material.

Become a Steward

The archive grows only
because stewards grow it.

Each steward receives an annual seed allocation from the archive — a small parcel of three to five varieties chosen for their region and growing history — on the condition that they save seed and return a portion to the library. The archive depends on this annual circulation.
