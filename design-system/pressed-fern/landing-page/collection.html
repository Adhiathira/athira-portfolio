The collection is not a catalog of what is easiest to grow. It is a record of what was worth saving. Each variety carries within it the accumulated observation of gardeners across centuries — their soil, their climate, their careful preference.

Solanum lycopersicum · Pre-1880
Mortgage Lifter

Developed in the 1930s by M.C. Byles of Logan, West Virginia. He crossed four of the largest tomatoes he could find over six years. Sold plants for $1 each until his mortgage was paid off. The fruit: massive, meaty, pink-red, sweet. Still the best large slicer in existence.

Grows 1.8–2.4m. Indeterminate. 80–90 days to maturity. Tolerates heat and drought.

Solanum lycopersicum
Green Zebra

Tom Wagner's 1983 creation: striped lime and amber, sharp acidic flavour. The first green tomato bred to be eaten ripe, not just pickled.

Modern Heirloom · 1983
Capsicum annuum
Pimento de Padrón

Galician pepper grown since the 17th century by the friars of Herbón monastery. Mild when small, occasionally searingly hot when large.

Spanish Heritage · Pre-1700
Brassica oleracea · Capitata
January King

An English winter cabbage of great antiquity. Blue-green outer leaves flush with burgundy in cold weather. Frost improves its flavour.

English Heritage · Pre-1867
Beta vulgaris · Cicla
Bright Lights Chard

Stalks of yellow, gold, orange, red, pink, and white — a botanical still-life in one patch. Mild and sweet, productive from June through November.

Modern Heirloom · 1990s
Cucumis sativus
Boothby's Blonde

A cucumber from the Boothby family farm in Livermore, Maine, grown since the 1800s. Cream-yellow skin, mild-flavoured, never bitter.

American Heritage · Pre-1900
Pisum sativum
Champion of England

A wrinkled marrowfat pea from 1843 — the prize-winning show pea of Victorian England. Still the sweetest shelling pea from open-pollinated seed.

Victorian Heritage · 1843
Allium porrum
Musselburgh

A Scottish leek first listed 1834. Fat, short-shanked, supremely cold-hardy. Still the standard by which all other winter leeks are judged.

Scottish Heritage · 1834
Brassica oleracea · Italica
De Cicco

Italian sprouting broccoli, introduced to America in 1890. After the main head is cut, it produces abundant side shoots for weeks.

Italian Heritage · 1890
