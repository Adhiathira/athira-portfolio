About

2017

Founded 2017

We Started
On A Lathe
And A Dare

Torn Press started in 2017 when Jess found a secondhand Neumann VMS-70 cutting lathe in a storage unit auction in Accrington. She paid £400 for it. It took 8 months to get it working. In that time, three more people joined: Callum (booking), Rhi (screen printing), and Dev (mastering). We cut our first lacquer in March 2018. It was not good. We cut another one. It was better.

We moved into the bus depot on Ordsall Lane in 2019. The pressing plant we'd been using for vinyl runs closed in 2020. We bought their equipment. Three hydraulic presses, a steam boiler, and more stampers than any four people should own.

89 releases later, the lathe is still running. So are we.

The Manifesto

Why We Still Press
Records In 2026

Streaming has made music infinitely available and nearly worthless to experience. You can listen to anything, anywhere, without commitment. The cost of this convenience is attention. Nobody listens to a stream the way they listen to a record.

A record requires a physical act: pulling it from a sleeve, placing it on a platter, dropping a needle. This act creates a relationship with what you're about to hear. You chose it. You handled it. The sound that comes out is the result of a physical interaction between plastic and metal. It costs something. Not much. Enough.

We press records because the music our bands make deserves to be heard that way. We press cassettes because not everyone has a turntable and tape is bulletproof. We print zines because the context of a release matters: who made it, where, under what circumstances, what it cost them.

We do not do this because streaming doesn't pay, although it doesn't. We do this because the format is the argument.
