Methodology

How Solum stays defensible.

Field protocols, standards alignment, and validation logic — built with the practitioner who has to sign the report.

The Approach

Built around the evidentiary standard, not convenience.

Soil investigation reports are submitted to regulators, used in legal proceedings, and inform land development decisions worth hundreds of millions of dollars. The practitioner who signs the report carries personal professional liability for its accuracy. Solum was built with that reality at the centre — not as an afterthought.

Every structural decision — from the way field forms are sequenced to the way laboratory results are matched to sample identifiers — was reviewed by a panel of practising environmental scientists and agronomists before it was finalised. The result is a platform where the default workflow produces a report that can withstand scrutiny without the practitioner having to manually check every output against the standard.

Principle 01

Capture precedes interpretation

Raw observations are locked once the site leaves the field. Interpretive text is generated from the captured data — not the other way around. No retrospective editing of field records.

Principle 02

Standards are not optional parameters

AS 4419, AS/NZS 2430, and applicable EPA method standards are embedded in the workflow — not selectable overlays. A user cannot complete a profile description that violates classification requirements.

Principle 03

Every decision is attributable

The practitioner who made each observation, each laboratory matching decision, and each interpretive judgement is named in the record. There is no anonymous data in a Solum project.

Standards Compliance

Pre-loaded for Australian and international field work.

Every classification system, test method reference, and reporting standard is maintained by the Solum science team. When a standard is updated, your report templates update with it — without any action on your part.

For international projects, Solum carries pre-loaded taxonomic equivalence mappings between the Australian system, USDA Soil Taxonomy, the FAO/WRB World Reference Base, and 40 country-specific national classifications.

Validation

How Solum catches errors before they reach the report.

Validation is built into every transition in the workflow — not applied as a final check. By the time a report is generated, every data point has passed through six distinct validation gates.

Gate 01

Field form completeness

Profile descriptions cannot be submitted with missing mandatory fields. The form guides the operator through any incomplete entries before sync is permitted.

Gate 02

Classification consistency

Horizon sequences are checked against classification key logic. A gleyed B horizon above an oxidised A horizon, for example, triggers a review flag before the profile is accepted.

Gate 03

Laboratory result matching

Every imported result is matched to a sample identifier. Unmatched or ambiguous results are quarantined — they cannot enter the report table until manually confirmed by the responsible practitioner.

Gate 04

Criteria comparison

Results are automatically compared against applicable screening criteria. Exceedances are flagged with their regulatory basis, magnitude, and recommended follow-up action before report generation begins.

Gate 05

Report completeness check

Before export, the report is checked against the selected standard's required section list. Missing sections, incomplete tables, and unresolved flags are listed with resolution instructions.

Gate 06

Practitioner sign-off

The responsible practitioner reviews an executive summary of all flags resolved, all data sources, and all standard references before applying their digital signature to the final document.
