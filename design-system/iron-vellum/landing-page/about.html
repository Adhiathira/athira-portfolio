Company

Built by people who had to sign the reports.

Origin

Ten years of assembling reports by hand.

Solum was founded by a team of environmental scientists and agronomists who spent the first decade of their careers producing soil investigation reports the same way everyone else did — field notebooks transferred to spreadsheets, laboratory results copy-pasted into Word documents, references to standards checked manually before each export.

The inefficiency was familiar enough to accept. What was harder to accept was the error rate: transposed sample identifiers, results filed to the wrong depth interval, classification decisions that could not be traced back to the field observation that supported them. None of these were catastrophic. But in a discipline where the practitioner's professional liability is attached to every page of the report, the margin for manual error should be zero.

We built Solum because no existing platform took the evidentiary standard seriously. They built for speed and convenience. We built for the practitioner who has to defend the report in front of a regulator, a lawyer, or a client who has just discovered contamination on land they acquired on the basis of a clean investigation.

"The margin for manual error in a soil investigation report should be zero. The industry accepted it wasn't. We disagreed."

— Dr. Annalie Forde, Co-founder
Values

How we make decisions.

I

Precision over convenience

Every workflow decision that sacrifices accuracy for speed is a decision against the practitioner. We will always choose the harder, more correct implementation.

II

The standard is not a preference

Classification and reporting standards exist because lives and livelihoods depend on them. Solum enforces them — not suggests them.

III

The practitioner is accountable, not the software

Solum is a tool for professionals. It does not make interpretive decisions on your behalf. It creates conditions where your decisions are defensible.

IV

Build for the hardest case

We design for the EIA under regulatory review, not the routine soil test. If the platform holds up in the hardest case, every easier case is handled with room to spare.
