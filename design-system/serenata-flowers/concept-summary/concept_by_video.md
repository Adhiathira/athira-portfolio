# Serenata Flowers — Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page loads immediately into a fully composed state: three-bar navigation visible at the top, a category header section with lifestyle photography on the left and editorial headline on the right. No entrance animation, no loading sequence. A promotional announcement banner may briefly appear and auto-dismiss at the very top of the viewport before the user engages. The experience begins in a static, ready state — the site trusts its clarity over its animation.

## Hero Experience

There is no conventional hero section. The top of the page begins with the category header section — a two-column editorial introduction (lifestyle photograph, descriptive headline and text) on a pastel green background. This section does not animate; it arrives fully composed. There is no full-viewport hero, no scroll-triggered entrance, and no ambient background motion. The emotional register is set through color (the pastel green background, the editorial typography) rather than through motion or spectacle.

## Section Transitions

All section transitions are hard straight-edge horizontal cuts. The primary mechanism is background color change: white product grid → pastel green category header → white grid → beige value props strip → white testimonials → grey footer. No animated dividers, no wave shapes, no fades between sections. The color alternation is the entire transition vocabulary. The visual rhythm of the page is pacing through color blocks, not motion events.

## Content Rhythm

Motion is minimal and functional. The only significant animation is lazy-loading: product images in the grid fade in gently with a slight upward drift as they scroll into the viewport. This is performance-driven rather than design-driven — it serves loading efficiency while providing a low-key entrance signal. There is no stagger, no orchestrated sequence, no headline-then-content choreography. All sections arrive as static composed states; the only motion is at the individual product card level during initial viewport entry.

## Footer Approach

The footer arrives with a hard straight edge and a shift to a medium grey background — the same color-blocking mechanism used throughout the page, applied as a final close. Multi-column utility links, social icons, and payment logos arrange into a dense grid. No entrance animation. The footer announces itself through the color change, not through motion.

## Motion Principles

The motion vocabulary is purely functional and entirely deferential to content. The lazy-load image appearance is the sole animated event, and it occurs at the product card level rather than at the section level. There is no GSAP, no ScrollTrigger orchestration, no scroll actors, and no ambient animation of any kind. The design philosophy is performance and clarity — every visible interaction pattern is in service of the transactional journey, and animation is not used to communicate brand value. The brand's emotional register is established through typography (the script wordmark), color (the forest green and pastel green palette), and photography (vibrant studio shots) rather than through motion.

## Video Placements

**1. Category Header Section — Lifestyle atmospheric loop**
- Suggested file: `category-header-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: A slow, barely-moving close-up of fresh flowers or foliage in soft natural light — petals, leaves, or stems with gentle movement from breath or a slight breeze; 8–10s seamless loop; the motion is imperceptible in still form but reads as alive on screen; the color register should align with the light pastel green tones of the section background; shot from slightly above or at an intimate close distance
- Duration: 8–10s loop
- Why video: The category header section is the editorial introduction to the commerce experience — it sets the tone before the product grid. A near-still ambient video of live botanical material gives the header section a textural warmth and biological aliveness that a static product photograph cannot provide. It communicates that the product is living and perishable — a quality central to the gifting context.

**2. Product Detail Page — Contextual gift ambient**
- Suggested file: `product-detail-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: A slow-motion close-up of a completed arrangement being set down on a surface — hands placing a wrapped bouquet onto a wooden table, flowers slightly settling, tissue paper rustling; 6–8s loop; warm interior light; the motion is gentle and deliberate, suggesting care and intentionality in the giving
- Duration: 6–8s loop
- Why video: The product detail page is the conversion moment — the page where the user decides whether to purchase. An ambient video that shows the product in its natural gifting context (being presented, being received, being placed) gives the page an emotional charge that a static studio shot cannot replicate. It closes the gap between seeing the product and imagining giving it.
