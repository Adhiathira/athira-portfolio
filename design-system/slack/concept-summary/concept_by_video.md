# Slack — Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page loads into the dark hero at full viewport height: the centered headline is already readable, the background atmospheric video is already playing, and the 3D scroll actor is already rotating slowly in the background plane behind the text. No entrance sequence, no element staging. The nav is fully transparent over the dark background. The page is live on arrival — the user's first impression is of a scene that has been running, not one that begins for their benefit.

## Hero Experience

The hero runs two simultaneous motion layers independent of scroll. Background: a full-viewport atmospheric video — dark-toned, slow, textured — providing ambient depth. Mid-ground: the 3D geometric scroll actor rotates continuously on its own axis, its iridescent metallic surface catching simulated light. These two layers create a sense of spatial depth (video behind, 3D object mid, text foreground) without explicit user interaction. A cursor-parallax effect may apply between the text and the 3D object, giving the depth a tactile responsiveness. When the user begins scrolling, the wide shallow concave arc divider at the hero's base rises into view — a static curve that creates a soft organic threshold between the dark hero and the first white section.

## Section Transitions

Three transition types define the page's rhythm. The arc divider at the hero's base: a single shallow concave curve scooping upward that creates a soft liquid-like boundary between dark and light registers. Straight horizontal hard cuts between all light-register sections: the default transition in the functional content zone. The animated sinusoidal wave at the card track section's bottom: approximately 2.5 peaks across the viewport width, continuously undulating in slow vertical oscillation independent of scroll — this wave is the page's most kinetically distinctive boundary event, and it marks the exit from the content-dense horizontal track into the section below.

## Content Rhythm

Scroll-triggered reveals are consistent and understated in the feature sections: elements fade in with a short upward slide as they enter the viewport. In list-like structures (feature grids, card groups), individual items animate in with a slight stagger cascade. These reveals are fast and non-blocking — they do not pause the user's scroll momentum. The horizontal card track section introduces a different motion register: vertical scroll is scroll-jacked to horizontal card advancement, with the cards progressing smoothly through the track. A subtle scrollbar at the track's bottom indicates position. Once the track is exhausted, normal vertical scroll resumes.

## Footer Approach

The footer arrives via a straight horizontal edge from the final content section. No distinctive entrance animation or transition event. The footer closes the page on a consistent note — its motion vocabulary (fade-in reveals) matches the light content sections rather than the dark hero's cinematic register.

## Motion Principles

Three motion registers operate simultaneously across the page. The persistent 3D scroll actor defines the deep background layer: it rotates continuously on its own clock, disappears behind solid-background sections as the page scrolls over it, and reappears in subsequent dark sections — a single continuous thread running the depth of the page. The atmospheric hero video is ambient and temporal, providing surface richness to the dark sections without narrative content. Scroll-driven reveals (fade + slide-up) define the foreground content layer in the feature sections. The animated wave divider is a structural boundary element — continuous, slow oscillation at the section seam. The overall principle is layered temporal independence: each motion layer runs on its own clock, and the user's scroll manages their exposure to each layer rather than triggering choreography.

## Video Placements

**1. Hero Section Background — Dark atmospheric texture loop**
- Suggested file: `hero-bg-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: A slow-moving abstract dark texture — shifting light patterns on a near-black surface, subtle volumetric haze moving gently, or microscopic surface details in very low light; the motion is barely perceptible from second to second but reads as clearly alive over a full loop; 12s loop; the tone is deep charcoal with occasional cool blue or warm amber light glints matching the iridescent quality of the 3D scroll actor
- Duration: 12s loop
- Why video: The hero background video establishes the page's cinematic register immediately. A static dark background would make the 3D object feel like it's floating in an empty void; the atmospheric video gives it a context — it inhabits a particular material world. The video's slow movement creates depth at the back of the scene without competing with the 3D object or the headline.

**2. Feature Section — Product environment ambient**
- Suggested file: `product-context-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: A slow pull-back or drift across a modern collaborative workspace — monitors showing a communication interface, natural light, a cup of coffee, hands near a keyboard but not typing; the scene is quiet and focused, suggesting productive presence rather than performed activity; 8s loop; warm interior tones contrasting with the dark hero register
- Duration: 8s loop
- Why video: The split media-text feature sections use static UI mockups to demonstrate capability. An ambient background video in one of these sections — placed behind a product screenshot — would give the product claim an environmental context: this is what the interface looks like when someone is actually using it, not when it's posed for a screenshot. Video bridges the gap between the product's visual design and its lived experience.
