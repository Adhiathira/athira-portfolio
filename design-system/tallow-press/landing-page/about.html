The Studio

Guldmark Press

Guldmark Press was founded in 2009 by Erik Guldmark in a railway arch in Bermondsey. The press began with a single Chandler & Price platen press salvaged from a closed Somerset printing house, four cases of 12pt Bembo, and a determination to make books that felt different in the hand.

Since then the workshop has grown to four presses, a Monotype caster, and a substantial collection of foundry and wood type accumulated over fifteen years of attending printer's fairs and estate sales across Britain and Northern Europe.

We produce editions in quantities that force us to know each print individually. No sheet leaves the workshop unsigned and inspected. When an edition is exhausted, it is gone — there are no reprint runs, no digital facsimiles.

What we believe

Press principles

Impression, not reproduction

A printed sheet should carry the physical evidence of its making. The deboss of type into paper, the slight ink squash at letter edges — these are not imperfections. They are the proof of contact.

Edition over scale

Editions of 40 to 100 copies allow us to maintain quality at every stage. Larger runs introduce mechanical compromises. We prefer the constraint of the small edition.

Type as material

Metal type has weight, surface, and wear. Wood type has grain. We treat both as materials, not merely as vehicles for letterforms — the specificity of a particular piece of type is part of the design.

Paper as collaborator

We select paper for each edition. Stock is not a commodity here. A slightly toothy surface, a warm white, a particular laid texture — these choices shape the finished piece as much as type selection does.
