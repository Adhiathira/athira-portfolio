How we work

The Press,
in steps

Hot-metal composition, hand-inking, cylinder pressure — a sequence unchanged since the nineteenth century.

Design & typography

Every Guldmark piece begins with type design. We draw letterforms by hand or adapt existing foundry designs. Spacing, leading, and column width are calculated for the press sheet, not the screen. No digital proof is a substitute for a printed pull.

Hot-metal composition

Body text is set on a Monotype caster or by hand from our type cases. Display lines are set in wood type — some pieces dating to the 1880s. The forme is locked into a steel chase, checked for evenness, then placed on the press bed.

Inking & makeready

Ink is mixed by weight from oil-based pigment concentrates. Colour is matched by eye against the paper, not a Pantone fan. Makeready — the adjustment of impression depth and packing — can take several hours for a two-colour piece.

Impression

Paper is fed by hand, one sheet at a time. The press exerts physical force — type presses into the fibres, leaving a deboss that catches light and shadow. No two pulls are identical. Each edition is hand-numbered and inspected before leaving the workshop.

Want this for your project?

We take on a small number of commissions each year — books, stationery, art editions, and institutional print work.
