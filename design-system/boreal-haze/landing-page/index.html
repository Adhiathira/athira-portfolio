How It Works

Three Data Streams.
One Forecast.

01
NOAA Satellite

Satellite Uplink

NOAA ACE satellite feeds real-time solar wind velocity and density, providing the earliest warning signal of incoming geomagnetic disturbances — 30 to 60 minutes before they reach Earth.

02
Ground Network

Magnetometer Network

48 ground stations across Norway, Iceland, Canada, and Alaska sample the geomagnetic field every 60 seconds. This dense auroral-zone coverage resolves fine-scale structure that satellite data alone cannot capture.

03
ML Algorithm

Predictive Algorithm

Spectra's forecast engine combines 27-day solar rotation models with live solar wind data to predict Kp 1–3 hours ahead. Accuracy degrades gracefully beyond 3 hours — we tell you what we know, and when we're uncertain.

The Science

Aurora Is Charged
Particle Physics

The solar wind — a continuous stream of charged particles from the Sun — travels outward at hundreds of kilometres per second. When it reaches Earth's magnetosphere, it interacts with the magnetic field in ways that depend critically on the orientation of the interplanetary magnetic field (IMF). A southward-pointing IMF drives magnetic reconnection, allowing solar energy to pour into the polar regions.

"400 km/s — average solar wind velocity that triggers G1 conditions and sets the auroral oval glowing."

In the upper atmosphere, electrons accelerate downward along field lines and collide with oxygen and nitrogen atoms. The colour of the aurora depends on which atoms are excited and at what altitude: green from oxygen at 100–150 km, red from oxygen above 200 km, violet and blue from nitrogen at lower altitudes. Spectra's predictive model traces this chain from solar source to optical emission.
