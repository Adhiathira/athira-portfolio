# Syan Tokyo — Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page loads into a fully composed state: the fixed nav is already visible at the top with all its elements in position, and the first content cluster of the asymmetric showcase is already on screen. No entrance animation, no loading sequence, no stagger on first load. The custom circular dot cursor appears immediately, replacing the default system cursor. The page invites scroll without announcing it.

## Hero Experience

There is no conventional hero section. The showcase pages begin directly with the first content cluster of the asymmetric editorial layout — an image and its paired text block, already composed in their final positions. The emotional impact is immediate and visual: the photography and the space around it together constitute the opening statement. There is no ambient background motion, no autoplay video, and no parallax layering. The opening is static, and the stillness is the design's first act of confidence.

## Section Transitions

Section transitions on this site are defined by structural shifts rather than visual events. Within a single showcase page, the "transition" between content clusters is the act of scrolling through generous whitespace — the negative space between clusters is itself the transition, a typographic pause before the next content pair arrives. Between pages, the entire layout paradigm changes: the asymmetric showcase gives way to the two-column staff grid, or to the minimal centered CTA. These structural shifts are the site's primary transition vocabulary.

## Content Rhythm

Scroll reveals are consistent and understated: as content items enter the viewport, they execute a brief fade combined with a gentle upward slide. The effect is uniform across all content types — images, text blocks, staff portraits — and applies to each item in sequence as it crosses the viewport threshold. The stagger is based on scroll position rather than a timed sequence. The result is a measured, calm revelation of content that matches the site's overall pace rather than announcing itself as a designed moment.

## Footer Approach

Each page closes with the minimal centered CTA section before the footer: a headline, a short text block, and the pill-shaped CTA button centered in the viewport. This terminal section has a simple fade reveal. The footer below it is a utility block — consistent with the same pure-white background and black text as the rest of the site. No color change, no visual announcement. The page closes as quietly as it opened.

## Motion Principles

Motion is used only where it aids the reading experience and nowhere else. The scroll-reveal fade-and-slide is the site's entire motion vocabulary — uniform in duration, uniform in easing, applied consistently to every content element across every page. There is no ambient animation, no continuous background motion, no interactive parallax, no GSAP orchestration of complex sequences. The custom cursor dot is the only persistent moving element, and it moves only with the user's hand. The motion philosophy is one of pure content deference: the work on screen is the subject, and motion exists only to introduce it gracefully.

## Video Placements

**1. Showcase Page — Atmospheric studio ambient background**
- Suggested file: `studio-ambient-bg.mp4`
- Subject: An extremely slow, barely-moving overhead shot of a clean minimal studio surface — white paper, a few pencils at rest, perhaps a folded magazine or an open book — in very even, flat, cool natural light; nearly motionless, with barely perceptible motion in the paper or a slight shadow variation; 15s loop; the video reads as a living white texture behind the main content rather than a distinct scene
- Duration: 15s loop
- Why video: The showcase page's pure white background is a design decision that prioritizes the photography over its environment. A very subtle atmospheric background video — so slow and white-toned as to be nearly invisible — could give the white ground a material presence rather than a digital void, while maintaining the design's extreme restraint. The challenge of this site is that any background video must be so subtle as to be noticed only subliminally. This placement earns its existence only if the video is essentially indistinguishable from a still at a glance.

**2. Staff Page — Ambient portrait context**
- Suggested file: `studio-portrait-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: An extremely slow tracking shot across a neutral surface or a gently out-of-focus architectural background — a white wall, a clean table, a studio corner — creating a spatial context for the portrait grid; minimal motion, warm-neutral tone consistent with the photography styling of the headshots; 10s loop
- Duration: 10s loop
- Why video: The staff page uses a strict two-column portrait grid with uniform photography. A near-still ambient background video could give the grid section a spatial context — suggesting a physical place where these people work — without competing with the portraits themselves. As with the showcase video, restraint is the primary requirement: the video must defer to the photography it contextualizes.
