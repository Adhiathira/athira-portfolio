The Studio

Three hands. One wheel. Forty years.

Roundwork Studio is a family workshop in San Marcos Tlapazola, a small village 45 minutes east of Oaxaca City.

Elena and Miguel Reyes

Elena learned from her grandmother, carrying forward the traditional Zapotec pottery traditions of the Valles Centrales — a practice rooted in clay, patience, and the particular rhythm of hand-coiled form.

Miguel handles the kiln work, sourcing their clay directly from the artisan cooperative in Miahuatlán. He manages firing temperatures by feel as much as by instrument, a skill accumulated across three decades of open-flame kilns.

Their daughter Catalina joined the studio in 2019 and brought the rounded, modernist forms that define the current collection — the softer silhouettes, the deeper foot rings, the belly proportions that make each piece want to sit in the hand.

Why We Make

Against the straight line

Every piece from the workshop is a rejection of industrial uniformity. Machine-pressed ceramics achieve perfect symmetry — and in doing so, lose the record of the maker's hand. The slight oval in a Roundwork bowl is not an error. It is evidence. It tells you that someone touched this, shaped it, coaxed it from a lump of earth into something that holds water and warmth. Imperfection, here, is the signature of authenticity.

Function follows rounded form. The haptic quality of a piece matters as much as its visual presence — perhaps more. An object that is difficult to hold, awkward to pour from, or unpleasant against the lip has failed its brief regardless of how beautiful it photographs. Roundwork pieces are designed to be used daily, to accumulate patina, to become more themselves over years of handling. The glaze is not a surface; it is a finish for living.

"A bowl that doesn't want to be held is a failure, regardless of how it looks."

How We Work

The studio principles

Slow production

We cap output at forty pieces per week. This is a hard limit — not a marketing position. Rushing a throwing session produces work we would not want in the world. We do not rush.

Fair wages

All studio wages are publicly listed on this website. Every person who contributes to a piece — from clay preparation to final glazing — earns a wage we would be comfortable printing on the label.

Zero waste glaze

One hundred percent of glaze scraps are reclaimed and recycled back into the glaze cycle. The process was developed in partnership with a materials scientist at the Universidad Tecnológica de los Valles Centrales.
