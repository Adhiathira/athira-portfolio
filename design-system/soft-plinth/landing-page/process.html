The Making

Slow by design.

We don't rush. The clay tells us when it's ready.

How We Work

Five steps. No shortcuts.

  1. 1

    Clay Selection

    Sourced from Oaxacan highlands cooperatives, our clay carries natural mineral deposits — iron oxides and silicates unique to the Sierra Norte. We select by hand, rejecting any batch that doesn't feel right between the fingers.

  2. 2

    Wedging & Centering

    Twenty minutes per piece, minimum. Wedging removes air pockets and aligns the clay's particle structure. Centering on the wheel brings the mass into perfect rotational balance — without this, nothing that follows will hold.

  3. 3

    Throwing

    Wheel-thrown at 180 RPM, walls thinned to 5mm. Every Roundwork piece is opened from the center out — no moulds, no templates. The hands are the only tools that touch the form at this stage. Slight variations between pieces are proof of presence.

  4. 4

    Bisque Firing

    980°C over 8 hours — a slow, deliberate climb. The kiln is loaded piece by piece, spacing calculated so heat can circulate evenly. We never rush the cool-down. Opening a kiln too early has broken more beautiful work than any mistake at the wheel.

  5. 5

    Glaze & Final Fire

    1260°C, twice-fired stoneware. Each piece is hand-dipped, brushed, or poured depending on the form. The final firing vitrifies the clay body completely — the result is dense, non-porous, and designed to last a lifetime of daily use.

The Clay

The clay chooses the form

Every Roundwork piece begins with earth from the Oaxacan highlands — a clay body rich in iron oxides, manganese, and naturally occurring silicates. This mineral composition gives our work its distinctive colour range: deep terracotta in the raw, shifting toward warm blacks and earth tones after high-fire reduction.

The physics of the material drive every design decision. Our clay body shrinks approximately 13% through the full firing cycle — so a bowl thrown at 28cm will finish at just under 25cm. We account for this from the first touch on the wheel. There is no correcting for shrinkage after throwing; the form must be designed with its final dimensions in mind from the start.

We source exclusively from cooperative producers in the Sierra Norte. The clay is extracted by hand, dried in open air, and slaked down slowly before it reaches us. None of it is bagged, blended, or pre-processed. We receive it rough and do the preparation ourselves — it takes longer, but the result behaves differently under the hands than commercial clay bodies. More responsive. More honest.
