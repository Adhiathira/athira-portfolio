# Soft Plinth — Design Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page begins in total darkness. On load, the viewport is pure midnight — no elements visible, no animation queued. After a 200ms hold, the aurora gradient mesh begins to materialize behind the hero container: three radial gradient pools fade from opacity 0 over 800ms, drifting fractionally as they emerge, the color arriving before any type or structure. Then the hero text initiates its glow-reveal sequence: each word starts at opacity 0 with text-shadow at 0 radius and 0 spread, and over 1200ms the text-shadow grows outward — teal emission bleeding into the dark — before the character opacity follows at 900ms. The opening communicates the system's core physics: light emerges from void, not the reverse. The user experiences arrival into an atmosphere rather than a page loading into a browser frame.

## Hero Experience

The hero section runs an animated gradient mesh built from three overlapping radial gradients positioned at different canvas coordinates, each drifting on a separate CSS keyframe cycle of 12s, 16s, and 20s with offset start delays. The gradients are set to mix-blend-mode: screen against the midnight field, producing the aurora borealis chromatic band effect — teal bleeding into violet where gradient pools overlap, isolated lime accents where a single high-frequency gradient pool is uncontested. No static screenshot can replicate this section: the aurora effect is definitionally temporal, dependent on the slow drift of color mass across a dark field. The hero headline sits above the gradient mesh at z-index 10, its text-shadow cycling through a 4s auroraGlow keyframe that modulates the teal emission radius between 8px and 20px — the text itself appears to pulse with the aurora rhythm beneath it.

**Video background:** `aurora-timelapse.mp4` — 15s loop of real aurora borealis time-lapse footage filmed from directly below, wide-angle lens, deep dark sky with electric green and violet bands shifting laterally across the frame. The footage plays at 0.6× speed in the browser to extend the loop. Why static gradient cannot substitute: the aurora's organic irregularity — the way color bands accelerate, pause, and fold back — is produced by charged particle physics, not mathematical easing curves. A CSS gradient mesh approximates the structure; the video delivers the truth of it.

## Section Transitions

Section boundaries are defined by aurora wave SVG dividers: full-width SVG elements containing a single luminous curved path rendered with stroke rather than fill. The path describes a shallow sinusoidal wave spanning the viewport width. On scroll approach — triggered at 80% of the divider's entry point — a `stroke-dashoffset` animation fires: the path draws itself left to right over 1400ms using a `liquid-emergence` easing of cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1). The stroke color is the next section's primary accent, and the stroke carries a filter: drop-shadow in the same color at 8px blur — the SVG line appears to glow as it draws. The cumulative effect across a full page scroll is of the user crossing a series of luminous thresholds, each announcing the next atmospheric zone. No section boundary is a flat rule; each is an event.

## Content Rhythm

Content elements enter the viewport via GSAP ScrollTrigger using the `cosmicReveal` animation: clip-path sweeps left to right from `inset(0 100% 0 0)` to `inset(0 0% 0 0)` over 900ms with cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1) easing, revealing content as though the aurora were uncovering it. Simultaneously, an `auroraGlow` text-shadow animation fires on all section headlines — the glow grows from 0 radius to its resting value over 600ms, delayed 200ms behind the clip-path start. Sibling elements stagger at 80ms intervals. Images enter with a two-phase animation: the clip-path reveal at full speed, followed by a 400ms bloom from filter: blur(6px) saturate(0.4) to crisp and fully saturated — as though the image is being tuned into focus from the atmospheric haze. The entire page rhythm communicates slow atmospheric emergence rather than the pop-in convention of most scroll animation systems.

## Footer Approach

The footer rises into view as a self-contained aurora environment: its background runs the full spectral gradient mesh at increased luminance compared to body sections, with teal, violet, and midnight in a three-stop radial gradient centered on the top edge. As the footer scrolls into the viewport, an aurora teal glow activates along its top edge — a 1px border-top that carries a box-shadow: 0 -8px 32px 0 rgba(0,229,200,0.35), growing from 0 spread to full intensity over 600ms with liquid-emergence easing. Internal footer elements stagger in at 60ms intervals using the cosmicReveal clip-path sweep. The aurora shimmer across gradient background is a separate keyframe running on a 12s cycle — the gradient mesh behind footer content drifts slowly, keeping the section alive as the user reads. The footer reads as both terminus and signal: the page ends, but the light persists.

## Motion Principles

Soft Plinth's timing philosophy is **liquid emergence** — transitions move with the physics of something materializing from atmospheric haze, not popping in from a defined off-screen position. The governing easing is cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1), chosen because it decelerates sharply at the end of its curve — the element arrives fully but gently, without bounce or overshoot. This easing matches the visual logic of aurora: color bands don't spring into place; they drift and arrive. Duration ranges span 800ms for micro-reveals (individual text elements, small UI components) to 2200ms for full-section transitions and gradient mesh drift. Glow effects (text-shadow animations, box-shadow blooms, SVG stroke glows) run at longer cycles of 8–20s — imperceptibly slow on human timescales but continuously changing, so the page never appears static even when the user is not scrolling. The sum of these principles produces a temporal experience calibrated to atmospheric observation rather than interaction efficiency.

## Video Placements

### 1. Hero — `aurora-timelapse.mp4`
**Role:** Full-bleed hero background, playing beneath the gradient mesh overlay
**Motion:** Real aurora borealis time-lapse from ground level — wide sky frame, deep midnight field, electric green and violet bands shifting, folding, and dissipating across the full frame. Played at 0.6× browser speed for extension. 15s loop with 2s crossfade at tail.
**Why video:** The CSS gradient mesh in the hero approximates the aurora's structure and color logic. The time-lapse delivers what no mathematical animation can: the organic irregularity of charged-particle physics — bands that accelerate unpredictably, fold back on themselves, pulse at variable intensity. This gap between approximation and source is precisely why video is required: the aesthetic premise of the entire system is grounded in a real natural phenomenon, and the hero is the moment to make that premise visible.

### 2. Services / Features — `gradient-refraction.mp4`
**Role:** Mid-page features or services section; plays as contained section background or within a featured card
**Motion:** Macro footage of light refracting through a glass prism in a dark studio environment — aurora-spectrum color bands (green, teal, violet, lime) shooting across a black matte surface. 8s loop. Camera locked; only the refracted light moves.
**Why video:** This placement literalizes the gradient glow aesthetic as a physical phenomenon rather than a design convention. Visitors encountering the section understand, without explanation, that the system's spectral palette has a real-world referent — light physics, not arbitrary color selection. The darkness of the prism footage matches the page's midnight field exactly; the refracted bands appear continuous with the CSS gradient mesh rather than inserted from outside the system.

### 3. About / Studio — `dark-water-surface.mp4`
**Role:** About section or studio identity section; full-bleed background at reduced opacity (0.4) with gradient overlay maintaining legibility
**Motion:** Slow-motion surface of dark water — 120fps filmed, played at 0.25× browser speed — with bioluminescent particles drifting through frame. Teal and violet glints catch and release. No strong light sources; only particle emission. 10s loop.
**Why video:** The darkness-plus-luminosity relationship in this footage is the visual grammar of the entire system expressed as biology rather than physics. Where the hero footage shows light at atmospheric scale and the refraction footage shows it at optical scale, the dark water footage shows it at organismic scale — light produced by living things in void. This progression (atmospheric, optical, biological) gives the page's video narrative an implicit argument: the aurora glow aesthetic is not a design trend but a pattern that recurs across scales of nature. The about section is the appropriate placement for this claim, as it is conventionally the space where a brand articulates its foundational values.
