# Soft Plinth — Design Concept by Image

## Tactile Inflated Geometry

Soft Plinth is a Claymorphism design system built around the physicality of molded clay — the sense that every interface element has been sculpted by hand, inflated from within, and left with the warmth of material touch. The foundational visual premise is tactile inflated geometry: shapes that bulge subtly at their centers, cast tinted shadows that match their own surface color rather than a neutral grey, and present highlight gradients along their upper edges as though light is catching the raised ridge of a freshly molded form. Unlike glassmorphism, which dematerializes surfaces into translucency, or neumorphism, which carves depth into a uniform plane, Claymorphism asserts mass — objects have weight, roundness, and the haptic suggestion that pressing them would yield.

## Clay-Like Materiality

The system's surface language is governed by clay-like materiality: every component reads as something made rather than rendered. Cards carry a minimum 24px border-radius expanding to 32–40px on larger containers, producing the rounded-rectangular silhouette of a pinched clay tablet. The color palette is deliberately muted and pastel — not bright primaries but the slightly-desaturated tones of dried ceramic glaze: soft terracotta, sage, lavender, peach, sky. These colors read as warm rather than saturated, grounded rather than neon. The background field is an off-white or warm cream — never pure white — creating the impression of a clay-dusted work surface onto which components have been placed rather than a screen into which they have been projected.

## Haptic Depth Through Colored Tinted Shadows

The shadow system inverts the convention of neutral grey drop-shadows. Every component in Soft Plinth carries haptic depth through colored tinted shadows: the shadow beneath each card or button is tinted to the element's own surface color at 30–40% opacity, offset downward by 12–20px, blurred at 30–50px radius. A lavender card casts a lavender shadow. A peach button casts a peach shadow. The effect produces the appearance of chromatic light passing through a translucent clay body and pooling beneath it — the object glows downward into its surface. Secondary inset shadows along the bottom and right interior edges reinforce the rounded, convex form — the card appears to curve away from the viewer at its edges, highest at center. This two-shadow system (outer tinted, inner inset) is the system's primary depth mechanism, replacing the z-elevation token system of flat or material design.

## Spring-Based Elastic Motion

Motion in Soft Plinth is spring-based elastic motion — every interactive event produces a micro-physical response that communicates squish and rebound. Button press: scale(0.93) over 120ms on mousedown, spring-releasing to scale(1.0) over 300ms via cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1) — the overshoot of the spring easing produces the characteristic bounce-back of compressed clay. Hover: scale(1.04) with shadow expansion over 300ms — the element rises slightly as though pressure is being applied from below. Card entry on scroll: GSAP elastic.out(1, 0.4) over 800ms, each card overshooting its resting y-position by approximately 8px before settling. The cumulative experience of navigating the page is of handling soft objects that respond to touch with material memory — they compress, release, and return to their form with the physics of clay rather than the instant snap of digital geometry.

## Soft Sculptural Surfaces

Section backgrounds in Soft Plinth are not uniform fields but soft sculptural surfaces: gentle radial gradients centered off-axis that create a subtle convex impression in the background plane itself, as though the viewport were looking at the slightly curved surface of a large clay slab. Section differentiation is achieved through hue shift within the pastel register — cream to blush to sage to sky — rather than through hard color contrast. Section boundaries are not ruled lines but organic SVG wave paths: sinusoidal curves rendered with a 3px stroke in the next section's accent color, carrying a matching drop-shadow at 4px blur. The wave paths draw themselves on scroll using stroke-dashoffset animation over 1000ms, producing the sense of a line being etched into a soft surface. The overall page reads as a continuously textured field rather than a stack of discrete rectangles.

## Rounded Moldable Forms and Color Energy

The type system in Soft Plinth is chosen to complement the rounded morphology of the components. Display type uses a typeface with natural optical curvature — letterforms that echo the rounded interior corners of the clay cards rather than the sharp geometry of sans-serif construction. Body copy uses a humanist sans that reads warm rather than technical. Letter-spacing is kept tight — 0 to 0.02em — preventing the airiness that would conflict with the system's material density. Color is used as energy within the palette: call-to-action elements carry the highest saturation value in the pastel range, distinguishing interactive affordances from decorative surfaces without departing from the system's muted register. Pull-quotes and badges use contrasting pastel hues — a sage badge on a lavender card, a peach quote on a cream field — creating visual rhythm through color rotation within a single tonal family. The system's expressive range is wide within its constraints: dozens of combinations feel native, none feel discordant, because every hue shares the same clay-glaze saturation level and warm undertone.
