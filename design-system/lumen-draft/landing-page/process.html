How we make

From earth to architecture

A complete account of how Foramen Studio works — from sourcing raw materials in the Alentejo to the final installation on site.

The four phases

A deliberate sequence

Each phase is necessary. None can be shortened. The time built into each stage is not inefficiency — it is the work.

01 Earth & form
Sourcing and throwing We source a high-iron stoneware clay body from Barrancos, in the eastern Alentejo — a region whose geology produces a clay with exceptional plasticity and a rich mineral character that survives high-fire conditions. All forms are thrown on the wheel or hand-built to specification. We do not use casting or industrial moulding. The decision to hand-build is not romantic — it is technical: cast forms have uniform wall thickness, which transmits heat differently and produces glaze results that differ systematically from thrown work.
→ assets/process-phase-throwing.jpeg
02 Glaze development
Material formulation Our glazes are compounded in-studio from raw materials: Cornish stone, silica, calcium carbonate, red iron oxide, wood ash from our kiln. For each commission, we develop a bespoke palette — typically two to four complementary glazes calibrated to the space's material language: the stone of the floor, the pigment in the plaster, the quality of light at different hours. Glaze development for a major commission takes four to six weeks of testing and iteration.
→ assets/process-phase-glaze.jpeg
03 The firing
1280°C — where intention ends We fire in a 120-litre electric kiln to cone 10 — 1280°C. The firing cycle takes sixteen hours. At peak temperature, iron glazes reduce and bloom; crystalline formations develop in the slower-cooling ash glazes. This is the phase of the process over which we have the least control, and the most experience. The kiln does not execute the glaze — it interprets it. No two firings produce identical results from the same recipe. This variance is not a defect; it is the signature of the material.
→ assets/process-phase-kiln.jpeg
04 Installation
Technical coordination For architectural commissions — floor tile, wall cladding, custom installation — we provide full technical support through the installation phase. This includes: calibrated piece selection to ensure visual consistency across large areas; grout specification matched to the glaze palette; and on-site consultation during the tiling process. We work directly with the project architect or contractor. Installation for a standard floor commission (up to 40 m²) takes two to three days.
→ assets/process-phase-installation.jpeg
Our palette

Six tones, one earth

Every glaze we use derives from the same geological source — the iron-rich clay of the Alentejo plateau, modified through mineral addition and firing temperature. The tonal range moves from raw sienna to deep slate, from pale bone to the near-black of fully oxidised iron.

Custom glaze development is available for all architectural commissions. Sample tiles are produced at cost and delivered within three weeks of commission agreement.

The space

Alfama workshop

Our workshop occupies a double-height converted warehouse three streets above the Fado district. The building's original terracotta tile floor — installed in 1923 — remains in situ. We did not replace it. It is both floor and archive.
