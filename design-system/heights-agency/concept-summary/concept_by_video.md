# Heights Agency — Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page loads fully formed. The dark navy topbar is present at full opacity from the first frame, followed immediately by the static navigation bar and the hero split. No entrance animation, no loading sequence, no choreography. The visitor arrives to a complete, static composition. The browser renders the page; no JavaScript motion layer activates on load.

## Hero Experience

The hero split is entirely static. The muted blue-gray left panel — containing the headline, body copy, and CTA button — does not animate. The lifestyle photograph on the right does not load progressively or fade in. The yellow CTA button in the topbar does not pulse or draw attention. The hero simply exists. There is no motion designed into the above-fold composition.

## Section Transitions

Sections appear as the user scrolls without any scroll-triggered reveals. There is no fade-in, no slide-up, no stagger. Elements are present in the DOM and become visible as the user's scroll position reaches them — the page renders like a document, not a sequence of animated scenes. Section boundaries are rectilinear horizontal cuts; no shape, gradient, or overlap communicates the transition between zones.

## Content Rhythm

The carousel in the mid-page full-bleed section is the only designed motion event on the page. Slides transition via crossfade or horizontal slide (0.2s or near-instant timing). The user advances slides with arrow controls. Beyond the carousel, all content — feature cards, form fields, footer columns — is static. No element animates into view, no count or progress indicator animates, no illustrative element moves. The page's rhythm is entirely user-scroll pacing, unassisted by any choreographic layer.

## Footer Approach

The footer enters the viewport as the user scrolls past the contact form section — no animation on entry. The dark blue utility zone with link columns and copyright text is a functional terminus. It does not announce itself. The page ends.

## Motion Principles

The only motion in the system is 0.2s ease hover transitions on all interactive elements — links, buttons, navigation items, topbar contact links. This is the minimum viable state-communication layer: when the cursor touches something interactive, the element changes state in 200ms. No keyframe animations exist. No scroll libraries are present. No parallax, no counter animations, no entrance choreography. The motion philosophy, if it can be called that, is: confirm interactivity, do nothing else.

## Video Placements

This site does not use video and its design register does not call for it. The corporate utility aesthetic — navy, Lato, symmetric grids, standard stock photography — is designed for direct service communication, not atmospheric brand expression. Video would shift the register toward aspiration or lifestyle, which is precisely what this design is not. The site's users are looking for an insurance product and a phone number; they are not waiting to be immersed.

If a single video placement were to be considered, it would be optional and strictly atmospheric:

**Optional — Hero Right Panel: community lifestyle ambient loop**
- Suggested file: `hero-bg-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: A wide, slow pan across a residential neighborhood or main street — recognizably suburban, warm morning light, no people present; 8–10s loop; shot to feel like a place rather than a demographic
- Duration: 8–10s loop
- Why (if at all): The hero's right panel uses stock photography that communicates "insured family" rather than "community we serve." An ambient aerial or ground-level shot of the specific area the agency operates in would replace a generic image with geographic specificity — the only video register that would serve this design's actual purpose.
