About

Built by engineers
who measured rooms.

We spent years doing acoustic calculations in Excel and drawing treatment plans in CAD. Lōmen is the tool we kept wishing existed — browser-native, science-first, fast enough for real design conversations.

Our mission

The room should be
modeled before it's built.

Acoustic problems discovered during construction cost money. Acoustic problems discovered after handover cost more. The tools that could prevent this — room simulation, modal analysis, material specification — lived in academic software that assumed you had a PhD to operate it.

We took the published science — Sabine, Eyring, Rayleigh, ISO 354, ISO 3382 — and made it run in a browser in under 8 seconds. No installation. No licensing. No specialist knowledge required to get a result you can act on.

Lōmen is for the studio designer, the home theatre integrator, the architect specifying a lecture hall, and the broadcast engineer signing off a facility — anyone who needs acoustic prediction grounded in published data, not intuition.

How we build

Three principles
that shape every decision.

01

Science before convenience

Every calculation in Lōmen references a published standard. Sabine and Eyring are both offered because neither is universally correct. ISO 354 absorption data is used verbatim — we don't adjust coefficients to make results look better.

02

Actionable over impressive

A result that tells you to add 8m² of broadband absorption at 63 Hz is more useful than a beautiful 3D rendering with no specification attached. Every Lōmen output is designed to end in a decision — not a conversation about what the data means.

03

Fast enough for real conversations

Acoustic design happens in design reviews, not offline. Lōmen's under-8-second analysis target exists because the tool needs to keep pace with the conversation — not interrupt it while a progress bar ticks forward.
