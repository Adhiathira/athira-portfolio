# The Botanist Florist — Concept by Image

## Brand Premise

A local Auckland florist positioned through aesthetic rather than scale. The Botanist Florist is a brick-and-mortar florist with an online delivery service, and its website makes no attempt to appear larger than it is — the design is grounded in the specific address, the physical studio, the named florists, and the texture of real flower-arranging work. The brand register is minimal and warm: premium local craft, not aspirational DTC. The site exists to move visitors from the homepage to a product category and a purchase, but it does this through editorial restraint and genuine photography rather than promotional pressure. The result is a site that feels like the brand's window display: composed, confident, and quietly beautiful.

## Color Architecture

White (#ffffff) covers all primary surfaces — navigation background, content panels, footer, blog section. A warm cream or light beige field appears as the secondary surface, most notably in the hero's right panel (the content side of the split-screen), creating a subtle temperature distinction between the photographic half and the typographic half. The dominant typographic color is near-black (#161616 body, #092222 headings — the heading color carries a barely perceptible cool-green undertone). The brand's identity color is dark forest green (#3f4d33), used for the primary CTA buttons, the logo wordmark, and the thin text labels in the page header. A warm golden brown (#b0915b) functions as the secondary accent — appearing as a subtle material signal in the photography rather than as a primary UI color. The palette communicates nature, warmth, and restraint without the beige-and-sage cliché: it grounds the brand in an actual place (a florist studio with concrete floors and warm directional light) rather than in a generic lifestyle category.

## Typography System

A two-family system creates the editorial character. Beausite Slick Web at a low weight (300) handles all headings: h1 at 39px, h2 at 27px, h3 and h4 in the 20–22px range. This is an elegant, fashion-adjacent grotesque — its light weight at display scale reads as refined restraint rather than delicacy. No bold, no italic, no weight contrast in the heading family; the hierarchy comes entirely from scale. FT Polar handles body copy and navigation links (16px/400) — a rounded, friendly contemporary sans-serif that contrasts with Beausite's slightly cooler geometry. Together, the two families map a familiar editorial split: Beausite makes editorial statements, FT Polar moves information. The type scale is compact (headings never feel oversized for a standard 1440px viewport) and the result is a typographic system that feels measured and considered without calling attention to itself.

## Spatial System

The page operates in two spatial modes. The hero is a full-viewport horizontal split: the left half is occupied entirely by a large photograph extending edge-to-edge of the left panel; the right half is a cream-toned content panel containing a section headline, a short body block, and two CTA buttons aligned left. The split is 50/50, creating compositional balance between the photographic and typographic halves. Below the hero, the page transitions to alternating split-media sections — each with a large rectangular photo on one side and a content column (heading, body, single CTA) on the other, alternating left/right positions across sections. A three-column blog card grid appears toward the footer. All sections use white background with a container-based layout; there are no full-bleed dark sections and no decorative section dividers. The footer continues on white, organized in four columns (logo, Links, Terms, Contact).

## Photography and Editorial Voice

Photography is the site's primary differentiator. All images are shot in a warm, natural-light register consistent with the florist's actual working environment: the storefront exterior, the studio floor with arrangements in progress, product compositions on neutral surfaces. The photography avoids the generic lifestyle flatness of stock imagery — it shows real work in a real place, with ambient detail (arranging tools, flowers in buckets, the concrete floor) visible in the frame. The hero photo centers on a person holding a completed bouquet in front of the storefront window, directly connecting the digital experience to the physical location. This editorial honesty is the brand's defining design choice: it signals an owner-operated business with genuine craft rather than a platform-built floral delivery service.

## Navigation and Wayfinding

The navigation sits on a white background bar below a thin announcement bar at the very top (yellow/coral background with dark promotional text). The nav structure is standard: the stacked logo wordmark (THE / BOTANIST in two tiers) on the left, a small set of product and utility links in the center, and account/cart/search icons on the right. Navigation links include direct product category access and specialty categories. The nav height is 32px — compact, unobtrusive. There are no dropdowns visible in the primary navigation; category access is direct. The nav does not compete with the content below it; it is a functional wayfinding bar, not a designed feature.

## Overall Design Character

The Botanist Florist is a minimal, photo-forward local business site with three consistent decisions: the warm-neutral white/cream surface that allows the floral photography to carry all chromatic energy; the 50/50 hero split that treats the storefront photograph with the same compositional weight as the editorial text; and the consistent alternating split-media section pattern that creates a predictable, readable rhythm down the page. The dark forest green button color is the only design decision that asserts brand identity through a UI element rather than photography. The design does not attempt the premium DTC aesthetic (no large display typography, no moody atmospheric sections, no extensive lifestyle photography). It is honest about what it is: a local florist's website, made carefully.
