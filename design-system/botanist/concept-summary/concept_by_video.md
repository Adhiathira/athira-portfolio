# The Botanist Florist — Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page loads with the announcement bar at the top, the navigation below it, and the hero split fully rendered at full opacity. No loading animation, no entrance sequence. The hero photograph on the left and the cream content panel on the right are already present. The visitor arrives to a complete, still composition.

## Hero Experience

The hero is static. The photograph on the left side of the split does not pan, parallax, or animate. The cream content panel on the right contains the heading, body copy, and two CTA buttons — all present from the first frame without fade-in or entrance choreography. The two buttons may carry a subtle hover state on interaction (likely a background color shift of ~0.2s), but they make no motion statements on load. The hero's only designed quality is compositional: the 50/50 split with a warm cream panel against the photograph creates a natural resting composition that asks nothing of the visitor except to read and scroll.

## Section Transitions

Content sections arrive without animation as the user scrolls. Each alternating split-media section — photograph one side, content the other — becomes visible as the viewport reaches it. There are no scroll-triggered reveals, no fade-ups, no stagger sequences. The sections stack vertically with hard horizontal boundaries. The only visual pacing device is the alternating left/right position of the photograph and content column between sections, which creates directional rhythm through composition rather than motion.

## Content Rhythm

No content rhythm animations are present in this design. Individual elements do not enter the viewport with reveals. The blog card grid toward the bottom of the page appears as a static three-column layout without any card-level choreography. The page is experienced as a document — complete at all times, scrolled through rather than revealed.

## Footer Approach

The footer arrives as a continuation of the white background. The four-column link structure (logo, Links, Terms, Contact) and the payment icon row below it are static. There is no closing visual statement. The page ends when the copyright strip ends.

## Motion Principles

This site has the minimum viable motion vocabulary: 0.2s ease transitions on hover states across all interactive elements (links, buttons, nav icons). No JavaScript animation library, no CSS keyframes on content elements, no scroll-triggered reveals. The motion philosophy is functional utility: confirm when something is clickable, do nothing else. The photography and typography are trusted to hold attention between points of interaction without any motion scaffolding.

## Video Placements

One section of this site would benefit from ambient video, and one is optional:

**1. Hero Left Panel — Studio ambient loop**
- Suggested file: `hero-studio-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: A slow drift through a flower arrangement in progress — hands holding stems, petals at close range, the concrete studio floor in soft out-of-focus background; warm directional window light; 8s loop, extremely slow motion; the movement should be barely perceptible, resembling a high-resolution photograph that breathes rather than a video that performs
- Duration: 8s loop
- Why video: The hero's left panel currently uses a single lifestyle photograph of the storefront exterior. The florist's strongest differentiator is the authenticity of its studio practice — the real work of arranging flowers in a specific place. A near-still ambient video from inside the studio (rather than outside the storefront) would move the hero from "exterior signage" to "you are here." The video would reinforce the brand's message — this is craft, not a delivery platform — in the register the design already occupies.

**2. Optional — "Events & Corporate" split-media section: Studio-in-motion ambient loop**
- Suggested file: `events-studio-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: A slow wide shot of the studio from above or at mid-height — arrangements being built on the worktable, flowers laid out by color, quiet preparation visible; 6s loop; the camera does not move, the flower arrangement does; the motion is in the material, not the frame
- Duration: 6s loop
- Why (if at all): The events section's split layout places a large photograph on the left side. A near-still ambient video of the studio in its working state would replace a single composed still with a continuous sense of activity — communicating that the team is actually present and doing the work, not represented by a portfolio image. This is the correct register for an events/corporate upsell: not aspirational lifestyle, but quiet professional capability.
