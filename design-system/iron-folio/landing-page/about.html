The method

Why we built it

Every tool we know was built for digitized text. We built one for the thing that comes before: the material itself.

Our position

Precision over convenience

The history of textual scholarship is a history of people getting close to things. Close to the folio, to the character, to the ligature. Close enough to know that the second a in a given word was written by a different hand than the first. That proximity is not inefficiency — it is method.

A tool that approximates is a tool that lies. Manuscript material does not approximate. Neither should the software that handles it.

Compendium was built from the inside of that problem. Our founders spent years working in special collections before they wrote any code. The first version of the platform was a set of scripts a paleographer wrote for herself. The second version was what happened when an engineer looked at those scripts and understood, without being told, that the constraint was the point.

We are not a general transcription service. We are not a document management system. We are a platform for scholars who need to work with materials that resist generalization — and who refuse to accept tools that pretend otherwise.

What we stand for

The principles behind the platform

  • i

    The material is the authority

    Every decision in the platform traces back to the document, not the database. Confidence scoring exists because the material sometimes resists certainty — and the system should say so.

  • ii

    Scholarship travels with the text

    Annotations, editorial decisions, and provenance notes are not metadata stored separately from the transcription. They are bound to the text at the character level, and they export with it.

  • iii

    Open standards, no lock-in

    TEI XML, IIIF manifests, and plain-text exports are first-class outputs. Institutional data belongs to institutions. We do not hold it for leverage.

  • iv

    Slow is not inefficient

    A platform that rushes a scholar through a folio produces a flawed edition. We design for the pace the work demands — which is often slower than software defaults allow.
