# Canva — Concept by Video

## Scroll Opening

The page loads with the hero fully rendered: gradient background at full opacity, the headline cluster centered and visible, the product demonstration video already playing in its windowed container below. No entrance animation on load — the hero is a live composition from the first frame. The navigation bar is transparent and present at the top, its links in purple against the gradient.

## Hero Experience

The hero's primary motion is the animated product demonstration video — a looping UI walkthrough showing design creation in action, presented in a styled application window container with rounded corners and a header bar mimicking an OS window frame. This video plays independently, not scroll-triggered, communicating product capability through movement rather than description. The background gradient is static; the visual energy comes from the product demo. The headline and CTA are present at full opacity without reveal animation. The hero communicates readiness and immediacy — the product is running before the user makes any decision.

## Section Transitions

As the user scrolls past the hero, the gradient gives way to a solid white section — the most dramatic visual transition on the page. This gradient-to-white cut is the primary pacing device: it signals the shift from emotional framing to informational evidence. All subsequent section-to-section transitions are white-to-white with straight horizontal edges, differentiated only by layout structure changes. The page closes on a second gradient section that mirrors the hero — another gradient-to-white transition in reverse, closing the narrative frame.

## Content Rhythm

Elements enter the viewport with a gentle fade-in and slight upward slide — a quick, non-intrusive reveal that introduces content without drawing attention to itself. Within each section, the headline fades in before the content or cards below it, establishing top-down reading order through a fractional stagger. Card groups animate together as a unit rather than individually staggered, preserving the sense of each section arriving complete.

The tabbed feature showcase introduces the page's first interactive animation: selecting a tab instantly swaps the card carousel below with different content. The horizontal carousel in the template showcase section is the page's one axis-switching moment — vertical scroll continues freely, but the user can navigate the gallery horizontally using an arrow button. The transition between card sets is clean and direct.

## Footer Approach

The footer arrives on a solid white background after the final gradient CTA section, with a straight-cut transition. The multi-column link structure is static. A sub-footer below the main columns contains social icons and legal links. No closing animation.

## Motion Principles

The motion vocabulary is minimal and content-serving. Scroll reveals are fast fade-ups with no spring physics, no parallax, no bounce. The animated product demo in the hero is the only persistent motion — it runs on its own clock, independent of scroll. Hover states on cards produce a subtle scale lift (~1.02). The horizontal carousel is user-driven rather than auto-playing. The design does not use motion to perform; it uses motion to introduce. The product demo video is the one moment where motion carries meaning — everywhere else, motion is a delivery mechanism for content, not content itself.

## Video Placements

Three sections are suited for ambient video treatment:

**1. Hero Background — Abstract gradient-colored design process loop**
- Suggested file: `hero-bg-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: An extreme close-up of a design creation process — a cursor moving across a canvas, color swatches appearing, an image being placed, all in warm ambient lighting; shot from above at a slight angle; the motion should be deliberate and satisfying, showing the act of making rather than the result; 8s loop; no face visible; abstract enough that it reads as creative process rather than a specific product interface
- Duration: 8s loop
- Why video: The hero's product demo video already communicates tool capability. A background video in the same section — behind the demo container — would add the atmospheric warmth of the creative act, distinguishing the hero from a product screenshot. The gradient currently provides color energy; a video at very low opacity behind the demo would provide human texture without competing.

**2. Tabbed Feature Showcase — Animated design asset creation loop**
- Suggested file: `feature-showcase-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: A slow, continuous drift across a design canvas showing diverse creative outputs — social media graphics, presentations, logos, print materials — materializing one after another in a sequence that cycles without pause; 10s loop; bright, multi-hued palette matching the brand color range; no text visible
- Duration: 10s loop
- Why video: The tabbed feature showcase uses static illustrative compositions to demonstrate variety. A video background for one of the tab states — the one showing the broadest category of templates — would communicate breadth and creative range more dynamically than any static grid can. The white section background would be replaced by a slow ambient showcase, masked to the card boundaries.

**3. Final CTA — Creative energy gradient loop**
- Suggested file: `cta-gradient-ambient.mp4`
- Subject: A slow, painterly drift across abstract color fields — the same purple/blue/teal of the brand gradient, moving imperceptibly slowly; pure color with no recognizable subject; 12s loop; seamlessly loopable; the motion should be so gradual it reads as a breathing background rather than an animation
- Duration: 12s loop
- Why video: The final CTA section currently uses a static gradient identical to the hero. A very slow animated gradient loop in this position would make the closing moment feel more alive than the hero — the brand gradient as a living surface, not a flat field. At 12s and near-imperceptible motion, it preserves the minimalist CTA composition while adding the temporal quality of presence.
