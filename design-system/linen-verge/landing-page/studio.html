About

Built by practitioners, for practice

Ledgerline was started by architects and contractors who could not find software that treated the specification record as seriously as the drawings.

Origin

In 2022, a contractor on a large civic project returned an issue package for the third time. The drawings were correct. The specification entries referenced in the annotation callouts were from an earlier revision that had been superseded four weeks prior — and no one had caught it because the annotation and the specification lived in two separate documents.

The project team spent eleven days resolving the discrepancy. The contractor was not at fault. The architect was not careless. The system for managing the record was simply not built to catch that class of problem.

Ledgerline is the tool we built to prevent that. Not to add process — to close the structural gap between drawings and the specifications they reference.

Values

What we believe about software for architecture

I

Software should support decisions, not describe them

A tool that records what was decided is useful. A tool that forces you to describe why before you can proceed is an audit system. Ledgerline is the former.

II

The drawing and the specification are the same document

They always were, conceptually. The problem was tooling that kept them apart. Every time they are stored separately, someone has to do the work of keeping them synchronized — and eventually they diverge.

III

Small teams working precisely beat large teams working approximately

Ledgerline is not designed for scale-at-any-cost. It is designed for firms who care about the quality of what they deliver — and who understand that documentation quality is part of design quality.
